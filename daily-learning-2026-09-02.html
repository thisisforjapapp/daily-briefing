The Spacecraft That Bounced Twice Before It Landed on a Comet

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In November 2014, after a ten-year, 6.4-billion-kilometre chase, the European Space Agency (歐洲太空總署) released a small lander toward a comet (彗星) hurtling through space — the first attempt in history to land a spacecraft on one. Before reading on, what do you think happened when its anchoring harpoons failed to fire on touchdown, and why would scientists still call the mission a triumph?
The Philae lander, which touched down on Comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko on 12 November 2014 — the first controlled landing on a comet. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

On 12 November 2014, after chasing Comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko (楚留莫夫–格拉希門克彗星) for over a decade since its 2004 launch, the European Space Agency's (歐洲太空總署) Rosetta (羅塞塔號) spacecraft released its small companion lander, Philae (菲萊號), toward the comet's surface. No spacecraft had ever attempted a controlled landing on a comet before, and mission engineers faced enormous uncertainty: the comet's weak gravity, its irregular rubber-duck shape, and its unpredictable, gas-venting surface made a precise touchdown extraordinarily difficult to plan for.

Philae's ice screws and anchoring harpoons (錨定魚叉) were designed to fire on contact and lock the lander to the comet's surface, but both failed. Instead, Philae bounced roughly one kilometre back into space, drifted for nearly two hours, bounced a second time, and finally settled in the shadow of a cliff at a site later named Abydos (阿拜多斯) — far from its intended sunlit landing zone. Without adequate sunlight to recharge its solar panels, the lander had only about 60 hours of battery power before falling silent.

In that brief window, Philae still transmitted the first images ever taken from a comet's surface and detected organic molecules (有機分子), including carbon compounds relevant to the building blocks of life. Rosetta, still orbiting, later located Philae wedged in a crevice using high-resolution imaging in 2016, finally solving the mystery of its final resting place. Rosetta's own measurements of the comet's water found a deuterium (氘) ratio unlike Earth's oceans, challenging the theory that comets alone delivered Earth's water, before the mission ended with Rosetta's own controlled crash onto the comet in September 2016.

鄭和下西洋——一支艦隊如何讓大明旗幟插上三十餘國海岸？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
1405年，明成祖朱棣派遣心腹宦官鄭和率領二萬七千餘人、逾兩百艘船艦組成的龐大艦隊出海遠航。你認為，這支艦隊出使的真正目的是甚麼？單純耀武揚威，還是別有政治圖謀？
背景速覽

核心人物：鄭和（本名馬和，回族出身，明成祖親信宦官，七次奉命下西洋）；明成祖朱棣（永樂帝，發動靖難之役奪位，下令遠航的皇帝）。

事件：1405年至1433年，鄭和先後七次率領龐大艦隊遠航東南亞、印度洋、阿拉伯半島及東非沿岸，訪問三十餘國。

時代背景：朱棣以「靖難之役」奪取姪兒建文帝帝位，即位後急欲宣揚國威、鞏固正統地位，並藉朝貢體系將四方諸國納入以大明為中心的外交秩序。

印尼三寶壟三保廟（Sam Poo Kong），為紀念鄭和下西洋途經爪哇而建。來源：維基媒體共享資源

靖難之役後，明成祖朱棣以藩王身份奪取姪兒建文帝的帝位，皇位來得不甚光彩，即位後亟需以顯赫功業鞏固統治正當性。1405年，他命心腹宦官鄭和統率一支規模空前的艦隊出海，隨行人員逾二萬七千人，船隻二百餘艘，其中最大的「寶船」據載長逾四十丈，配備羅盤導航與多重船艙分隔設計，堪稱當時全球最先進的遠洋船隊。艦隊沿途廣賜絲綢瓷器等珍寶予各國君主，換取朝貢與臣服的政治承認，藉此將明朝的權威延伸至南海、印度洋以至更遠的海域。

七次遠航歷時二十八年，足跡遍及占城、爪哇、蘇門答臘、錫蘭（今斯里蘭卡）、古里（今印度卡利卡特）、忽魯謨斯（今波斯灣一帶）以至東非沿岸的麻林（今肯尼亞）。艦隊不僅是外交使團，更肩負軍事威懾之責：1411年鄭和在錫蘭捲入當地王位紛爭，一舉擒獲國王亞烈苦奈兒並押解回南京，震懾南洋諸國。艦隊返航時亦帶回長頸鹿等珍禽異獸，被朝廷附會為傳說中的瑞獸「麒麟」，成為向朝野宣示天命所歸的政治符號。

1424年明成祖駕崩後，繼位的明仁宗以耗費國庫、勞民傷財為由一度叫停遠航，及至明宣宗短暫恢復，鄭和已於1433年第七次遠航途中病逝於古里，艦隊隨即返航。此後明朝逐漸轉向保守的海禁政策，遠洋航海檔案相傳遭兵部官員銷毀，龐大寶船船隊自此湮沒於歷史，直至數百年後西方列強憑藉遠洋艦隊叩關，方令後人重新審視這段曾經領先世界的航海盛業因何驟然中斷。

The General Who Marched War Elephants Over the Alps to Reach Rome

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In 218 BCE, Carthaginian general Hannibal Barca (漢尼拔·巴卡) chose the least likely invasion route imaginable — leading an army, complete with war elephants (戰象), over the freezing, near-impassable Alps (阿爾卑斯山) rather than sailing directly to Italy. Before reading on, what do you think this brutal detour cost his army, and why would he choose the hardest possible path to reach Rome (羅馬)?
Context

Key figures: Hannibal Barca (漢尼拔·巴卡), Carthaginian (迦太基) general; his father Hamilcar Barca (哈米爾卡·巴卡), who reportedly made young Hannibal swear eternal hostility to Rome (羅馬).

Event at stake: Hannibal's 218 BCE crossing of the Alps (阿爾卑斯山) with an army including war elephants (戰象), opening the Second Punic War's (第二次布匿戰爭) Italian campaign.

Big picture: Rome and Carthage (迦太基), the two dominant Mediterranean powers, had already fought the First Punic War (264–241 BCE) over Sicily; Carthage's loss left deep resentment and set the stage for a second, far bloodier conflict.

A bust believed to depict Hannibal Barca, the Carthaginian general who led an army across the Alps in 218 BCE. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Carthage (迦太基) lost Sicily and paid crushing reparations after the First Punic War (第一次布匿戰爭, 264–241 BCE), and Hannibal Barca (漢尼拔·巴卡) grew up amid his family's determination to reverse that humiliation, reportedly swearing as a boy never to be a friend to Rome (羅馬). By 218 BCE, commanding Carthaginian forces in Spain, Hannibal calculated that Rome's navy controlled the Mediterranean too completely for a seaborne invasion, and that Rome's Italian allies might defect if a Carthaginian army appeared on Italian soil unexpectedly. He chose the route no one thought possible: marching overland through Gaul (高盧) and directly across the Alps (阿爾卑斯山) into Italy.

The crossing, undertaken in autumn as snow already blanketed the high passes, proved catastrophic. Hannibal's army faced ambushes from hostile Alpine tribes, narrow icy paths that sent men, pack animals, and equipment plunging into ravines, and a shortage of food that forced soldiers to eat their pack animals. Ancient sources describe blasting through a rockfall-blocked pass using vinegar and fire to crack the stone, though modern historians debate the story's accuracy. Of the roughly 38,000 infantry and thousands of cavalry Hannibal had led out of Spain, only a fraction survived the fifteen-day crossing, along with a handful of the war elephants (戰象) that famously accompanied the army.

Despite the devastating losses, the surviving force stunned Rome by simply existing on Italian soil, and Hannibal went on to win a string of shocking victories, most devastatingly at Cannae (坎尼) in 216 BCE, where he encircled and annihilated a Roman army roughly twice the size of his own. Yet Hannibal never received the reinforcements or siege equipment needed to take Rome itself, and Rome's strategy of avoiding open battle while attacking Carthaginian territory elsewhere eventually forced his recall to Africa, where he was defeated at Zama (扎馬) in 202 BCE, ending the war in Rome's favor.

The crossing devastated his army through ambushes, terrain, and starvation, leaving only a fraction of his roughly 38,000 infantry alive by the time he reached Italy.

龍樹的「空」——一輛馬車拆開之後，剩下的是甚麼？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
龍樹主張世間萬物皆「空」，並非指甚麼都不存在，而是指沒有任何事物擁有獨立不變的自性。你認為，若把一輛馬車的車輪、車軸、車廂逐一拆解，「馬車」這個東西究竟去了哪裡？
背景速覽

核心人物：龍樹（約公元150–250年，南印度人），中觀學派創始人；鳩摩羅什（344–413年，後秦高僧），將龍樹著作譯成漢文的譯經大師。

事件：龍樹著《中論》，提出「緣起性空」思想，以嚴密邏輯論證一切法皆無獨立自性。

時代背景：龍樹身處印度部派佛教興盛時期，說一切有部等學派主張諸法各有真實自性（實有論），龍樹的中觀思想正是針對這種實有論而提出的根本挑戰。

龍樹菩薩畫像，中觀學派創始人，著有《中論》闡釋「緣起性空」思想。來源：維基媒體共享資源

龍樹活躍於公元二至三世紀的南印度，當時佛教內部部派林立，其中說一切有部等學派主張構成世界的基本元素各自擁有真實不變的自性，即使外在現象生滅變化，這些基本元素本身仍恆常存在。龍樹在其代表作《中論》中正面挑戰此說，提出「緣起性空」的核心主張：一切事物皆由因緣和合而生，既然依賴其他條件才能存在，便不可能擁有獨立、不變、不依賴他者的自性，這便是「空」的真義——並非虛無斷滅，而是否定事物擁有固定不變的本質。

龍樹慣用嚴密的邏輯論證方式，其中最著名的是「四句否定」：對任何命題，他會依次否定「有」、「無」、「亦有亦無」、「非有非無」四種可能，藉此顯示語言與概念本身的局限，逼使聽者放棄以固定範疇理解實相的執著。他常以馬車為例說明：所謂「馬車」不過是車輪、車軸、車廂等部件依特定方式組合而成的假名，若逐一拆解各部件，遍尋不著一個獨立於部件之外、可稱之為「馬車」的實體，人的自我亦復如是，不過是五蘊和合的假名，並無獨立不變的靈魂實體。

龍樹的中觀思想在印度佛教史上引發長期爭辯，說一切有部學者批評「空」論恐將導致因果虛無、修行無意義的危險後果，龍樹則反駁正因為一切法性空，因果緣起才得以成立——若諸法真有固定自性，反而無法解釋事物如何生滅變化。五世紀高僧鳩摩羅什將《中論》譯成漢文，中觀思想遂大舉傳入中國，深刻影響三論宗、天台宗以至禪宗的義理發展，六祖惠能「本來無一物」的頓悟精神，亦與龍樹「空」的思想一脈相承。

🧠 嘗試不翻閱作答： 龍樹「空」的概念真正否定的是甚麼？他如何以馬車為例說明這個道理？
對照答案

The Runner Who Can Never Catch the Tortoise, According to Pure Logic

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Ancient Greek philosopher Zeno of Elea (埃利亞的芝諾) argued, through pure logical reasoning, that the fastest runner in the world could never overtake a tortoise (烏龜) given even the smallest head start — no matter how fast the runner moved. Before reading on, can you spot where his logic seems airtight, and why it took over two thousand years to fully resolve?
Context

Key figures: Zeno of Elea (埃利亞的芝諾), 5th-century BCE Greek philosopher; his teacher Parmenides (巴門尼德), whose philosophy of unchanging, singular reality Zeno's paradoxes were designed to defend.

Problem at stake: whether motion (運動) and plurality are logically coherent, given Zeno's argument that a runner can never overtake a tortoise with a head start.

Moment: Zeno's paradoxes (悖論), known to us only through later writers like Aristotle (亞里士多德), composed to defend Parmenides's controversial claim that change and motion are illusions.

A portrait bust of Zeno of Elea, whose paradoxes of motion challenged ancient assumptions about infinity and change. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Zeno of Elea (埃利亞的芝諾) was a student of Parmenides (巴門尼德), whose philosophy held that true reality is a single, unchanging whole and that motion and change are mere illusions of the senses — a deeply counterintuitive claim that invited ridicule. Zeno responded not by defending the claim directly, but by constructing paradoxes (悖論) designed to show that the alternative, believing motion is real, leads to even more absurd logical contradictions. His most famous paradox imagines Achilles (阿基里斯), the swiftest of Greek heroes, racing a tortoise (烏龜) that starts with a modest head start.

Zeno argued that by the time Achilles reaches the tortoise's starting point, the tortoise has already crawled a little further ahead. By the time Achilles closes that new gap, the tortoise has advanced again, however slightly. This process repeats infinitely, with the tortoise always occupying a new position just ahead, seemingly proving that Achilles can approach but never actually overtake the tortoise — a conclusion that flatly contradicts everyday experience, where faster runners obviously do overtake slower ones carrying a head start.

Aristotle (亞里士多德) attempted a partial rebuttal by distinguishing potential infinite divisibility from actual infinite distance, but a fuller resolution only arrived with the development of calculus (微積分) in the 17th century, particularly the mathematics of convergent infinite series, showing that infinitely many shrinking time intervals can still sum to a finite total time, allowing Achilles to catch up in finite time despite infinite steps. The paradox remains a foundational case study in philosophy of mathematics, testing the relationship between abstract logical argument and physical reality every time a new generation of students first encounters it.

The paradox seemed to prove that a faster runner could never overtake a slower one with a head start, since closing each gap always leaves a new, smaller gap ahead. The development of calculus and convergent infinite series later showed that infinitely many shrinking intervals can still sum to a finite total time, allowing the runner to catch up after all.

The Four-Year-Olds Who Waited for a Second Marshmallow

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In the late 1960s, researchers offered young children a simple choice: eat one marshmallow (棉花糖) right now, or wait alone in a room for several minutes without eating it and receive two marshmallows instead. Before reading on, what do you think determined which children could wait — and what happened when researchers tracked those same children decades later?
Marshmallows, the treat at the centre of Walter Mischel's famous delayed-gratification experiments. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Beginning in the late 1960s, psychologist Walter Mischel (沃爾特·米歇爾) and colleagues at Stanford University (史丹福大學) ran a series of experiments with preschool-age children, offering each child a treat, often a marshmallow (棉花糖), with a choice: eat it immediately, or wait alone in a room, without any distraction, for roughly 15 minutes to receive a second treat as a reward for waiting. The setup was designed to isolate a single capacity — delaying immediate gratification (延遲滿足) for a larger future reward — under conditions simple enough for young children to understand.

Follow-up studies years later, tracking the same children into adolescence and adulthood, reported correlations between how long a child had waited and various later-life outcomes, including academic performance and self-reported measures of coping ability, feeding a popular narrative that willpower measured in early childhood predicts lifelong success. The marshmallow test (棉花糖測試) became one of psychology's most widely cited findings, frequently invoked in parenting advice and education policy as evidence that teaching self-control early could reshape a child's future.

A 2018 replication by Tyler Watts, Greg Duncan, and Haonan Quan, using a much larger and more socioeconomically diverse sample than Mischel's original small, largely affluent Stanford preschool cohort, found the correlation between waiting time and later outcomes shrank dramatically once family background and household income were statistically controlled for. Rather than raw willpower alone predicting success, a child's home environment and material security appeared to shape both their ability to wait and their later outcomes independently, suggesting the original finding, while real, had been substantially overstated as a standalone predictor.

Why Skin Puckers Into Bumps When There's No Cold and No Fur

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Goosebumps (雞皮疙瘩) appear on human skin during cold weather, fear, and even while listening to a moving piece of music — despite humans having nowhere near enough body hair for the reaction to serve any obvious purpose today. Before reading on, what do you think goosebumps were originally for, and why would the same reflex fire during an emotional song?
Goosebumps, or piloerection, triggered by tiny muscles at the base of each hair follicle. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Goosebumps (雞皮疙瘩), medically termed piloerection (豎毛反應), occur when tiny muscles called arrector pili (豎毛肌), attached to the base of each hair follicle, contract and pull the hair upright, puckering the surrounding skin into small bumps. The reflex is controlled involuntarily by the sympathetic nervous system (交感神經系統), the same branch responsible for the broader "fight or flight" response, which is why goosebumps tend to appear alongside a racing heart or a sudden jolt of adrenaline (腎上腺素) rather than as an isolated skin reaction.

In cold conditions, raising the hairs traps a thicker layer of insulating air close to the skin, a mechanism that worked well for human ancestors covered in dense fur, much as it still does for cats and dogs, whose raised fur adds meaningful insulation and can make a frightened animal appear larger to a threat. Modern humans retain the same muscular reflex despite losing most of that fur over evolutionary time, making the thermoregulatory benefit largely vestigial (退化痕跡) — the mechanism fires exactly as it did in furrier ancestors, even though it no longer meaningfully warms a nearly hairless body.

The same sympathetic pathway also activates during strong emotional experiences entirely unrelated to temperature or threat — a swelling moment in a film score, an unexpected chill of awe at a piece of art, or a wave of nostalgia can all trigger goosebumps through the release of norepinephrine (去甲腎上腺素) onto the same follicle muscles. Researchers studying this "aesthetic chills" response suggest it may be an accidental byproduct of a defensive reflex, repurposed by emotional and social parts of the brain that happen to share the same neural wiring, rather than serving any separate function of its own.

💊 Health

The Egg Yolk America Was Told to Fear for Fifty Years

🤔 Predict Before You Read
For decades, health authorities advised limiting dietary cholesterol (膳食膽固醇), especially from egg yolks, warning that eating cholesterol directly raised blood cholesterol (血膽固醇) and heart disease risk. Before reading on, what do you think large-scale nutrition research eventually found about the actual link between the cholesterol you eat and the cholesterol in your blood?
The chemical structure of cholesterol, whose dietary intake has a smaller effect on blood levels than long assumed. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The dietary-cholesterol-fear era traces largely to physiologist Ancel Keys's (安塞爾·基斯) mid-20th-century research and his influential Seven Countries Study, which linked saturated fat (飽和脂肪) intake to blood cholesterol and heart disease across different national diets. Although Keys's own later work found dietary cholesterol itself had a comparatively minor effect on blood cholesterol for most people, the simpler, more alarming message — that eating cholesterol-rich foods like egg yolks directly raises blood cholesterol — spread through public health guidance, and official caps on dietary cholesterol persisted in US dietary guidelines for decades.

For most people, the liver (肝臟) tightly regulates the body's own cholesterol production in response to dietary intake: eating more dietary cholesterol prompts the liver to produce somewhat less endogenous cholesterol, partially offsetting the intake, a compensatory mechanism that limits how much a typical meal's cholesterol content actually moves blood levels. Large prospective studies, including a widely cited analysis published in the Journal of the American College of Nutrition, found no significant association between moderate egg consumption and increased cardiovascular disease risk in generally healthy populations, distinguishing egg-yolk cholesterol from the more consistently harmful effects of trans fats (反式脂肪) and excess saturated fat.

In 2015, the US Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee formally removed its long-standing recommendation to limit dietary cholesterol to 300 milligrams per day, stating that "cholesterol is not considered a nutrient of concern for overconsumption" for the general population based on accumulated evidence. The change didn't declare eggs a health food without limit, and people with certain conditions like diabetes or existing high LDL cholesterol are still sometimes advised caution, but it marked a rare, explicit reversal of decades-old public health guidance once individual studies accumulated enough weight.

🏋 Sports Science

Rolling on a Foam Cylinder Loosens Muscles Without the Strength Cost of Stretching

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Static stretching (靜態伸展) held before a workout has been shown to temporarily reduce strength and power output. Before reading on, what do you think happens to strength when athletes use a foam roller (泡沫軸) on tight muscles instead — does it carry the same performance penalty, or something different?
A foam roller, used for self-myofascial release before or after training. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Foam rolling (泡沫軸滾動), also called self-myofascial release (自我筋膜放鬆), involves applying body weight through a firm foam cylinder against a muscle group, rolling slowly back and forth to apply sustained pressure. It became popular in gyms and physical therapy clinics as a self-administered alternative to a therapist's hands-on massage, marketed as a way to "release" tight fascia (筋膜), the connective tissue sheath surrounding muscles, and improve joint range of motion (關節活動範圍) before or after exercise.

A 2015 systematic review by Cheatham and colleagues found foam rolling reliably produces short-term increases in joint range of motion, comparable in magnitude to static stretching, but critically without static stretching's well-documented acute reduction in maximal strength and power output. This distinction made foam rolling attractive as a pre-workout warm-up option: athletes could gain flexibility benefits without the same performance cost that research has consistently found accompanies holding a static stretch for 20 seconds or more immediately before lifting or sprinting.

The exact mechanism behind foam rolling's effect remains genuinely debated: some researchers argue it works primarily through direct mechanical changes to fascia and muscle tissue, while others point to neurological explanations, such as reduced nervous system sensitivity to stretch or simply a temporarily higher pain tolerance from the pressure applied. Regardless of mechanism, current evidence, including later meta-analyses in the International Journal of Sports Physical Therapy, supports foam rolling as a reasonably effective addition to warm-ups and recovery routines, though the range-of-motion benefit tends to fade within roughly 10 to 15 minutes unless followed by movement.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

The Business Partner Benjamin Graham Invented Just to Explain the Stock Market

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In his 1949 investing classic, Benjamin Graham (班傑明·葛拉漢) introduced an imaginary business partner named Mr. Market (市場先生) to explain how investors should think about daily stock price swings. Before reading on, what do you think this partner's defining trait was, and why would Graham suggest sometimes simply ignoring what he says?
Benjamin Graham, whose 1949 book The Intelligent Investor introduced the Mr. Market allegory. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In The Intelligent Investor (1949), Benjamin Graham (班傑明·葛拉漢), widely regarded as the father of value investing (價值投資), introduced an allegory to explain how emotionally erratic stock market (股票市場) prices can be. He imagined the reader as a business partner with a fictional character named Mr. Market (市場先生), who shows up at your door every single day offering to either buy your shares or sell you his, always naming a different price depending entirely on his mood that day.

On some days, Mr. Market (市場先生) arrives wildly optimistic, naming a price far above what the underlying business is actually worth; on other days he's despondent, offering to sell his share of the business for far less than its real value. Graham's point was that a rational investor is never obligated to transact with Mr. Market on any given day — you can simply ignore his mood swings entirely, or take advantage of them when his price diverges sharply from your own independent estimate of the business's intrinsic value (內在價值), buying when he's fearful and selling when he's euphoric.

Warren Buffett (華倫·巴菲特), Graham's most famous student, has repeatedly credited the Mr. Market allegory as one of the most valuable mental models in investing, noting that treating daily price quotes as a servant to consult rather than a master to obey protects investors from being swept into buying at euphoric peaks or panic-selling at irrational lows. The allegory's core insight — that price and value are not the same thing, and only one of them fluctuates on a manic daily schedule — remains a foundational concept in value investing more than seventy years after Graham first wrote it.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

The Money That Was Supposed to Flow to Poor Countries — and Didn't

Economist Robert Lucas Jr., who documented capital's puzzling reluctance to flow toward poorer countries. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
Through the late 20th century, capital consistently flowed toward already-wealthy nations rather than toward capital-scarce developing countries (發展中國家), even though standard economic theory predicted the opposite pattern should dominate global investment flows.
📖 Theory
Neoclassical growth theory (新古典增長理論), building on Robert Solow's (梭羅) framework, predicts capital should flow from capital-rich, low-return rich countries toward capital-poor countries, where each additional dollar of investment should generate higher returns due to greater scarcity.
⚠️ Anomaly
Economist Robert Lucas Jr. (盧卡斯), in an influential 1990 paper, documented that observed international capital flows ran overwhelmingly in the opposite direction, concentrating in already-developed economies rather than seeking the theoretically higher returns available in poorer nations.
🧪 Challenger
Researchers including Alfaro, Kalemli-Ozcan & Volosovych (2008) proposed that gaps in institutional quality (制度質素), human capital (人力資本), and policy risk — not the simple capital-scarcity math of neoclassical theory — better explain why investors systematically avoid poorer countries; how much each factor contributes remains an open question in development economics.
💡 Money doesn't automatically flow to where textbook returns say it should — it flows to where investors trust it will still be there tomorrow.
📖 Lucas, R.E. Jr. (1990), "Why Doesn't Capital Flow from Rich to Poor Countries?", American Economic Review 80(2); Alfaro, L., Kalemli-Ozcan, S. & Volosovych, V. (2008), Review of Economics and Statistics 90(2). Never recycles Card 10 domain (individual value-investing psychology vs. macroeconomic international capital-flow theory).

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 6 & 10 — The Marshmallow You Don't Eat: Walter Mischel's preschoolers who resisted eating one marshmallow immediately, in exchange for two later, tended toward better outcomes decades on, provided their environment made "wait" a promise worth trusting. Benjamin Graham's Mr. Market makes an almost identical demand of investors: resist the temptation to react to his daily mood swings — his cheerful high prices, his panicked low ones — and instead wait for a business's real value to reveal itself over time.

So what: In both cases, the hard part was never resisting a treat or a stock price in the abstract — it's resisting a specific, present, emotionally loud signal that's asking you to act right now, in favor of a calmer judgment that only pays off later.
