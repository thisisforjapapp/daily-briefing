🔭 Astronomy & Cosmos

The Photograph Carl Sagan Had to Beg NASA to Take

🤔 Predict Before You Read
On Valentine's Day 1990, at the edge of the solar system, NASA turned a spacecraft that had already finished its main mission around for one last photograph — not of a planet or moon, but of what looked like empty space. Before reading on, what do you think that photo actually showed, and why do you think one scientist had to fight for years just to get it taken?
Earth as a single pixel (the faint bluish-white dot inside the brownish light band), photographed by Voyager 1 from about 6 billion kilometres away on 14 February 1990. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

By 1990, Voyager 1 (航海者一號) had completed its primary mission past Jupiter and Saturn and was speeding out of the solar system, its cameras scheduled to be permanently switched off to conserve power for its remaining instruments. Planetary scientist Carl Sagan (卡爾·薩根) had proposed years earlier that before that shutdown, the probe should turn back toward the inner solar system and photograph Earth from roughly 6 billion kilometres away. NASA engineers resisted the idea for years: the manoeuvre risked pointing the camera close enough to the Sun to damage its optics, and the resulting image offered no scientific data whatsoever — only, Sagan argued, perspective.

Sagan eventually persuaded mission managers, and on 14 February 1990, Voyager 1 rotated to capture a 60-frame mosaic of the solar system, including one frame aimed precisely at Earth. Within that single frame, our entire planet appeared as a pale bluish-white point smaller than a single pixel, caught inside a scattered band of sunlight that was purely an optical artifact of the camera's own internal reflections, not a real halo around Earth. Nothing in the raw image identified the dot as home; no oceans, continents, or cities were visible — it took Sagan's own narration to make the photograph famous.

Sagan's 1994 book and lecture built around the photograph argued that "everyone you love, everyone you know, everyone you ever heard of" lived out their lives on that single suspended mote of dust, framing the image as a lesson in cosmic humility rather than a scientific discovery. Voyager 1's cameras were switched off permanently soon after, but the spacecraft kept transmitting plasma and cosmic-ray data, becoming the first human-made object to reach interstellar space (星際空間) in 2012 and continuing to send signals today from over 25 billion kilometres away.

靖難之役——燕王朱棣如何以「清君側」之名，奪走姪兒的皇位？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
建文元年，明太祖駕崩，皇太孫朱允炆繼位後大舉削藩，多位叔父藩王或廢或死。鎮守北平的燕王朱棣卻按兵不動良久，才終於起兵。你認為，他為何遲遲不動，最終又如何以一隅之地的兵力，擊敗坐擁全國賦稅與大軍的朝廷？
背景速覽

核心人物：燕王朱棣（明太祖第四子，鎮守北平；後來的明成祖）；建文帝朱允炆（明太祖皇太孫，1398年繼位）。

事件：建文元年（1399年），朱棣以「清君側，靖國難」為名起兵，史稱靖難之役，歷時三年攻入南京，建文帝下落不明，朱棣稱帝改元永樂。

時代背景：明太祖為防權臣篡位，分封諸子為藩王手握重兵；建文帝繼位後採納齊泰、黃子澄削藩之議，數月內廢殺周王、湘王等多位叔父，燕王朱棣自感朝不保夕。

靖難之役軍事路線圖。燕王朱棣自北平起兵，繞過重兵防守的城池，直取南京。來源：維基媒體共享資源

建文帝繼位後，聽從近臣齊泰、黃子澄「強幹弱枝」之議，雷厲風行削藩：周王被廢為庶人，湘王闔府自焚，代王、齊王相繼被囚。燕王朱棣鎮守北平，手握重兵卻遠在邊陲，眼見諸兄弟接連遭殃，深知下一個目標便是自己。他一面裝瘋賣傻拖延時間，暗中卻在王府後院操練精兵、打造兵器，以打鐵聲掩蓋操練聲響。建文元年七月，朝廷密令逮捕燕王府官屬，朱棣終於圖窮匕見，誅殺前來執行密令的官員，正式起兵，打出「清君側，靖國難」旗號，史稱「靖難之役」。

起兵之初，朱棣僅有北平一隅之地與數萬兵力，朝廷卻坐擁全國賦稅與號稱百萬大軍，形勢懸殊。然而建文帝用人不當，先後委任年邁的耿炳文、志大才疏的李景隆統兵，屢戰屢敗；朱棣則親率精銳騎兵長途奔襲，避實擊虛，多次繞過重兵防守的城池，直取糧道與後方。建文四年（1402年），朱棣採納謀士姚廣孝之議，捨棄逐城攻取的緩兵之計，率軍直撲南京，沿途守將望風而降，六月南京城破。

城破之日，皇宮燃起大火，建文帝從此下落不明，成為明史一大懸案，有傳聞指他喬裝僧人逃亡海外，亦有傳言死於火場，終明一代眾說紛紜。朱棣隨即登基，是為明成祖，改元永樂，其後遷都北平（易名北京）、派鄭和下西洋、編纂《永樂大典》，開創明代盛世；但為肅清建文舊臣，亦大興株連之獄，方孝孺被誅十族，牽連逾八百人，成為這場皇位爭奪戰最血腥的尾聲。

The Night Enemy Soldiers Sang Carols Across No Man's Land

🤔 Predict Before You Read
By December 1914, the Western Front had settled into a bloody stalemate of opposing trenches often just tens of metres apart, and both sides' high commands had explicitly forbidden any fraternisation with the enemy. Before reading on, what do you think happened along parts of that front on Christmas Eve — and how far do you think it actually went?
Context

Key figures: ordinary British, German (德軍) and French frontline troops; no single commanding officer authorised what followed.

Event at stake: the Christmas Truce (聖誕休戰), an unofficial, spontaneous ceasefire that broke out along stretches of the Western Front (西線) on 24–25 December 1914.

Big picture: after just five months of war, the promised quick victory had collapsed into grinding trench warfare (壕溝戰) with horrific casualties, and exhausted troops on both sides were increasingly aware the war would not end soon.

A contemporary Illustrated London News depiction of British and German troops meeting in no man's land during the 1914 Christmas Truce. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

On Christmas Eve 1914, along several sections of the Western Front, particularly near Ypres (伊珀爾) in Belgium, German soldiers began decorating their trenches with candles and small trees and singing carols such as "Stille Nacht." British troops in opposing trenches, some only 30 metres away, heard the singing and began responding with carols of their own. Isolated shouted greetings crossed no man's land (無人地帶), the shell-cratered strip separating the two front lines, and by dawn on Christmas Day, small groups of unarmed men from both sides began cautiously climbing out of their trenches to meet in the open.

What followed varied enormously by sector but included exchanges of food, tobacco, and souvenirs such as buttons and helmets, joint burial parties for the dead lying between the lines, and in several documented locations, informal football (足球) matches played with tins or makeshift balls on the frozen ground. Soldiers exchanged addresses and posed for photographs together, briefly treating men they had been trying to kill days earlier as fellow human beings sharing an unwanted war. Commanding officers on both sides were largely absent from these encounters, or chose to look away, at least initially.

High commands on both sides were alarmed once reports reached them, fearing the truce would undermine soldiers' willingness to fight, and issued strict orders against any repeat fraternisation; by 1915, artillery bombardments were deliberately scheduled for Christmas Eve to prevent a recurrence. The 1914 truce was never centrally organised and its exact scale is still debated by historians, but letters, diaries and regimental records from dozens of separate locations confirm it happened independently and simultaneously across a war that would ultimately kill an estimated 8.5 million soldiers before its end in 1918.

荀子「性惡論」——他為何說人性本惡，卻仍相信教育能讓人成聖？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
孟子主張「人性本善」，認為人皆有惻隱、羞惡之心的萌芽，只需擴而充之即可成德。同一儒家陣營內，另一位大儒卻反其道而行，直言人性本惡。你認為，他如何在否定人性本善的前提下，仍然主張人人皆可通過後天努力成為聖人？
背景速覽

核心人物：荀子（約公元前313–前238年，戰國末期儒家代表人物，曾任稷下學宮祭酒）。

核心主張：性惡論——人生而有好利、疾惡、耳目之欲等本能，若順其自然發展必生爭奪暴亂；善非天生，而是後天「化性起偽」的人為努力成果。

時代背景：戰國末年禮崩樂壞，孟子性善論雖為儒家正統，但荀子目睹亂世人心險惡，認為若不正視人性中趨利避害的一面，禮法教化便失去存在的根基。

荀子畫像。他主張「性惡論」，認為善是後天教化的成果，而非天生本能。來源：維基媒體共享資源

荀子在《性惡》篇開宗明義提出「人之性惡，其善者偽也」，直接挑戰孟子「性善」之說。他指出，人生來便有好利、嫉妒、耳目聲色之欲等本能傾向，若任其自然發展而不加約束，必然導致爭奪、殘賊、淫亂，社會秩序蕩然無存。荀子特別強調，這裡的「偽」並非「虛偽」之意，而是「人為」——指後天教化與努力雕琢的成果，如同工匠加工木材、金屬一般，需要外力矯正方能成器。

荀子並非否定人可以為善，恰恰相反，他認為「塗之人可以為禹」——即使是路上的凡夫俗子，只要肯努力學習禮義，皆可能成為像大禹一樣的聖人。他提出「化性起偽」的工夫論：聖人制定禮義法度，正是為了矯正人性中趨利避害的本能，使人透過師法教化、積累學習，逐漸克服天生的惡性傾向，養成合乎禮義的行為習慣，最終達至善的境界。

荀子這套學說在後世評價毀譽參半——正因主張禮法外在約束的重要性，其兩位弟子韓非、李斯，日後分別成為法家理論奠基者與秦朝丞相，助秦以嚴刑峻法統一天下，令後世儒者對荀子頗有微詞，甚至一度將其排除於儒家道統之外。然而，荀子對禮樂教化制度化的重視，其後深刻影響漢代經學與後世的禮制建構，其「性惡論」與孟子「性善論」的對立，至今仍是中國哲學史上探討人性論最重要的一組辯題。

🌿 Philosophy — Western

The Thought Experiment for Designing a Fair Society Without Knowing Your Place in It

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Imagine you had to design the rules for an entire society — its laws, its distribution of wealth, its social structure — but before seeing the result, you would not know whether you'd be born rich or poor, healthy or disabled, in the majority or a persecuted minority. Before reading on, what kind of society do you think people would design under that condition?
Context

Key figure: John Rawls (約翰·羅爾斯, 1921–2002), American political philosopher, Harvard University (哈佛大學).

Problem at stake: what principles of justice would rational people agree to if they had to choose fairly, without knowing their own future position in society.

Rival view: utilitarianism (功利主義), which judges justice by whichever arrangement maximises total aggregate welfare, even at the cost of severe hardship for a minority.

John Rawls, 1971, the year he published A Theory of Justice and introduced the veil of ignorance. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In his 1971 book A Theory of Justice, John Rawls (約翰·羅爾斯) proposed a thought experiment (思想實驗) to identify fair principles for organising society. He asked readers to imagine a hypothetical "original position" (原初狀態) in which the people choosing society's basic rules sit behind a veil of ignorance (無知之幕): they know general facts about economics, psychology and human society, but nothing about their own eventual race, sex, class, talents, religion, or even their personal idea of what makes a good life. Rawls argued this artificial ignorance is precisely what makes the resulting choice genuinely fair, since no one can rig the rules to favour the specific person they already know they will become.

Rawls argued that behind the veil, rational, self-interested people would not gamble on a utilitarian (功利主義) arrangement that might maximise total welfare but risk leaving them destitute if they turned out to be among the worst-off; instead they would favour principles protecting whoever ends up worst-off in society. This reasoning produced his difference principle (差異原則): social and economic inequalities are only justified if they work to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged members of society, alongside a prior guarantee of equal basic liberties for everyone, which he ranked as taking strict priority over any distributive concern.

Critics attacked the theory from multiple directions: philosopher Robert Nozick (羅伯特·諾齊克) argued the difference principle ignored how people justly acquired their holdings in the first place, defending a more libertarian view of entitlement; communitarian (社群主義) critics like Michael Sandel argued Rawls's abstract, history-less individual behind the veil was an incoherent fiction, since real people's identities are inseparable from their concrete social attachments. Despite the criticism, the veil of ignorance remains one of the most widely taught tools in political philosophy for testing whether a policy is fair to the people it affects least.

🧠 Psychology

The Jam Tasting Table That Sold More When It Offered Less

🤔 Predict Before You Read
A grocery store set up two versions of a tasting table on different days: one offering 24 different flavours of jam, the other offering just 6. Common sense suggests more options should attract more interested shoppers and more sales. Before reading on, which table do you think led to more actual jam purchases?
Barry Schwartz, who popularised the jam-tasting findings in his 2004 book The Paradox of Choice. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In a well-known 2000 field experiment, psychologists Sheena Iyengar (希娜·艾恩嘉) and Mark Lepper set up a tasting booth in an upscale grocery store, alternating between displaying 24 varieties of jam (果醬) and just 6 varieties on different days. The larger display attracted more browsing shoppers overall — 60% of passersby stopped to look, compared to 40% at the smaller display — confirming that abundant choice does draw initial attention and curiosity.

The purchasing outcome reversed that pattern sharply: only about 3% of shoppers who browsed the 24-jam table went on to actually buy a jar, compared to roughly 30% of those who browsed the 6-jam table — a tenfold difference in conversion despite the larger display's greater initial appeal. Iyengar and Lepper attributed this to the cognitive burden of comparing many similar options, which made shoppers more prone to indecision, and to heightened anticipated regret over whether a rejected alternative might have tasted better.

Psychologist Barry Schwartz (巴里·施瓦茨) popularised the finding in his 2004 book The Paradox of Choice, arguing that beyond a certain point, additional options increase decision-making effort and post-choice regret faster than they increase the odds of finding a genuinely better option, sometimes leading people to avoid deciding altogether. Later replications produced mixed results depending on context — choice overload (選擇超載) proved strongest when options were complex, unfamiliar, or hard to compare — but the finding reshaped how retailers think about curating rather than maximising the number of options offered.

🫀 Physiology

Why Watching Someone Else Yawn Makes You Yawn Too

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Simply seeing, hearing, or even reading about someone else yawning measurably increases how often you yawn yourself — a pattern documented not only in humans but in chimpanzees and dogs. Before reading on, do you think this "contagious" effect is mainly a physiological reflex, or something more tied to social bonding?
The yawn reflex is shared widely across social mammal species; contagious yawning, however, is far rarer and closely tied to social bonding. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Yawning (打哈欠) — the reflexive wide-opening of the mouth with a deep inhalation — is one of the most contagious behaviours known: simply seeing, hearing, or even reading about another person yawning measurably increases how often a person yawns themselves, an effect documented not only in humans but in chimpanzees, dogs, and several other social species. Contagious yawning typically begins to appear in humans around age four or five, roughly coinciding with the developmental emergence of theory of mind (心智理論), the ability to model what another person is thinking or feeling.

Brain-imaging studies point to the mirror neuron system (鏡像神經元系統) and regions tied to empathy and social cognition, particularly the precuneus (楔前葉), as more active during contagious yawning than during a spontaneous yawn, suggesting the behaviour is linked to unconscious social mimicry rather than pure physiological need. Supporting this, people who score lower on empathy measures, and some individuals with autism spectrum conditions (自閉症譜系), show reduced susceptibility to catching a yawn from others, though findings on the strength of this link vary across studies.

The deeper evolutionary function remains genuinely debated: one leading hypothesis holds that contagious yawning evolved to synchronise arousal and alertness across a social group, useful for coordinating sleep-wake transitions or vigilance in a troop of primates; another treats it as a byproduct of general motor mimicry rather than a specifically evolved social signal. What is well established is that familiarity strengthens the effect — people catch yawns more readily from close family and friends than from strangers, a pattern that has itself been used as an informal measure of social bonding in research settings.

The Vitamin Pill That Was Supposed to Prevent Cancer and Made It More Likely

🤔 Predict Before You Read
By the late 1980s, dozens of observational studies had found that people eating diets rich in beta-carotene had lower rates of lung cancer, generating excitement about beta-carotene supplements as a cheap protective pill for smokers and asbestos workers. Before reading on, do you think the randomized trials that followed confirmed this protective effect?
Concentrated beta-carotene supplements behaved very differently in clinical trials than beta-carotene consumed as part of whole food. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Beta-carotene (β-胡蘿蔔素) is the orange plant pigment the body converts into vitamin A, abundant in carrots, sweet potatoes and leafy greens, and by the 1980s numerous observational studies had linked diets rich in it to lower lung cancer (肺癌) rates. Excited by this correlation, researchers designed large randomized controlled trials to test whether a concentrated beta-carotene supplement could actively prevent cancer in high-risk groups, rather than merely being one marker among many of an already healthy diet.

The Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta-Carotene (ATBC) trial in Finnish male smokers and the US CARET trial in smokers and asbestos-exposed workers both delivered a shock when results arrived in the mid-1990s: rather than reducing cancer risk, beta-carotene supplements were associated with an 18 to 28% increase in lung cancer incidence and higher overall mortality among smokers, severe enough that CARET's own safety monitoring board halted the trial nearly two years early on ethical grounds.

Researchers now suspect the isolated, concentrated supplement behaves very differently in the body than beta-carotene consumed as part of whole food, possibly interacting badly with oxidative stress already present in smokers' lungs, though the exact mechanism remains debated. The episode became a textbook caution in nutrition science: a nutrient's association with good health in food form does not automatically mean a concentrated pill of that nutrient is safe, and it strongly reinforced clinical guidance against high-dose beta-carotene supplementation specifically for current smokers.

🏋 Sports Science

What Rolling on a Foam Cylinder Actually Does to Your Muscles

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Foam rollers became a near-universal gym accessory on the promise that they physically break up scar tissue and "release" tight fascia. Before reading on, do you think foam rolling actually changes the physical structure of muscle and fascia, or is something else going on?
A foam roller. Research suggests its benefits come from neurological effects on pain perception rather than any physical reshaping of fascia. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Foam rolling (泡沫軸放鬆) is a form of self-myofascial release (自我筋膜放鬆) in which a person applies sustained body-weight pressure to a muscle by rolling it over a dense foam cylinder, popularised from the early 2000s onward as a cheap alternative to sports massage. Marketing claims frequently asserted that the technique physically breaks up adhesions or "knots" in the fascia (筋膜), the connective tissue sheath surrounding muscles, and lengthens tissue that has become chronically tight.

The physical force a person can realistically generate by rolling their own body weight over a foam cylinder is far too small to mechanically deform dense fascia, which can withstand much greater pressures without changing shape, a fact that has pushed researchers toward neurological rather than mechanical explanations. Cheatham et al.'s 2015 systematic review found foam rolling reliably produces short-term increases in joint range of motion (關節活動範圍) without the strength or power losses associated with prolonged static stretching, likely via reduced neural excitability and altered pain perception rather than any structural tissue change.

Evidence for foam rolling meaningfully reducing delayed-onset muscle soreness (延遲性肌肉痠痛, DOMS) after hard training is mixed and generally modest, and it shows little to no measurable effect on longer-term strength or hypertrophy gains one way or the other. The most defensible practical use to emerge is as a low-cost pre-exercise warm-up tool for temporarily improving mobility without the performance costs of long static holds, rather than as a therapeutic technique capable of permanently changing tissue structure.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

The Stock Bubble So Absurd It Made Isaac Newton Blame the Madness of People

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In early 1720, shares in a British trading company with almost no actual profitable trade to its name rose roughly tenfold in a matter of months, drawing investment from dukes, clergymen, and one of history's greatest scientists. Before reading on, what do you think the company actually did to justify that surge — and do you think it ended well?
Coat of arms of the South Sea Company, whose 1720 share collapse became one of history's first modern stock market bubbles. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The South Sea Company (南海公司) was founded in 1711 with a monopoly on British trade with South America, a market Spain (西班牙) tightly restricted, meaning the company's actual trading profits were always minimal. In 1719, the company proposed converting a large portion of Britain's national debt into its own shares, promising the government reduced interest payments in exchange for the exclusive right to do so — a financial restructuring scheme, not a trading breakthrough, that nonetheless required convincing the public the company was extraordinarily profitable.

Company directors talked up vague prospects of vast South American riches while restricting share allotments to insiders and manipulating the subscription price upward, and speculative fever spread through London society: shares rose from around £128 in January 1720 to roughly £1,000 by August, drawing in investors from members of Parliament to Isaac Newton (艾薩克·牛頓), who reportedly sold an early position for a solid profit, then bought back in near the peak and lost a large sum when the price collapsed. Newton is widely quoted as remarking he "could calculate the motion of the heavens, but not the madness of people."

The bubble collapsed by autumn 1720 once new joint-stock ventures diverted capital and confidence faltered, wiping out fortunes across British society and triggering a parliamentary investigation that uncovered bribery of government ministers by company directors. The Bubble Act (泡沫法案) of 1720, originally passed to protect the South Sea Company's position by restricting rival joint-stock companies, is now remembered chiefly as an early, blunt attempt at securities regulation, and the episode remains a foundational case study in how a plausible-sounding financial restructuring can inflate into a full-blown speculative mania.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

Why Inflation and Unemployment Were Supposed to Never Rise Together

Arthur Burns, US Federal Reserve Chair from 1970 to 1978, presided over the 1970s stagflation the Phillips curve could not explain. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
Through the 1970s, the United States and much of the industrialised world experienced stagflation (停滯性通脹) — simultaneously high inflation and high unemployment, most severely from 1973 onward following the OPEC oil embargo (石油禁運), a combination mainstream economic policy considered close to a statistical impossibility.
📖 Theory
The Phillips curve (菲利普斯曲線), based on A.W. Phillips's 1958 observation of British wage data, proposed a stable inverse trade-off between unemployment and inflation: policymakers could deliberately accept somewhat higher inflation to buy lower unemployment, or vice versa, treating the relationship as a dependable dial governments could turn.
⚠️ Anomaly
Federal Reserve Chair Arthur Burns (亞瑟·伯恩斯) presided over the anomaly directly: unemployment and inflation rose together through the 1970s rather than trading off, with US inflation reaching double digits alongside unemployment above 8% by 1975 — a combination the simple Phillips curve had no room to explain.
🧪 Challenger
Milton Friedman (米爾頓·佛利民) and Edmund Phelps had predicted the breakdown before it happened, arguing any trade-off vanishes once workers' inflation expectations (通脹預期) adjust, producing a "natural rate" of unemployment; this evolved into today's standard expectations-augmented framework, though how flat the curve has become in recent decades, and why, remains an open question.
💡 A relationship that looked like an iron law from one decade of data quietly assumed people would never adjust their expectations — the moment they did, the law broke.
📖 Phillips, A.W. (1958), Economica 25(100). Friedman, M. (1968), “The Role of Monetary Policy,” American Economic Review 58(1). Never recycles Card 10 domain (18th-century stock speculation vs. 20th-century macroeconomic policy theory).

Cards 1 & 5 — The View From Nowhere: Voyager 1's Pale Blue Dot photograph and Rawls's veil of ignorance both work by deliberately stripping away a personal vantage point — literally erasing Earth's visible detail in one case, hypothetically erasing your own position in society in the other — to reveal a picture that's too easily distorted by standing too close to it.
