The Photograph of Something You’re Never Supposed to See

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In April 2019, an international team unveiled the first-ever photograph of a black hole, an object whose entire defining property is that not even light can escape it. Before reading on, what do you think a “photograph” of something that traps all light could possibly show, and how would you even build a telescope capable of seeing something so far away and so small?
The first-ever image of a black hole (黑洞), M87*, released 10 April 2019 by the Event Horizon Telescope (事件視界望遠鏡) collaboration. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

On 10 April 2019, an international collaboration called the Event Horizon Telescope (事件視界望遠鏡, EHT) unveiled something astronomers had spent a century predicting but never seeing: an actual photograph of a black hole (黑洞). The target was M87* (M87∗), a supermassive black hole roughly 6.5 billion times the mass of the Sun, sitting 55 million light-years away at the centre of the galaxy Messier 87 (梅西耶87星系). Since no single telescope on Earth is large enough to resolve something so distant and compact, EHT linked eight radio observatories across four continents into one Earth-sized virtual instrument using very-long-baseline interferometry (甚長基線干涉測量法).

The resulting image showed a glowing, lopsided ring of superheated plasma (電漿) encircling a dark central shadow, the closest visual evidence yet of an event horizon (事件視界), the boundary beyond which nothing, not even light, can escape. The shadow’s size matched predictions from Albert Einstein’s (愛因斯坦) general relativity (廣義相對論) with striking precision, a theory published over a century earlier and never before tested this directly. Capturing it required synchronising atomic clocks at every site to a precision of one part in ten quadrillion, then physically shipping half a tonne of hard drives, since the raw data was too vast to transmit online, to supercomputers at MIT Haystack Observatory (麻省理工學院海斯塔克天文台) for correlation.

The 2019 image confirmed decades of theoretical work almost overnight and gave the public its first real look at one of physics’ strangest predictions rather than an artist’s rendering. EHT followed up in 2022 with an image of Sagittarius A* (人馬座A∗), the comparatively modest black hole at the centre of our own Milky Way (銀河系), and later added a polarisation map revealing the magnetic field lines twisting around M87*’s shadow. With more telescopes now joining the array each year, astronomers expect future images sharp enough to watch matter actually swirl and vanish at a black hole’s edge in near-real time.

四十二天的皇帝——李自成攻陷北京與明朝的覆亡

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
崇禎十七年（1644年）三月，一支由驛卒出身的起義軍領袖率軍攻入北京，明朝末代皇帝在紫禁城後方一座小山自縊殉國。你認為，這位起義軍領袖其後能否順利建立長治久安的新王朝？他最終的命運又是如何？
背景速覽

核心人物：李自成（闖王）；崇禎帝朱由檢；明將吳三桂。

事件：1644年李自成攻陷北京，明朝覆亡；隨後吳三桂引清兵入關，李自成兵敗身死。

時代背景：明末天災連年、遼餉屢加、驛站裁撤引發失業潮，加上官僚腐敗，激起大規模民變。

李自成畫像，明末民變領袖，1644年攻陷北京，明朝覆亡。來源：維基媒體共享資源

李自成，陝西米脂人，早年在驛站充當驛卒，崇禎二年（1629年）朝廷為節省開支裁撤驛站，李自成因而失業，其後投身起義軍，輾轉成為闖王高迎祥麾下部將，高迎祥於崇禎九年（1636年）兵敗被殺後，李自成繼稱「闖王」，成為明末民變聲勢最大的一支武裝。崇禎年間陝西、河南接連大旱、蝗災，加上遼東軍餉屢屢加派，百姓賦稅沉重，民不聊生，李自成軍隊提出「均田免賦」、「迎闖王，不納糧」的口號，所到之處開倉放糧，深得饑民擁戴，隊伍迅速壯大至數十萬人。

崇禎十七年（1644年）正月，李自成在西安稱帝，國號大順，改元永昌，隨即揮軍東進，三月十九日攻破北京城，崇禎帝在煤山（今景山）一棵老槐樹下自縊殉國，臨終衣襟上寫下「諸臣誤朕」，統治二百七十六年的明朝就此滅亡。李自成入城後嚴令軍紀，一度秋毫無犯，然而其部將劉宗敏隨後對明朝降臣及富戶大肆拷掠追贓，引發士紳階層強烈不滿，鎮守山海關的明將吳三桂本已有意投降大順，聞訊後轉而引清兵入關。

同年四月，李自成親率大軍進逼山海關，與吳三桂及清攝政王多爾袞聯軍在石河一片石激戰，大順軍寡不敵眾，一片石之役慘敗，被迫退回北京，倉促稱帝後即棄城西走，大順政權由盛轉衰不過月餘。李自成其後轉戰陝西、湖廣一帶，屢戰屢敗，翌年（1645年）於湖北通山縣九宮山遭地方鄉勇襲殺，一代梟雄結局成謎，明清易代的巨變自此底定。

The Cannon That Ended the Middle Ages

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In the spring of 1453, a 21-year-old sultan positioned an enormous cannon, too heavy to move quickly, outside walls that had defended the world’s most fortified city for over a thousand years. Before reading on, what do you think finally let the attackers breach walls that had turned back every previous siege, and what long-term shift in world trade did the city’s fall help trigger?
Context

Key figures: Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II (穆罕默德二世); Byzantine Emperor Constantine XI Palaiologos (君士坦丁十一世).

Event at stake: The 53-day siege and fall of Constantinople (君士坦丁堡) on 29 May 1453.

Big picture: The end of the roughly 1,100-year Byzantine Empire (拜占庭帝國), a turning point often used to mark the close of the medieval period in European history.

A fresco depicting the 1453 siege of Constantinople (君士坦丁堡), which ended the Byzantine Empire (拜占庭帝國). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Constantinople’s (君士坦丁堡) triple-layered Theodosian Walls (狄奧多西城牆) had turned back every siege for over a thousand years, so when the 21-year-old Ottoman (鄂圖曼) Sultan Mehmed II (穆罕默德二世) inherited the throne determined to finally capture the city, he needed something the walls had never faced. He commissioned a Hungarian engineer named Orban (奧爾班) to cast an enormous bombard nearly nine metres long, capable of firing stone balls weighing over 270 kilograms, and gathered an army of roughly 80,000 men against a defending garrison of barely 7,000, including hired Genoese (熱那亞) soldiers under Giovanni Giustiniani.

The siege began in April 1453, with Mehmed’s cannon (大炮) pounding the walls day and night while Byzantine Emperor Constantine XI Palaiologos (君士坦丁十一世) organised increasingly desperate nightly repairs. When defenders strung a great chain across the Golden Horn (金角灣) to block naval attack, Mehmed had his ships hauled overland on greased wooden rails around the barrier, a stunt that stunned the defenders. After 53 days, Ottoman troops finally breached the walls on 29 May through a small unlocked gate called the Kerkoporta (凱爾卡波爾塔門), and Constantine XI died fighting in the breach, still wearing his imperial purple, his body never conclusively identified afterward.

Constantinople’s fall ended the Byzantine Empire (拜占庭帝國) after nearly 1,100 years as the last living link to ancient Rome (羅馬), and Mehmed II, now styled “the Conqueror” (征服者), converted the great church of Hagia Sophia (聖索菲亞大教堂) into a mosque and made the city his new Ottoman capital, later renamed Istanbul (伊斯坦堡). Greek scholars fled west carrying classical manuscripts that helped fuel the Renaissance (文藝復興) in Italy, while Ottoman control over the overland trade routes (貿易路線) to Asia pushed European powers to seek sea routes instead, a search that would soon lead Columbus (哥倫布) west across the Atlantic.

石棺中的頓悟——王陽明如何在瘴癘之地悟出「知行合一」

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
正德三年（1508年），一位因觸怒權宦而被貶至貴州荒僻驛站的官員，在一個石穴中日夜靜坐沉思。某夜，他忽然大悟，從此改變了中國思想史的走向。你認為，他悟出的道理，與當時盛行、要求人窮究外物之理的程朱理學，會有甚麼根本分別？
背景速覽

核心人物：王陽明（王守仁，1472–1529），明代思想家；因觸怒宦官劉瑾遭貶謫貴州龍場。

核心思想：龍場悟道——「聖人之道，吾性自足」；知行合一，反對將知、行截然二分。

時代背景：明代中葉程朱理學成為科舉正統，王陽明早年篤信格物窮理卻屢屢受挫，貶謫絕域反而促成其思想突破。

王陽明（王守仁）畫像，明代思想家，1508年於貴州龍場悟道，提出「知行合一」。來源：維基媒體共享資源

王陽明，本名王守仁，浙江餘姚人，早年篤信朱熹「格物致知」之說，相傳曾依朱熹教誨「格」庭前竹子七日七夜，欲窮究竹中之理，結果一無所得，反而病倒，自此對程朱理學心生疑惑。正德元年（1506年），宦官劉瑾擅權，王陽明因上疏營救因彈劾劉瑾而下獄的官員戴銑等人，觸怒劉瑾，被廷杖四十，幾乎斃命，其後更遭貶謫至萬山叢棘、瘴癘橫行的貴州龍場（今貴州修文）擔任驛丞，途中劉瑾更派人暗殺，王陽明佯裝投水始得脫身。

龍場地處偏遠，言語不通，隨行僕從相繼病倒，王陽明索性鑿石為棺，自誓「吾惟俟命而已」，日夜端居澄默，反覆思索「聖人處此，更有何道」。正德三年（1508年）某夜，他忽然大悟格物致知之旨，明白「聖人之道，吾性自足，向之求理於事物者誤也」，即所謂「龍場悟道」——天理本自具足於人心，毋須外求於事事物物。此後他提出「心即理」，又倡「知行合一」，主張知與行本是一體，真知必然表現於行動，空談道理而不實踐，只是「未知」。

王陽明「知行合一」正是針對當時科舉士人空談性理、知行脫節的流弊而發，晚年他進一步提煉出「致良知」之說，認為人人心中皆有與生俱來、能辨是非善惡的「良知」，為學工夫只在於掃除私欲遮蔽，將良知不受阻礙地推致於日用行事之中。王陽明一生歷經貶謫絕域、平定寧王之亂等重大考驗，其學說後世稱為「陽明心學」，與朱熹「程朱理學」分庭抗禮，影響遍及明清士人，並東傳日本，成為江戶時代陽明學的思想源頭。

The Philosopher Who Went Looking for a Monster and Found a Bureaucrat

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In 1961, philosopher Hannah Arendt travelled to Jerusalem expecting to witness an obviously monstrous man on trial for organising the logistics of mass murder during the Holocaust. Before reading on, what do you think she found instead when she actually observed the defendant in the courtroom, and how might that observation have changed her whole theory of how atrocities happen?
Context

Key figures: Hannah Arendt (漢娜·鄂蘭, 1906–1975), German-American philosopher; Adolf Eichmann (阿道夫·艾希曼), Nazi bureaucrat on trial in Jerusalem.

Idea at stake: Arendt’s 1963 concept of the “banality of evil” (平庸之惡), developed while covering Eichmann’s trial for The New Yorker.

Big picture: A challenge to the assumption that history’s worst atrocities require monstrous perpetrators, arising from her direct observation of an ordinary-seeming bureaucrat on trial for organising mass murder.

Hannah Arendt (漢娜·鄂蘭), whose coverage of the 1961 Eichmann trial produced her controversial concept of the “banality of evil” (平庸之惡). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

German-American philosopher Hannah Arendt (漢娜·鄂蘭), who had fled Nazi Germany (納粹德國) in 1933, travelled to Jerusalem (耶路撒冷) in 1961 to cover the trial of Adolf Eichmann (阿道夫·艾希曼) for The New Yorker magazine. Eichmann had organised the logistics of deporting millions of Jews to concentration camps (集中營) during the Holocaust (大屠殺), and Arendt expected to observe a monstrous, obviously evil figure. Instead she found a nondescript, almost dull bureaucrat who insisted throughout the trial that he had merely followed orders and advanced his career, showing no apparent hatred or ideological fervour toward his victims.

In her controversial 1963 book Eichmann in Jerusalem (《艾希曼在耶路撒冷》), Arendt coined the phrase “the banality of evil” (平庸之惡) to describe what she witnessed: Eichmann committed unprecedented atrocities not out of monstrous conviction but through a disturbing lack of thought, an unwillingness to imagine his victims’ perspective, and a bureaucratic focus on efficiently completing his assigned tasks. This challenged the widespread assumption that history’s worst crimes require exceptionally evil individuals, suggesting instead that ordinary conformity, careerism, and thoughtlessness within a totalitarian (極權主義) system could produce equally horrific outcomes. The book provoked fierce backlash from readers who felt it minimised Eichmann’s culpability.

Critics, including many former friends, accused Arendt of letting Eichmann off too easily and of blaming Jewish councils that had cooperated under duress with Nazi deportation orders, a controversy that cost her lasting friendships within the intellectual community she belonged to. Later historians, drawing on additional archives, have argued Eichmann was more ideologically committed than he appeared at trial, complicating Arendt’s original portrait, though her core insight, that ordinary thoughtlessness within a bureaucratic (官僚) system can enable atrocity, remains widely cited across psychology (心理學), political science (政治學), and organisational ethics today.

🧠 Psychology

Why the Least Skilled Often Feel Most Confident

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In a 1999 psychology study, researchers tested people’s ability to judge grammar, logic, and humour, then asked each participant to estimate how well they had done compared to everyone else. Before reading on, what pattern do you think emerged between how poorly someone actually performed and how accurately they judged their own performance?
The Dunning-Kruger effect (鄧寧-克魯格效應) — confidence and actual competence plotted against each other. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In 1999, psychologists David Dunning (大衛·鄧寧) and Justin Kruger (賈斯汀·克魯格) at Cornell University (康乃爾大學) tested participants on grammar, logic, and humour, then asked each person to estimate their own percentile ranking compared to everyone else who took the test. They found a consistent pattern: participants who scored in the bottom quartile substantially overestimated their own ability, often guessing they had performed above average, while the most skilled participants tended to slightly underestimate their own relative standing. This asymmetry, now widely known as the Dunning-Kruger effect (鄧寧-克魯格效應), suggested that the skills needed to perform a task well overlap heavily with the skills needed to judge one’s own performance accurately.

The underlying explanation is what Dunning and Kruger called a “dual burden”: someone who lacks competence in a domain typically also lacks the specific knowledge required to recognise their own mistakes, creating a kind of double blindness where incompetence masks itself from the very person experiencing it. Meanwhile, highly skilled people often assume tasks that feel easy to them must feel similarly easy to others, a related bias called the “curse of knowledge” (知識的詛咒), which leads them to underestimate how unusual their own competence actually is relative to the general population.

The original finding has faced serious statistical scrutiny in recent years; some researchers argue that a similar bottom-overestimates, top-underestimates pattern emerges from simple regression to the mean (均值回歸) and measurement noise even in randomly generated data, without any genuine psychological effect at all. Dunning himself has since clarified that the core insight isn’t about stupid people being uniquely overconfident, but about how everyone, in domains where they personally lack expertise, tends to lack the specific self-awareness needed to recognise that gap, a much broader and more universally applicable caution.

The Spasm With No Known Purpose That Everyone Gets Anyway

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Hiccups strike almost everyone, from developing fetuses in the womb to grown adults after eating too fast, yet they serve no clear survival function that scientists have been able to identify. Before reading on, what do you think actually triggers the sudden, involuntary spasm behind a hiccup, and why does it come with that distinctive “hic” sound?
The phrenic nerve (膈神經) and diaphragm (橫膈膜) — the reflex pathway behind every hiccup. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

A hiccup begins with a sudden, involuntary contraction of the diaphragm (橫膈膜), the dome-shaped muscle beneath the lungs that normally contracts smoothly and continuously during ordinary breathing. This spasm is triggered through a reflex arc (反射弧) involving the phrenic nerve (膈神經), which runs from the neck down to the diaphragm, and the vagus nerve (迷走神經), which connects to the throat, stomach, and diaphragm alike; irritation anywhere along this pathway, from eating too quickly, swallowing air, drinking carbonated drinks, or sudden temperature changes, can set the reflex off unpredictably.

Roughly a tenth of a second after the diaphragm contracts, the sudden inrush of air causes the glottis (聲門), the space between the vocal cords, to snap shut abruptly, producing the characteristic “hic” sound. Because both the phrenic and vagus nerves pass close to numerous other structures in the neck and chest, hiccups can occasionally signal an underlying problem elsewhere, such as acid reflux (胃酸倒流) irritating the oesophagus or, in persistent rare cases lasting more than 48 hours, nerve compression or even a stroke (中風), though the overwhelming majority resolve on their own within minutes.

No one has ever established a clear evolutionary purpose for hiccups, though one intriguing theory notes that fetuses hiccup frequently in the womb, and some researchers speculate the reflex may be an evolutionary leftover from amphibian ancestors (兩棲類祖先) who used similar muscle contractions to push water past developing gills while keeping air out of primitive lungs. Most home remedies, holding your breath, drinking water upside down, or being startled, work by interrupting the vagus or phrenic nerve signal rather than through any special ingredient, which is why such wildly different tricks can all occasionally succeed.

💊 Health

What the Evidence Actually Says About Skipping Meals on Purpose

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Intermittent fasting (間歇性斷食), eating only within a restricted daily window or skipping meals entirely on certain days, has become one of the most popular diet trends in recent years, credited with everything from weight loss to slowed ageing. Before reading on, what do you think the actual scientific evidence shows: does fasting produce benefits beyond what you’d get from simply eating fewer calories overall?
Intermittent fasting (間歇性斷食) — research suggests its benefit comes mainly from eating less overall, not from unique metabolic effects. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Intermittent fasting (間歇性斷食) covers several distinct approaches, including time-restricted eating (限時進食), where all meals happen within a set daily window such as eight hours, and alternate-day fasting (隔日斷食), where calorie intake is drastically cut every other day. Proponents point to animal studies and a handful of human trials showing that beyond simple calorie restriction, fasting periods can trigger a shift researchers call “metabolic switching” (代謝轉換), where the body depletes its glucose (葡萄糖) stores and begins burning fat for fuel, producing ketones (酮體) that some researchers believe carry additional cellular benefits.

A landmark 2022 randomised trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine, however, compared time-restricted eating directly against simple calorie-matched restriction and found no significant difference in weight loss or metabolic markers between the two approaches when total calories consumed were kept equal. This finding, echoed by several subsequent meta-analyses, suggests that for most people, intermittent fasting’s real-world benefit comes primarily from making it easier to unintentionally eat less overall, since a shorter eating window naturally limits opportunities to snack, rather than from any unique metabolic magic occurring specifically during the fasting hours themselves.

Fasting isn’t risk-free for everyone: people with a history of disordered eating (飲食失調), pregnant or breastfeeding women, and individuals with diabetes (糖尿病) managing blood sugar with medication should approach any fasting protocol cautiously and ideally under medical supervision. For healthy adults who find a restricted eating window genuinely easier to sustain than traditional calorie counting, intermittent fasting remains a perfectly reasonable tool, but current evidence does not support the stronger claims sometimes made about unique anti-ageing or disease-reversing effects beyond what any effective, sustainable calorie deficit would produce.

🏋 Sports Science

The 30-Minute Protein Window That Isn’t Really There

🤔 Predict Before You Read
For years, gym culture has insisted you must consume protein within roughly 30 minutes after finishing a workout, or you’ll waste much of the muscle-building benefit of your session, a rule often called the “anabolic window.” Before reading on, what do you think more rigorous research has found about how strict this timing requirement actually is?
Bodybuilding (健美運動) — decades of research now show total daily protein intake matters far more than precise post-workout timing. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The “anabolic window” (合成代謝窗口) theory holds that muscle protein synthesis (肌肉蛋白質合成) is dramatically elevated for a brief period immediately after resistance training, and that consuming protein outside this narrow window squanders much of a workout’s muscle-building potential. This idea gained popularity partly from early studies on fasted athletes and became embedded in gym culture through supplement marketing, leading many lifters to rush post-workout shakes within minutes of racking their last set, sometimes even prioritising speed over quality or adequate quantity of protein intake.

An influential 2013 meta-analysis by Brad Schoenfeld (施恩菲爾德) and Alan Aragon (阿拉貢) reviewed the existing research and found that when total daily protein intake (蛋白質攝取量) is already adequate, roughly 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight, the precise timing of any single protein dose has little to no meaningful effect on muscle growth or strength gains over weeks and months. Muscle protein synthesis actually stays elevated for 24 to 48 hours after resistance training, not just 30 minutes, meaning the real “window” is far wider than gym folklore suggests, especially for anyone who ate a normal meal within a few hours of training.

What still matters far more than precise timing is hitting adequate total daily protein and getting it spread across multiple meals, roughly three to four servings of 0.3 to 0.4 grams per kilogram of bodyweight each, since this spacing keeps muscle protein synthesis elevated more consistently throughout the day than concentrating it all in one huge post-workout dose. For most recreational lifters, worrying obsessively about a supplement shake the moment they finish a set matters far less than simply eating enough protein-rich food somewhere across the day.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

The Chart That Predicted GameStop Thirteen Years Early

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In January 2021, shares of video-game retailer GameStop (遊戲驛站) shot up over 1,700% in under two weeks, driven not by hedge funds or institutional investors but by a coordinated buying wave from retail traders (散戶投資者) organising on the Reddit forum r/WallStreetBets. Before reading on, what do you think was actually happening mechanically to the hedge funds who had bet against GameStop, and had anything like this ever happened before at an even larger scale?
Volkswagen’s (福士汽車) share price during the October 2008 short squeeze (逼空) — briefly the world’s most extreme, thirteen years before GameStop. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

By late 2020, hedge funds including Melvin Capital (梅爾文資本) had built enormous short positions (沽空倉位) against GameStop (遊戲驛站), betting its stock would keep declining as the struggling brick-and-mortar retailer lost ground to digital game downloads, with short interest at one point exceeding 100% of all available shares. Traders on the Reddit forum r/WallStreetBets (華爾街賭注論壇), led by an amateur investor known as Roaring Kitty (咆哮小貓), identified this vulnerability and began coordinating enormous, sustained buying, driving the price from under $20 to over $480 within days and forcing short sellers to buy back shares at ever-higher prices to limit their losses.

This buying-to-cover dynamic is called a short squeeze (逼空), and it wasn’t new: in October 2008, an even more extreme squeeze briefly made Volkswagen (福士汽車) the world’s most valuable company on paper, after Porsche (保時捷) quietly revealed it had secretly accumulated over 74% of Volkswagen’s shares through derivatives, cornering the market against short sellers who suddenly discovered almost no shares remained available to buy back, sending the stock up roughly 350% in two trading days before regulators intervened over the lack of disclosure.

Both squeezes reveal the same underlying mechanic: when short sellers (沽空者) collectively bet against more shares than realistically exist in free float, any sudden, coordinated buying pressure can force them into a self-reinforcing scramble to buy back stock at any price, regardless of the company’s actual business fundamentals. GameStop’s 2021 episode additionally triggered congressional hearings (國會聽證會) into trading-app restrictions after Robinhood and other brokers temporarily halted purchases, and Melvin Capital, which lost billions, shut down entirely by 2022, a reminder that concentrated short positions carry risks that go beyond ordinary business analysis.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

Why Striking Oil Sometimes Makes a Country Poorer

A Dutch natural gas (天然氣) field near Slochteren — the 1959 discovery that gave “Dutch disease” (荷蘭病) its name. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
Venezuela (委內瑞拉), once Latin America’s richest nation and holder of the world’s largest proven oil (石油) reserves, saw its GDP shrink over 75% between 2013 and 2021 alongside hyperinflation (惡性通脹) exceeding 1,000,000%, collapsing catastrophically despite, not because of, sitting atop enormous petroleum wealth.
📖 Theory
Economists W. Max Corden and J. Peter Neary formalised “Dutch disease” (荷蘭病) in 1982, named for the Netherlands’ manufacturing decline after 1959 gas discoveries: resource booms appreciate the currency and pull capital into extraction, crowding out manufacturing and agriculture, leaving economies dangerously undiversified once commodity prices fall.
⚠️ Anomaly
Yet Norway (挪威) and Botswana (博茨瓦納) built stable, diversified economies from similarly large resource windfalls, while Venezuela and Nigeria suffered severe damage from comparable oil wealth, a divergence that currency-appreciation mechanics alone cannot explain.
🧪 Challenger
Economists Halvor Mehlum, Karl Moene, and Ragnar Torvik (2006) argue institutional quality (制度質素), not resource wealth itself, determines outcomes: strong property rights and accountable governance channel windfalls productively, while weak institutions let resource rents fuel corruption and rent-seeking (尋租) — though how much institutions can improve after a boom begins remains an open question.
💡 Oil and gas don’t curse a country — weak institutions turn a windfall into a trap.
