Cliffs Made of Gas and Light: JWST’s (韋伯太空望遠鏡) Cosmic Cliffs (宇宙峭壁) in the Carina Nebula (船底座星雲)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In July 2022, NASA (美國太空總署) unveiled one of the first full-colour images from the James Webb Space Telescope (韋伯太空望遠鏡): a jagged, glowing landscape that looked exactly like a mountain range at sunset, complete with peaks, valleys and a hazy blue horizon. Before reading on, what do you think these towering “cliffs” were actually made of?
The Carina Nebula (船底座星雲) in its entirety, a vast star-forming region about 7,600 light-years away — home to the smaller “Cosmic Cliffs” region JWST imaged in close-up. Credit: Harel Boren / Wikimedia Commons

On 12 July 2022, NASA (美國太空總署) released one of the James Webb Space Telescope’s (韋伯太空望遠鏡) first full-colour images: a rugged landscape of glowing peaks and valleys nicknamed the Cosmic Cliffs (宇宙峭壁), sitting at the edge of a star-forming region called NGC 3324 within the vast Carina Nebula (船底座星雲), roughly 7,600 light-years away. The “cliffs” are not rock at all but the wall of a giant cavity carved into a cloud of cold gas and dust by ultraviolet radiation and fierce stellar winds pouring off young, massive stars just out of frame — a boundary between star-birth and empty space, glowing where starlight strikes it directly.

What made the image extraordinary was what Hubble (哈勃太空望遠鏡) could never fully show: Webb’s (韋伯太空望遠鏡) near-infrared camera (NIRCam) sees through the obscuring dust that blocks visible light, revealing dozens of previously hidden protostars — stars still forming inside their dusty cocoons — as pinpoints of light scattered across the cliff face, some shooting out narrow jets of gas visible as faint red streaks. Earlier Hubble images of the same region showed a similarly dramatic but far hazier scene, with much of this stellar nursery’s inner structure lost behind curtains of dust that infrared light simply passes through.

The Cosmic Cliffs (宇宙峭壁) sit within the broader Carina Nebula (船底座星雲), one of the largest and brightest star-forming regions in the night sky, home to some of the most massive, luminous stars known, including Eta Carinae (船底座伊塔星), which may one day explode as a supernova. Astronomers use the region as a laboratory for watching how radiation from young, massive stars actively reshapes and eventually disperses the very clouds that gave birth to them — a process called photoevaporation, visible in real time along the eroding edges of the cliffs Webb captured in unprecedented detail.

「白登之圍」：劉邦兵敗匈奴與漢初和親政策的起點

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
漢朝開國皇帝親率大軍北伐強敵，卻反被圍困於荒山七日，糧道斷絕，隨時可能全軍覆沒。你認為，最終他是憑武力突圍，還是靠其他手段脫身？
背景速覽

核心人物：漢高祖劉邦；匈奴冒頓單于；謀士陳平。

事件：公元前200年，白登之圍，劉邦率軍北伐匈奴，反遭冒頓單于圍困於白登山（今山西大同附近）七日。

時代背景：漢朝甫立國不久，國力未穩，北方匈奴在冒頓單于統領下勢力空前強盛，屢屢南侵，劉邦決意御駕親征，卻輕敵冒進。

劉邦（漢高祖）畫像，白登之圍的主角。來源：維基媒體共享資源

公元前200年，漢高祖劉邦聞韓王信勾結匈奴叛漢，親率三十二萬大軍北伐。冒頓單于故意示弱，將精銳兵馬與肥壯牛馬隱藏起來，只讓老弱殘兵出面迎戰，誘使劉邦輕敵冒進。劉邦不待步兵主力到齊，僅率先頭騎兵疾馳至白登山（今山西大同附近），豈料冒頓早已布下四十萬精銳騎兵，將劉邦及其先鋒部隊團團圍困於山上，內外音信斷絕，糧草補給隨即中斷，漢軍陷入進退兩難的絕境，一連七日未能突圍，軍心漸漸動搖。

危急關頭，謀士陳平獻計，暗中派人攜帶厚禮，秘密求見冒頓單于的閼氏（王后），並暗示劉邦已備妥美女圖像，一旦解圍便將獻上絕色美人取悅單于，恐怕會奪去閼氏的寵愛。閼氏聞言心生忌憚，加上原本與劉邦約定會師的另一路匈奴援軍遲遲未至，冒頓心中亦生疑慮，擔心腹背受敵，於是聽從閼氏勸說，主動撤開包圍圈一角。劉邦趁著大霧瀰漫、視線不清之際，下令全軍張弓搭箭戒備，緩緩自缺口突圍而出，終於與後續步兵主力會合，撿回一命。

白登之圍後，劉邦深刻體會漢朝立國未穩、國力遠遜匈奴的現實，遂採納劉敬建議，改採「和親」政策，將宗室女子遠嫁匈奴單于，並每年餽贈絲綢、酒食等厚禮，換取邊境暫時安寧。這項忍辱負重的外交策略延續至漢初數代君主，惠帝、文帝、景帝皆蕭規曹隨，直至國力累積至漢武帝一朝，方才一改守勢，轉為主動出兵反擊匈奴。白登之圍因此成為漢初對外政策的重要轉折點，也奠定了「先忍讓、後圖強」的治國思路。

The Five Minutes That Turned the Pacific: The Battle of Midway (中途島海戰), 1942

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Six months after Pearl Harbor (珍珠港), Japan’s navy (日本海軍) still held the initiative in the Pacific, planning to lure and destroy America’s remaining aircraft carriers near a tiny atoll. Before reading on, what single piece of American intelligence do you think made the difference between an ambush and a trap?
Context

Key figures: Admiral Chester Nimitz (尼米茲), US Pacific Fleet commander; Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto (山本五十六), Japanese Combined Fleet commander; codebreaker Joseph Rochefort (羅徹福特).

Event at stake: The Battle of Midway (中途島海戰), fought 4–7 June 1942 near Midway Atoll (中途島) in the central Pacific.

Big picture: Six months after Pearl Harbor (珍珠港), Yamamoto planned to draw out and destroy America’s remaining carriers by attacking Midway — but American codebreakers had already read enough of his plan to set a trap of their own.

USS Yorktown (約克鎮號) struck by a torpedo during the Battle of Midway (中途島海戰), 4 June 1942. Credit: US Navy / Wikimedia Commons

By mid-1942, Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto (山本五十六) planned to finish what Pearl Harbor (珍珠港) had started by luring America’s three remaining aircraft carriers into a decisive battle near Midway Atoll (中途島), a strategically placed pair of islets northwest of Hawaii (夏威夷). But US Navy codebreakers at Station HYPO, led by Joseph Rochefort (羅徹福特), had partially cracked Japan’s naval code JN-25 and suspected the target was Midway; to confirm it, they had the base broadcast a fake message reporting a water-shortage, then intercepted a Japanese radio report referencing exactly that problem at “AF” — confirming Midway as the target.

Forewarned, Admiral Chester Nimitz (尼米茲) rushed the carrier USS Yorktown (約克鎮號), badly damaged weeks earlier at the Battle of the Coral Sea (珊瑚海海戰), through emergency repairs in just 72 hours to join Enterprise (企業號) and Hornet (大黃蜂號) in ambush position northeast of Midway, hidden from Japanese scout planes. On the morning of 4 June, American dive bombers arrived at the exact moment Japan’s four carriers — Akagi (赤城), Kaga (加賀), Sōryū (蒼龍) and Hiryū (飛龍) — were mid-refuel and rearming aircraft on their decks, fuel lines and bombs exposed with no fighter cover aloft to intercept.

Within minutes, dive bombers from Enterprise (企業號) and Yorktown (約克鎮號) set three of the four Japanese carriers ablaze in a single strike, and Hiryū (飛龍) was sunk later that day after a desperate counterattack that fatally damaged Yorktown. Japan lost four fleet carriers, a heavy cruiser, and hundreds of experienced pilots and aircrew it could never fully replace, while the US lost only Yorktown and a destroyer. The battle permanently shifted naval air superiority in the Pacific to the United States (美國), just six months after Pearl Harbor (珍珠港), and historians widely regard it as the war’s turning point in the Pacific theatre.

法藏「因陀羅網」：華嚴宗如何以寶珠比喻萬法互攝互入

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
一位高僧為了向女皇解釋「萬物互相包含」這個抽象道理，決定不用長篇說教，而是搬來十面鏡子做了一場現場示範。你認為，他打算展示的畫面會是甚麼樣子？
背景速覽

核心人物：法藏（643–712），華嚴宗三祖，武則天時代高僧。

核心思想：因陀羅網喻，出自《華嚴經》，以帝釋天宮殿寶珠網比喻萬法互攝互入、一即一切、一切即一的圓融境界。

時代背景：唐代佛教宗派林立，華嚴宗致力建立一套涵蓋「理事無礙、事事無礙」的圓融宇宙觀，以回應當時佛教內部對現象與本體關係的種種爭論。

法藏畫像，十三世紀日本繪畫。來源：維基媒體共享資源

法藏是唐代華嚴宗實際創宗者，深受武則天禮遇，曾多次奉詔於宮中及長安各大寺院講經說法。相傳武則天讀《華嚴經》艱澀難明，法藏遂設計了一場著名的「十面鏡子」示範：於室內四面八方及上下共安置十面鏡子，中央置一尊佛像，並點燃一支蠟燭。武則天入室觀看，只見十面鏡子彼此互相映照，佛像與燭光的影像在鏡與鏡之間重重反射，層層疊疊，無窮無盡，卻又秩序井然，毫不混亂，直觀呈現出「一即一切、一切即一」的義理。

這場示範所要展現的，正是《華嚴經》「因陀羅網」之喻：傳說忉利天主帝釋天的宮殿懸掛著一張綴滿寶珠的巨網，每一顆寶珠皆映現出其餘所有寶珠的影像，而每顆寶珠中的影像，又各自映現出全部寶珠的影像，如此重重無盡，互攝互入。法藏藉此說明，宇宙萬法看似各自獨立，實則彼此緣起相依、互相含攝——任何一法的存在，都以其餘一切法的存在為條件，牽一髮而動全身，這正是「法界緣起」思想的核心。

法藏另著〈金獅子章〉，以宮殿中一尊金獅子像為喻，指出獅子的「金」（本體）與「獅子相」（現象）看似有別，實則理事圓融無礙，二者不可分割，進一步將因陀羅網的抽象譬喻落實為具體可感的教學工具。這套「理事無礙、事事無礙」的圓融哲學，經澄觀、宗密等後繼者發揚，深刻影響宋明理學「理一分殊」的思想架構，亦透過禪宗與日本華嚴宗東傳，成為東亞佛教思想史上最具系統性的形上學體系之一。

Back to the Things Themselves: Edmund Husserl (胡塞爾) and the Birth of Phenomenology (現象學)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
A philosopher proposes a strange discipline: before asking whether anything you perceive actually exists “out there,” first set that question aside entirely and describe, with total precision, exactly how it appears to your consciousness. Before reading on, what do you think could be gained by deliberately suspending judgment about the real world just to study appearances?
Context

Key figures: Edmund Husserl (胡塞爾, 1859–1938), German philosopher; studied under Franz Brentano (布倫塔諾); later influenced Martin Heidegger (海德格) and Jean-Paul Sartre (沙特).

Idea at stake: Whether philosophy should start from unexamined assumptions about an external world, or instead begin with the rigorous, careful description of how things actually appear to consciousness.

Big picture: Writing in early 20th-century Germany against both rigid natural science and vague speculative metaphysics, Husserl sought a new “rigorous science” grounded directly in lived experience.

Edmund Husserl (胡塞爾) in the 1910s, founder of phenomenology (現象學). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Edmund Husserl (胡塞爾) founded phenomenology (現象學) by taking up his teacher Franz Brentano’s (布倫塔諾) idea of intentionality (意向性) — the claim that consciousness is always consciousness of something, never simply free-floating awareness. In his Logical Investigations (1900–01) and Ideas (1913), Husserl argued that before philosophy asks whether the external world truly exists as we perceive it, it should first master a more basic task: describing, with total rigour, exactly how objects present themselves to consciousness, stripped of unexamined assumptions inherited from science, common sense, or prior philosophy.

His central method, the epoché (存而不論) or “bracketing,” asked philosophers to deliberately suspend judgment about whether perceived objects really exist independently, not to deny the external world, but to redirect attention entirely onto the structure of experience itself — how a chair, a memory, or a mathematical proof actually appears to a mind encountering it. Husserl’s motto, “to the things themselves” (面向事物本身), captured this ambition: philosophy’s job was not to theorise about reality from a distance, but to examine, patiently and without presupposition, the raw texture of lived experience as it is actually given.

Husserl’s method reshaped 20th-century continental philosophy: his student and later rival Martin Heidegger (海德格) redirected phenomenology (現象學) toward the question of Being itself in Being and Time, while Jean-Paul Sartre (沙特) and Maurice Merleau-Ponty (梅洛龐蒂) built existentialist and embodied theories of consciousness on Husserl’s foundations. Late in life, stripped of his professorship and library access under Nazi race laws because of his Jewish ancestry, Husserl died in 1938 largely isolated from the philosophical mainstream he had founded — a painful irony given how thoroughly his method had already reshaped the field.

Why Choosing Your Own Lottery Numbers Feels Luckier: The Illusion of Control (控制錯覺)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Two people each buy a lottery ticket with identical odds of winning — one picks their own numbers, the other is handed a ticket with numbers already chosen for them. Before reading on, do you think the person who picked their own numbers would value their ticket the same as the other person, or differently?
Ellen Langer (蘭格), the Harvard psychologist behind the 1975 illusion-of-control study. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In a landmark 1975 study, psychologist Ellen Langer (蘭格) sold office workers $1 lottery tickets, letting half the participants choose their own numbers while simply handing pre-selected tickets to the rest — a purely cosmetic difference, since every ticket carried identical odds. Just before the drawing, Langer’s team approached each participant and asked what price they would accept to sell their ticket back. Participants who had chosen their own numbers demanded, on average, roughly four times more money than those who had been handed a ticket, despite knowing full well that chance alone would determine the outcome.

Langer termed this the illusion of control (控制錯覺): an inappropriate sense of personal influence over outcomes that are, in reality, governed purely by chance. Her follow-up experiments identified the specific factors that trigger it — active choice, competition against a weaker-seeming opponent, familiarity with the materials involved, and practice or rehearsal beforehand — all cues normally associated with skill-based tasks, which the mind appears to misapply to genuinely random ones. Simply making a choice, even a meaningless one, was enough to make an outcome feel more controllable and therefore more valuable.

The illusion of control (控制錯覺) shows up well beyond lotteries: casino gamblers often throw dice harder for high numbers and softer for low ones, as if muscular effort could influence a physical outcome already determined by momentum and friction, and retail investors frequently trade more actively than a passive index strategy would justify, misreading their ability to analyse a stock as control over its price. Recognising the specific cues that trigger this illusion — choice, competition, familiarity, practice — is often the first step toward resisting overconfidence in situations genuinely governed by chance rather than skill.

🫀 Physiology

Why Fear and Cold Both Raise Your Skin: The Science of Goosebumps (雞皮疙瘩)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
A chilling piece of music, a frightening noise, and a cold gust of wind can all produce the exact same physical reaction on your skin — tiny bumps rising around each hair. Before reading on, what do you think connects such completely different triggers — temperature, fear, and even beautiful music — to one single reflex?
Goosebumps (雞皮疙瘩), caused by the arrector pili muscles (豎毛肌) contracting around each hair follicle. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Goosebumps, medically called cutis anserina (雞皮疙瘩), form when tiny bands of smooth muscle called arrector pili (豎毛肌), attached to the base of every hair follicle, contract and pull the follicle upright, creating a small raised bump in the surrounding skin. This reflex is controlled entirely by the sympathetic nervous system (交感神經系統) — the same fight-or-flight network that speeds the heart and dilates the pupils — meaning goosebumps arise completely involuntarily, without any conscious muscular effort, triggered automatically whenever this branch of the nervous system fires in response to cold, fear, or intense emotion.

The reflex is an evolutionary holdover from furrier ancestors: raising each hair trapped a thicker layer of insulating air against the skin, providing genuine warmth in cold conditions, while the same reflex also made a frightened or threatened animal’s coat visibly puff up, making it appear larger and more intimidating to a predator or rival — a phenomenon called piloerection (立毛反應), still highly effective in cats and porcupines today. In modern, largely hairless humans, neither benefit meaningfully applies anymore, yet the underlying nerve circuit never disappeared, so the reflex still fires reliably even though it no longer does the job it evolved for.

Strikingly, the same reflex also fires in response to powerful music, awe-inspiring scenery, or emotionally charged memories — a phenomenon researchers call frisson (音樂寒顫), sometimes nicknamed “skin orgasm.” Brain-imaging studies suggest frisson activates reward and pleasure circuitry similar to that triggered by food or other strongly reinforcing stimuli, hijacking a defensive, temperature-regulating reflex for an entirely unrelated emotional purpose. Because arrector pili muscles are controlled involuntarily, no amount of conscious effort can produce goosebumps on command — they arise only when the sympathetic nervous system genuinely registers cold, threat, or overwhelming feeling.

💊 Health

Do Collagen Supplements (膠原蛋白補充劑) Actually Firm Your Skin?

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Collagen is the most abundant protein in the human body and a key structural component of skin — and it’s now sold in powders, drinks, and capsules promising firmer, more hydrated skin. Before reading on, do you think swallowing collagen actually gets incorporated into your skin the way the marketing implies?
Human skin structure (皮膚結構), showing the collagen-rich dermis layer that supplements target. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Collagen (膠原蛋白) is the most abundant protein in the human body, forming the structural scaffolding of skin, cartilage, and tendons; natural production declines by roughly one percent per year starting in a person’s mid-twenties, contributing to thinner, less elastic skin over time. This decline has fuelled a large market for oral collagen supplements (膠原蛋白補充劑), marketed on the premise that ingesting collagen directly restores what the body is losing — but this framing glosses over a basic fact of digestion: swallowed collagen protein is broken down by stomach acid and enzymes into individual amino acids and small peptides long before it ever reaches the bloodstream, exactly like any other dietary protein.

Despite this, several small randomised trials and a 2021 systematic review published in the International Journal of Dermatology found modest but measurable improvements in skin elasticity and hydration among people taking hydrolysed collagen peptides for eight to twelve weeks, compared with placebo. The proposed mechanism isn’t that collagen itself reaches the skin intact, but that specific collagen-derived peptides (含羥脯胺酸胜肽) act as signalling molecules, prompting the body’s own fibroblast cells (成纖維細胞) to ramp up their own collagen production — an indirect, peptide-signalling effect rather than direct replacement, and one still not fully mapped at the mechanistic level.

Two caveats temper the modest positive findings: effect sizes across studies remain small, and a substantial share of the supporting research has been funded or conducted by supplement manufacturers themselves, a known source of bias in nutrition science. For most people, ensuring adequate overall dietary protein, sufficient vitamin C (維他命C) — an essential cofactor the body needs to synthesise its own collagen — and consistent sun protection likely matters more for long-term skin structure than any specific collagen supplement, which at best appears to offer a modest assist rather than the dramatic reversal implied by marketing claims.

🏋 Sports Science

Do Compression Garments (壓縮衣) Really Speed Up Recovery?

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Marathon runners, footballers, and gym-goers increasingly wear tight compression sleeves and tights after training, convinced it helps them recover faster for the next session. Before reading on, do you think the evidence for this is strong, mixed, or essentially non-existent?
A full-body compression garment (壓縮衣), the type of tight-fitting sportswear tested in recovery research. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Compression garments (壓縮衣) — tight-fitting sleeves, socks, and tights applying steady external pressure to the limbs — are widely worn during and after exercise on the theory that they reduce muscle soreness, limit swelling, and speed recovery of strength and power. An influential 2014 meta-analysis published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine by Hill and colleagues pooled results across multiple controlled trials and found small-to-moderate reductions in delayed-onset muscle soreness (延遲性肌肉痠痛, DOMS) and modestly faster recovery of muscle function after exercise-induced damage, though the researchers cautioned that overall evidence quality remained mixed and effect sizes were generally small.

Several mechanisms have been proposed to explain the modest benefit: external pressure may enhance venous and lymphatic return, helping clear metabolic byproducts and reduce fluid pooling and swelling in damaged tissue; compression may also physically limit muscle oscillation during high-impact or eccentric movements, reducing the micro-tearing that triggers soreness in the first place. A third, harder-to-rule-out factor is psychological: the firm, supportive sensation of compression garments may itself reduce perceived soreness independent of any underlying physiological change, meaning some of the measured benefit could reflect expectation as much as mechanism.

Not all compression products are equal: medical-grade garments delivering 15–20 mmHg or higher pressure have more consistent supporting evidence than generic athletic compression wear, whose actual pressure levels are rarely disclosed or standardised. Evidence for wearing compression garments during exercise to boost performance itself is considerably weaker than for the post-exercise recovery use case; most of the measurable benefit shows up specifically when garments are worn in the hours after a hard session, making post-workout compression a reasonable, low-risk addition to a recovery routine rather than a proven performance enhancer.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

Deliberately Causing a Recession to Kill Inflation: Paul Volcker (沃爾克) and the 1980s Disinflation

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In 1979, a US Federal Reserve (聯邦儲備局) chairman took office facing double-digit inflation and made a decision few politicians would dare: deliberately engineer a severe recession to break the public’s expectation that prices would keep rising. Before reading on, what do you think happened to his popularity — and to inflation — over the next few years?
Paul Volcker (沃爾克), Federal Reserve (聯邦儲備局) chairman 1979–1987, in 1989. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

When Paul Volcker (沃爾克) took over as chairman of the Federal Reserve (聯邦儲備局) in August 1979, US inflation was running above 11 percent and climbing, driven partly by years of loose monetary policy and repeated oil shocks. In October 1979, in a move later nicknamed the “Saturday Night Massacre,” Volcker shifted the Fed’s entire operating strategy away from targeting interest rates directly and toward tightly restricting the growth of the money supply instead, deliberately allowing interest rates to rise however high the market demanded — a sharp break from decades of Fed practice designed above all to avoid triggering exactly the kind of economic pain this shift would soon cause.

The federal funds rate (聯邦基金利率) spiked to nearly 20 percent by 1981, dragging the economy through a severe double-dip recession that pushed unemployment to 10.8 percent by late 1982, the worst US jobless rate since the Great Depression at that time. The pain fell hardest on interest-rate-sensitive industries: homebuilders nailed plywood coffins labelled with Volcker’s name and mailed them to the Fed, and farmers drove tractors to Washington to blockade the Federal Reserve building in protest, furious at borrowing costs that made mortgages, farm loans, and car financing suddenly unaffordable across the country.

Yet the strategy worked precisely as Volcker (沃爾克) intended: US inflation fell from roughly 13.5 percent in 1980 to about 3.2 percent by 1983, breaking the entrenched expectation that prices would keep spiralling upward and restoring the Fed’s long-term credibility as an inflation fighter. The recovery that followed helped set the stage for the sustained low-inflation growth of the 1980s and 1990s, and Volcker’s willingness to absorb severe short-term political and economic pain for long-term monetary credibility remains a foundational case study cited by central bankers confronting inflation crises worldwide.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

One Boatlift, Two Economists, No Agreement: The Mariel Boatlift (馬列爾偷渡潮) Wage Debate

Cuban refugees arriving during the 1980 Mariel boatlift (馬列爾偷渡潮), the natural experiment at the centre of a decades-long economics dispute. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
In 1980, Fidel Castro (卡斯特羅) allowed roughly 125,000 Cuban refugees to leave for the United States via the port of Mariel (馬列爾) over a few chaotic months, expanding Miami’s labour force by about seven percent almost overnight, concentrated heavily among low-skilled workers competing for similar jobs.
📖 Theory
Standard labour economics predicts that a sudden, large increase in the supply of low-skilled workers should depress wages and employment for competing native low-skilled workers, all else being equal — a straightforward application of basic supply-and-demand reasoning to local labour markets.
⚠️ Anomaly
Economist David Card’s (卡德) influential 1990 study compared Miami’s low-skill wages and unemployment against similar cities and found essentially no measurable negative effect on native low-skilled workers — a result long cited as evidence that local labour markets absorb immigration shocks more easily than simple models predict.
🧪 Challenger
In 2017, George Borjas (博爾哈斯) reanalysed the data using a narrower comparison group — non-Hispanic male high-school dropouts — and reported significant wage declines for that specific subgroup, reigniting fierce methodological debate; economists Peri and Yasenov (2019) defended Card’s broader approach, questioning the reliability of Borjas’s small sample. The dispute remains unresolved, a genuinely open question in immigration economics.
💡 Even economists working from the same natural experiment can reach opposite conclusions depending on exactly which workers they compare.
📖 Card, D. (1990). Industrial and Labor Relations Review, 43(2). Borjas, G. (2017). ILR Review, 70(5). Peri, G. & Yasenov, V. (2019). Journal of Human Resources, 54(2).

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 8 & 9 — Real But Modest: Collagen supplements and compression garments are both products with plausible biological mechanisms, small real effects confirmed by legitimate research, and marketing that vastly oversells what that research actually shows. Collagen peptides may modestly nudge skin cells to produce more of their own collagen; compression may modestly aid venous return and limit muscle oscillation — genuine physiology in both cases, wrapped in hype that promises transformation rather than the incremental assist the data actually supports.

So what: When evaluating a wellness product, look past whether a mechanism sounds plausible and ask what effect size the actual trials measured — “modest but real” and “dramatic as advertised” are very different claims, and usually only one of them is true.
