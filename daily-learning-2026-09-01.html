The Ten Days Hubble Pointed at "Nothing" and Found 3,000 Galaxies

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In December 1995, astronomers pointed the Hubble Space Telescope at a tiny patch of sky in Ursa Major that looked, to the naked eye, completely empty — no stars, no visible objects, nothing worth studying. The telescope's director spent ten consecutive days of his own discretionary observing time on it anyway, over strong objections from other scientists. Before reading on, what do you think a ten-day stare at "empty" sky actually revealed — and why would risking that much telescope time on nothing be worth it?
The Hubble Deep Field (1995) — a ten-day exposure of a seemingly empty patch of sky in Ursa Major that revealed roughly 3,000 previously unseen galaxies. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In December 1995, Robert Williams (羅伯特·威廉斯), director of the Space Telescope Science Institute (太空望遠鏡科學研究所), made a controversial call: he spent 100 of his own discretionary observing orbits — roughly ten consecutive days — pointing the Hubble Space Telescope (哈伯太空望遠鏡) at a single patch of sky in the constellation Ursa Major (大熊座), no larger than a grain of sand held at arm's length. Ground-based telescopes showed nothing there but a scattering of faint stars. Many astronomers criticized the decision as a reckless waste of one of the most oversubscribed instruments in scientific history on what looked like empty darkness.

Hubble captured 342 separate exposures over those ten days, later combined into a single image now known as the Hubble Deep Field (哈伯深空). Rather than emptiness, the image revealed nearly 3,000 previously unseen galaxies (星系) packed into that tiny sliver of sky — spirals, ellipticals, and faint smudges so distant their light had traveled billions of years to reach the telescope, showing the universe as it looked when it was a fraction of its current age. The result suggested galaxies extend to the faintest limits any telescope of that era could detect, with no empty gaps once you looked closely enough.

The experiment was repeated and pushed further in 2003–2004 as the Hubble Ultra-Deep Field (哈伯超深空), staring even longer at another apparently blank patch and finding roughly 10,000 galaxies, some among the earliest formed after the Big Bang (大爆炸). Together, the images reshaped how astronomers estimate the total number of galaxies in the observable universe, revising figures upward into the hundreds of billions. The lesson embedded in both images has outlived the specific numbers: pointed carefully enough, even the sky's most convincingly "empty" corners turn out to be dense with things no one had thought to look for.

西南戰爭——武士刀為何擋不住徵兵農民的步槍？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
1877年，日本明治政府剛推行徵兵制不過數年，一支由農民組成、訓練未足一年的新式陸軍，竟要迎戰由西鄉隆盛率領、以精銳武士為骨幹的九州叛軍。你認為，缺乏世代習武根基的徵兵農民，最終能否擊敗身經百戰的武士軍團？決定勝負的，會是甚麼因素？
背景速覽

核心人物：西鄉隆盛（號稱「維新三傑」之一，薩摩藩出身，舉兵領袖）；大久保利通（西鄉昔日盟友，時任內務卿，明治政府實際掌權者）；山縣有朋（徵兵制的主要推手，陸軍卿）。

事件：1877年，鹿兒島士族不滿明治政府廢刀令與秩祿處分，擁立西鄉隆盛舉兵反抗，史稱「西南戰爭」，最終於城山之役潰敗，西鄉切腹自盡。

時代背景：明治維新推行「四民平等」、廢除武士特權、改行徵兵制，觸怒大量失去俸祿與地位的士族；西南戰爭是明治初年規模最大、也是最後一場士族反叛。

西南戰爭（1877年）軍事行動示意圖，標示西鄉軍與政府軍主要交戰地點。來源：維基媒體共享資源

明治政府自1873年起推行徵兵令，規定男丁不論出身皆須服役，同時陸續實施廢刀令與秩祿處分，取消武士配刀特權及世襲俸祿。鹿兒島（薩摩藩舊地）士族對此極度不滿，聚集於西鄉隆盛創辦的私學校，私下操練，隱然自成一股武裝力量。1877年初，明治政府察覺私學校圖謀不軌，密令運走鹿兒島的軍火儲備，激起學生強烈反彈，衝入軍械庫奪取武器。西鄉隆盛原本無意舉兵，眼見部下情緒已無法平息，最終選擇順應民意，率領約一萬三千名士族北上，正式起兵反抗中央政府。

叛軍南下攻打熊本城，守城的谷干城指揮鎮台守軍頑強抵抗，成功拖延西鄉大軍達五十餘日，讓政府軍贏得寶貴的集結時間。雙方其後在熊本近郊的田原坂展開日本內戰史上最慘烈的血戰，鏖戰十七晝夜，政府軍憑藉徵兵農民與警視隊拔刀隊的步槍與火炮優勢，逐步壓制以武士刀近戰見長的薩摩軍。田原坂一役成為戰爭轉捩點，西鄉軍傷亡慘重，被迫且戰且退，往鹿兒島方向撤退，昔日盟友大久保利通此時正坐鎮東京，全力調度討伐軍資源。

經過數月轉戰，西鄉隆盛率殘部退守鹿兒島城山，被政府軍重重包圍，兵力已不足四百人。1877年9月24日，政府軍發動總攻，西鄉在激戰中身受重傷，由親信別府晉介介錯，切腹自盡，「西南戰爭」至此平定。這場戰爭是明治初年規模最大的士族叛亂，也是日本武士階級最後一次以武裝形式對抗中央政府；徵兵農民組成的新式陸軍證明步槍與現代軍事組織足以擊敗傳統武士，象徵日本軍事現代化正式取代舊有武士制度。

The Fortress Stormed for Gunpowder That Became a Revolution's First Day

🤔 Predict Before You Read
On 14 July 1789, a crowd of Parisians marched on a centuries-old fortress-prison, not primarily to free prisoners — it held only seven at the time — but to seize the gunpowder stored inside. Before reading on, what do you think happened when the fortress's governor, badly outnumbered, first tried to negotiate a surrender — and why would this single, fairly small clash come to symbolize an entire revolution?
Context

Key figures: Bernard-René de Launay (德洛奈), governor of the Bastille (巴士底獄); the Paris crowd and newly-formed citizen militia; Camille Desmoulins (德穆蘭), whose fiery speech two days earlier helped ignite the unrest.

Event at stake: the storming of the Bastille (攻陷巴士底獄) fortress-prison on 14 July 1789, seized primarily for its gunpowder stores.

Big picture: France's monarchy faced a deepening financial crisis and food shortages; the recently convened Estates-General (三級會議) and Third Estate's National Assembly (國民議會) had raised hopes of reform that the king's troop movements around Paris seemed to threaten.

A contemporary depiction of the storming of the Bastille, 14 July 1789. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

By July 1789, Paris was gripped by rumors that King Louis XVI (路易十六) was massing troops around the city to crush the newly self-declared National Assembly (國民議會) and reverse the reforms it demanded. Camille Desmoulins (德穆蘭), a young lawyer, leapt onto a café table on 12 July and delivered an impassioned speech urging Parisians to arm themselves before the crown struck first. Crowds raided the Hôtel des Invalides (榮軍院) the next morning, seizing roughly 30,000 muskets, but found almost no gunpowder to fire them — and knew a large store sat behind the walls of the Bastille (巴士底獄).

The Bastille was a medieval fortress-turned-state-prison holding just seven inmates that day, but its 30-meter walls and cannon made it both a practical target for gunpowder and a hated symbol of royal absolutism. Governor Bernard-René de Launay (德洛奈) initially negotiated with a delegation, but confusion and gunfire broke out, and after several hours of chaotic fighting that killed roughly 100 attackers, de Launay surrendered under a promise of safe passage. The crowd broke that promise almost immediately: de Launay was beaten, stabbed, and beheaded, his head paraded through the streets of Paris (巴黎) on a pike.

News of the fall of the Bastille spread rapidly through France, triggering the "Great Fear" (大恐慌) — waves of peasant uprisings against feudal landlords across the countryside — and convinced Louis XVI to formally recognize the National Assembly's authority days later. Though militarily minor compared to later revolutionary battles, the storming became the founding myth of the French Revolution (法國大革命), commemorated ever since as Bastille Day (巴士底日), France's national holiday. The fortress itself was demolished within months, its stones sold as souvenirs, leaving almost no physical trace beyond an outline still marked in a Paris square.

荀子性惡論——人性本惡，聖人之道從何而來？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
孟子主張「性善」，認為人天生具備惻隱、羞惡之心；荀子卻反其道而行，斷言「人之性惡，其善者偽也」。你認為，若人性本惡，禮義道德這些美好的東西，究竟從何而來？一個相信人性本惡的思想家，會如何解釋社會秩序何以可能？
背景速覽

核心人物：荀子（荀況，戰國末期趙國人，儒家代表人物之一）；孟子（性善論倡議者，荀子理論上的對立面）；韓非、李斯（荀子門下弟子，後成法家代表人物）。

事件：荀子提出「性惡論」，主張人性本惡，善行乃後天禮義教化所致，直接挑戰孟子的性善主張。

時代背景：戰國末期禮崩樂壞、諸侯混戰不休，儒家內部就人性本質展開激烈辯論，各家亟需為亂世提出治國之道的理論根基。

荀子畫像，戰國末期儒家思想家，提出「性惡論」。來源：維基媒體共享資源

荀子生於戰國末期禮制崩壞、戰亂頻仍的趙國，親眼目睹社會秩序徹底失控的亂象，這深刻影響了他對人性的判斷。他直言「人之性惡，其善者偽也」——人與生俱來只有好利、疾惡、耳目之欲等本能，若順其自然發展，必然引發爭奪、殘賊，導致社會混亂；所謂善良品行，並非天生本性，而是「偽」，即後天人為努力教化的結果。這番論斷正面挑戰了孟子「人皆有惻隱之心」的性善主張，成為儒家內部一場延續千年的重要辯論。

荀子以「木受繩則直，金就礪則利」為喻，說明彎曲的木材唯有經過墨繩矯正才能挺直，鈍化的金屬唯有經過磨礪才能鋒利；人性亦然，唯有透過「禮義」與「師法」的持續教化與約束，才能將與生俱來的惡性導正為善行。他特別強調「師法」的重要性——沒有良師益友的引導與規範制度的約束，人心中好利求勝的本能便無從節制。這套主張與孟子截然不同：孟子認為教育是喚醒人心中本有的善端，荀子則認為教育是徹底改造、壓制與扭轉本性的過程。

荀子這套「化性起偽」的理論，賦予禮法制度極高的正當性與必要性——正因人性本惡，社會才更需要嚴謹的禮義規範與明確的師法教導來維持秩序，這也是他晚年在稷下學宮講學時反覆強調的重點。有趣的是，他門下兩位最著名的弟子韓非與李斯，日後皆脫離儒家框架，發展出強調法令刑賞而非禮義教化的法家思想，某程度上正是將荀子「性惡」前提推向極端後的邏輯結果，也讓後世對荀子究竟該歸入儒家抑或視為法家先聲，爭論至今未休。

The Bet Blaise Pascal Said You Can't Refuse to Place

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Blaise Pascal argued that even if you have no evidence either way for God's existence, pure self-interested logic still compels you to bet that God exists. Before reading on, what do you think his reasoning was — and can you spot the flaw that centuries of philosophers have since pointed at?
Context

Key figures: Blaise Pascal (帕斯卡), 17th-century French mathematician (數學家) and philosopher; the skeptical libertines (自由思想家) he wrote against at Port-Royal (皇家港修道院).

Problem at stake: whether it is rational to believe in God (上帝) when reason alone cannot prove or disprove God's existence.

Moment: Pascal's unfinished notes, published posthumously in 1670 as the Pensées (沉思錄), written while defending Christianity to skeptical, rationalist contemporaries.

Blaise Pascal, whose posthumously published Pensées contains the wager now bearing his name. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Blaise Pascal (帕斯卡) approached the question of God's existence not as a theologian appealing to faith, but as a mathematician applying decision theory (決策理論) under uncertainty. He argued that reason alone cannot settle whether God exists, so a thinking person is effectively forced to wager one way or another through how they choose to live. Rather than trying to prove God's existence, Pascal's Pensées (沉思錄) instead asked: given that uncertainty, which bet is rational to place, purely in terms of what you stand to gain or lose?

His argument, now known as Pascal's Wager (帕斯卡的賭注), laid out the stakes as asymmetric: if you believe in God and God exists, you gain infinite reward; if you believe and God doesn't exist, you lose comparatively little; if you disbelieve and God exists, you risk infinite loss; if you disbelieve and God doesn't exist, you gain little. Since any finite cost is dwarfed by an infinite potential reward or loss, Pascal concluded that believing is the only rational bet, regardless of how improbable God's existence might seem.

Critics attacked the wager almost immediately. Denis Diderot (狄德羅) raised the "many gods" objection: the same logic could justify betting on any of thousands of possible deities, each demanding contradictory forms of worship, leaving the wager unable to pick one. Others noted that belief isn't a switch a person can simply flip for strategic advantage — you cannot sincerely believe in God purely because the expected-value math favors it. The wager nonetheless remains a foundational case study in decision theory (決策理論) under uncertainty, still taught wherever expected-value reasoning meets questions reason alone cannot answer.

Why a Tax Refund Feels Different From a Paycheck, Even Though Money Is Money

🤔 Predict Before You Read
A dollar earned from your salary and a dollar received as a surprise tax refund are, mathematically, identical — perfectly interchangeable. Before reading on, what do you think happens when researchers track how people actually spend windfall money versus earned income — and why might your brain refuse to treat both as the same currency?
Money is fungible in theory, but mental accounting shows people sort it into separate, non-interchangeable categories. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In the 1980s, economist Richard Thaler (泰勒) documented a pattern he called "mental accounting" (心理帳戶): rather than treating money as a single, fungible pool, people mentally sort it into separate categories — rent money, grocery money, vacation savings, "fun money" — and treat transfers between those categories as psychologically costly, even though a dollar is a dollar regardless of which mental envelope it came from. This directly contradicts standard economic theory, which assumes money is perfectly interchangeable and that only total wealth, not its labeled source, should influence spending decisions.

Thaler and psychologist Eric Johnson (詹森) demonstrated a striking version of this in 1990 casino experiments: gamblers who had just won money treated subsequent bets made with those winnings very differently from bets made with their original stake, taking noticeably bigger risks with what they mentally labeled "house money" (莊家的錢) — because losing it back felt less like a real loss than losing money they'd brought from home. The same effect explains why people who receive a surprise bonus or tax refund often spend it more freely on indulgences than they would an equivalent amount from their regular salary.

Mental accounting shows up constantly outside the casino: people carry high-interest credit card debt (信用卡債) while simultaneously keeping low-interest savings accounts untouched, mentally treating the two as separate, non-interacting buckets rather than netting them against each other, which a purely rational actor would do instantly. Recognizing the bias doesn't require abandoning budgeting categories entirely — Thaler himself noted mental accounts can be a useful self-control tool — but it does mean noticing when a label on money, rather than its actual value, is quietly steering a decision.

Why Reading in the Back Seat Makes Some Stomachs Turn

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Sailors, car passengers, and airplane travelers can all develop nausea from motion that a driver at the same wheel rarely feels. Before reading on, what do you think is actually happening inside the body during motion sickness — and why might staring at a fixed point on the horizon genuinely help?
Motion sickness arises from conflicting signals between the inner ear's vestibular system and the eyes. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Motion sickness (暈動症) arises from a mismatch between two of the body's motion-sensing systems: the vestibular system (前庭系統), fluid-filled canals in the inner ear that detect actual physical movement and acceleration, and the visual system, which reports what the eyes perceive. Reading a book in a moving car creates exactly this conflict — the inner ear correctly senses the car turning, braking, and accelerating, while the eyes, fixed on a stationary page, report no motion at all. The brain receives two flatly contradictory signals about whether the body is moving, and struggles to reconcile them.

Psychologist Michel Treisman's (特里斯曼) influential 1977 hypothesis proposed an evolutionary explanation for why this sensory conflict (感官衝突) produces nausea specifically: throughout most of human evolutionary history, this exact pattern of contradictory motion signals — feeling movement without seeing it, or vice versa — reliably indicated that a neurotoxin (神經毒素) had impaired the nervous system, since no other common cause would create that mismatch. The nausea and vomiting reflex, on this account, isn't a malfunction at all; it's the body's ancient defense mechanism misfiring, treating the sensory confusion as a false alarm for poisoning and attempting to purge it.

This explains several well-known remedies: staring at a fixed point on the distant horizon (地平線) realigns the visual system with what the inner ear already senses, resolving the conflict directly, which is why drivers rarely get sick while passengers reading or looking at phone screens often do. Repeated exposure also helps through vestibular habituation (前庭適應) — the brain gradually adjusts its expectations, which is why sailors develop "sea legs" over days at sea, and why the same adjustment temporarily reverses once back on solid ground as "land sickness," proving the mismatch, not the motion itself, is the real trigger.

💊 Health

The Symptom With No Name Until One Letter Invented a Syndrome

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In 1968, a single anecdotal letter to a medical journal, describing symptoms the author felt after eating at Chinese restaurants, gave rise to a widely believed food-safety myth that persists over fifty years later. Before reading on, what do you think decades of controlled, blinded scientific testing eventually found about the ingredient blamed?
Monosodium glutamate (MSG) crystals — decades of blinded studies found no consistent link to the symptoms once blamed on it. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In 1968, physician Robert Ho Man Kwok (郭浩民) wrote a brief, informal letter to the New England Journal of Medicine describing numbness, weakness, and palpitations he sometimes felt after eating at Chinese restaurants, speculating that monosodium glutamate (麩胺酸鈉, MSG), a common flavor-enhancing seasoning, might be responsible. The letter wasn't a controlled study — just one person's personal anecdote — yet it rapidly spread through media coverage and popular culture, coining the term "Chinese Restaurant Syndrome" (中餐館症候群) and cementing MSG as a widely feared food additive almost overnight, based on essentially no rigorous evidence at all.

Subsequent double-blind, placebo-controlled studies (雙盲安慰劑對照研究) — where neither researchers nor participants knew who received MSG versus an inert substance — consistently failed to reproduce the claimed reaction reliably. A 2000 review by Geha and colleagues in the Journal of Nutrition, pooling multiple rigorous trials, found no consistent evidence that MSG in typical dietary amounts causes the symptoms described, even among people who believed themselves sensitive to it; suggestibility and expectation, rather than the ingredient itself, appeared to explain most reported reactions under blinded conditions.

MSG occurs naturally in tomatoes, parmesan cheese, and seaweed, and is chemically identical whether extracted or manufactured; major regulatory bodies including the FDA and WHO classify it as safe (GRAS status) at typical dietary levels. Food historians and scientists have also increasingly criticized the "Chinese Restaurant Syndrome" label itself as reflecting mid-20th-century xenophobia toward Chinese cuisine specifically, since the same seasoning used liberally in many Western processed foods rarely draws the same scrutiny or fear.

🏋 Sports Science

The 30-Minute Post-Workout Window That Turned Out to Be Hours Wide

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Gym culture has long insisted you must consume protein within roughly 30 minutes of finishing a workout, or you'll waste the muscle-building benefits of the session entirely. Before reading on, what do you think happens to muscle growth if that window is missed by an hour or two — and how wide does research actually suggest the real window is?
Total daily protein intake, not precise post-workout timing, is what the evidence says drives muscle growth. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The "anabolic window" (合成代謝窗口) belief holds that a narrow, roughly 30-to-60-minute period immediately following resistance training is uniquely critical for consuming protein, and that missing it meaningfully undermines muscle protein synthesis (肌肉蛋白質合成) and hypertrophy gains. The idea gained enormous traction through supplement marketing and widely repeated gym folklore, encouraging the popular image of lifters rushing a shaker bottle to their mouths the instant they rack the final set, treating minutes of delay as if they carried real consequences for months of training progress.

An influential 2013 meta-analysis by Alan Aragon (阿拉貢) and Brad Schoenfeld (勳菲爾德) in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition reviewed the existing evidence and found the timing effect, where it existed at all, was far smaller than assumed and easily overridden by other factors — most notably total daily protein intake (每日總蛋白質攝取量), typically recommended around 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight. A direct randomized trial by Schoenfeld's team the same year found no significant strength or hypertrophy difference between immediate versus several-hours-delayed post-workout protein, provided total daily intake was matched.

Muscle protein synthesis actually remains elevated for roughly 24 to 48 hours after a resistance training session, not minutes, meaning the effective "window" is closer to a full day than a rushed half-hour. A pre-workout meal containing protein, still being digested during and after training, further widens this window considerably, making the urgency behind shaker-bottle sprints largely unfounded for anyone eating regular, protein-adequate meals across the day. Total daily protein intake and consistent training, not precise timing, remain the dominant drivers of muscle growth.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

The Day a Strategy Built to Prevent Crashes Helped Cause One

🤔 Predict Before You Read
On 19 October 1987, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 22.6% in a single trading day — still the largest one-day percentage drop in stock market history — despite no major economic catastrophe triggering it that morning. Before reading on, what do you think caused a crash of that scale with no clear external shock, and how might a strategy designed to protect investors from losses have made things worse?
The S&P 500 around Black Monday, 19 October 1987, when the Dow fell 22.6% in a single session. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Through the mid-1980s, a strategy called portfolio insurance (投資組合保險), developed by finance professors Hayne Leland (利蘭) and Mark Rubinstein (魯賓斯坦), grew popular among large institutional investors. It used computer models to automatically sell stock index futures (股指期貨) as markets fell, aiming to synthetically replicate a protective put option and limit downside losses without ever needing to sell the actual underlying stocks. By October 1987, tens of billions of dollars in institutional assets were managed under versions of this strategy, all programmed to react to falling prices in essentially the same automated way.

On 19 October 1987 — later called Black Monday (黑色星期一) — a modest initial decline triggered automated portfolio insurance programs to sell futures contracts, which pushed prices down further, triggering yet more automated selling in a self-reinforcing feedback loop that program trading (程式交易) accelerated far faster than human traders could intervene or interpret. The Dow fell 508 points, 22.6%, in a single session, with no comparable single-day percentage crash before or since, despite underlying corporate earnings and economic fundamentals showing no equivalent deterioration that day.

Markets recovered relatively quickly, regaining their pre-crash levels within about two years, but regulators concluded that automated, correlated selling strategies acting in unison had turned a manageable decline into a self-inflicted crash. The New York Stock Exchange subsequently introduced circuit breakers (熔斷機制) — automatic trading halts triggered by large rapid price moves — specifically to interrupt this kind of feedback loop before it could cascade again, a direct regulatory response still in use across major exchanges worldwide today.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

The Trade-Off That Vanished the Moment Everyone Learned About It

Milton Friedman, whose expectations-augmented Phillips Curve explained why the 1970s inflation-unemployment trade-off broke down. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
Through the 1970s, the 1973 and 1979 oil shocks (石油危機) pushed US inflation above 11% while unemployment simultaneously climbed toward 9% — high inflation and high unemployment ("stagflation," 停滯性通脹) arriving together, a combination existing economic models said should not happen.
📖 Theory
Economist A.W. Phillips's (菲利普斯) 1958 empirical study, popularized by Paul Samuelson and Robert Solow, described a stable inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment. Policymakers treated this Phillips Curve (菲利普斯曲線) as a dial they could adjust through demand management.
⚠️ Anomaly
1970s stagflation broke the curve's core prediction: inflation and unemployment rose together for years rather than trading off, leaving standard Keynesian demand-management tools unable to fix either problem without worsening the other.
🧪 Challenger
Milton Friedman (弗里德曼) and Edmund Phelps's (菲爾普斯) expectations-augmented Phillips Curve (1967–68) argued the trade-off only holds short-term: once workers and firms adjust their inflation expectations, the curve shifts and the trade-off disappears at a "natural rate" of unemployment (自然失業率) — reshaping central banking toward credible inflation-targeting.
💡 A policy trade-off stops working the moment the people it's aimed at start expecting it — reality adjusts to being modeled.
📖 Phillips, A.W. (1958), Economica 25(100); Friedman, M. (1968), "The Role of Monetary Policy," American Economic Review 58(1); Phelps, E.S. (1967), Economica 34(135). Never recycles Card 10 domain (portfolio-insurance feedback loops in equity markets vs. macroeconomic policy trade-off theory).

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 5 & 11 — When the Model Assumes You Won't React: Pascal's Wager calculates an infinite expected payoff for believing in God, treating belief as a stake you can rationally choose to place once the arithmetic favors it. Milton Friedman's expectations-augmented Phillips Curve made a strikingly similar point about economic policy: once workers and firms catch on to a government's plan to trade a little more inflation for a little less unemployment, they adjust their expectations, and the very trade-off a simple model predicted quietly disappears.

So what: Any tidy decision framework — about faith, investing, or economic policy — has a shelf life once the people inside it start anticipating the rule itself; a model that assumes the world holds still rarely survives contact with people who are paying attention.
