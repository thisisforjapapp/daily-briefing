🔭 Astronomy & Cosmos

The Guest Star That Blazed for 23 Days: How Song-Dynasty Astronomers Recorded SN 1054 — and Left Modern Science an Exact Birth Certificate for the Crab Nebula (蟹狀星雲)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In 1054 CE, Chinese court astronomers recorded a “guest star” bright enough to see in daylight for over three weeks. What do you think happens to a star that suddenly becomes that bright — and what might be left behind centuries later?
The Crab Nebula (蟹狀星雲) — the expanding remnant of a supernova recorded by astronomers in 1054 CE. Credit: Wikimedia Commons / NASA, ESA

On 4 July 1054, imperial astronomers in Song-dynasty China (宋朝), and independently in Japan and the Arab world, recorded a “guest star” (客星) blazing in the constellation Taurus (金牛座) — visible in daylight for 23 days and at night for nearly two years. The Song History (宋史) fixed its position near a star called Tianguan (天關), giving modern astronomers an exact explosion date 900 years later. This was a core-collapse supernova (超新星), the explosive death of a massive star. Naked-eye supernova records this precise are exceptionally rare, letting scientists calculate the expansion rate of the resulting debris with unusual accuracy.

What that star left behind is the Crab Nebula (蟹狀星雲, Messier 1) — an expanding shell of glowing gas roughly 11 light-years across, the first entry in Charles Messier’s (梅西耶) 1758 comet-hunting catalogue. At its centre spins the Crab Pulsar (蟹狀星雲脈衝星), a neutron star (中子星) barely 20 kilometres wide but denser than anything except a black hole, rotating 30 times per second and sweeping a lighthouse beam of radiation across Earth on every turn. Composite images combining Hubble’s optical data, Chandra’s X-rays, and JWST’s infrared reveal filaments still expanding at roughly 1,500 km/s.

The Crab Nebula matters because its birth date is known almost to the day — a gift no other supernova remnant offers. That certainty makes it a calibration tool for dating other remnants and testing models of neutron-star spin-down (自轉減速). It is also a rare moment where East Asian court record-keeping (史官制度), often dismissed as bureaucratic formality, became indispensable to modern physics: without Song-dynasty astronomers logging a strange light near Tianguan, the Crab’s age would be far less certain. It remains one of the most-imaged objects in the sky, a favourite target for testing new telescopes.

五十萬大軍一日覆沒，皇帝被俘：土木堡之變如何擊碎明朝盛世神話

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
假如你是明朝兵部尚書，皇帝執意御駕親征，寵信的太監堅持繞道經過自己家鄉炫耀，你會如何勸阻？若勸阻失敗，你又會如何部署撤退路線？
背景速覽

核心人物：明英宗朱祁鎮（御駕親征）、太監王振（慫恿出征並擅自改道）、兵部尚書鄺埜（隨行陣亡）、也先（瓦剌太師，率軍伏擊）、于謙（事後力主固守北京）。

事件：正統十四年（1449年）八月，土木堡之變，明軍於河北懷來土木堡遭瓦剌軍圍殲，英宗被俘。

時代背景：明朝立國已逾八十年，承平日久，武備鬆弛。北方蒙古瓦剌部在也先率領下統一諸部，屢犯邊境。王振專權，慫恿年僅二十二歲的英宗仿效祖先御駕親征，卻毫無軍事準備，倉促出兵。

土木堡之變（1449年）相關史料圖像。來源：維基媒體共享資源

正統十四年（1449年）七月，瓦剌太師也先兵分四路大舉南侵，大同告急。年僅二十二歲的明英宗在宦官王振慫恿下，不顧兵部尚書鄺埜等大臣力諫，倉促決定御駕親征。出征僅二日便籌糧不足、軍心浮動；數十萬大軍由京師倉猝拼湊而成，缺乏統一指揮，糧草補給更是一片混亂。行至大同，前線敗報頻傳，王振驚慌下令班師回朝，卻仍一意孤行，堅持繞道蔚州，只因蔚州是他的家鄉，想讓皇帝駕臨故里炫耀鄉里。

大軍剛出蔚州數十里，王振又恐大隊人馬踐踏家鄉莊稼田產，臨時改變路線折返宣府大道，白白耽誤兩日行程，讓也先騎兵得以追及。八月十三日，明軍退至土木堡（今河北懷來），本可趁夜疾進二十里入居庸關固守，王振卻堅持等候千餘輛輜重車到齊才紮營，貽誤戰機。土木堡地勢高而缺水，瓦剌軍隨即包圍水源。次日，也先佯裝議和，誘明軍移營就水，趁陣勢混亂發動總攻，明軍全線潰散，鄺埜、張輔等重臣戰死，王振為亂軍所殺，英宗本人被瓦剌騎兵俘虜——開國以來從未有過的奇恥大辱。

土木堡之變震動朝野，京師精銳幾乎全軍覆沒，也先隨即挾持英宗兵臨北京城下。危急關頭，兵部侍郎于謙力排南遷之議，擁立英宗之弟郕王（即後來的景泰帝）即位，穩定人心，並親自督戰北京保衛戰，擊退瓦剌軍。英宗雖於一年後被釋放歸國，卻已淪為太上皇，其後更爆發奪門之變重奪帝位，冤殺于謙。土木堡之變暴露明朝中期武備廢弛、宦官干政的深層危機，此後明朝由盛轉衰，終明一代再未能恢復開國初年的軍事氣勢。

Twenty-Seven Ships Against Thirty-Three: How Nelson’s (納爾遜) “England Expects” Signal and a Daring Break in the Line Destroyed Napoleon’s Navy at Trafalgar — and Cost Him His Life

🤔 Predict Before You Read
You command a fleet outnumbered in ships, sailing directly at the enemy’s broadside in the age of sail, when the standard tactic was parallel lines exchanging fire. What would make a head-on perpendicular attack worth the risk?
Context

Key figures: Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson (納爾遜, 1758–1805), British fleet commander; Admiral Pierre-Charles Villeneuve (維爾納夫), Franco-Spanish fleet commander; Vice-Admiral Cuthbert Collingwood (科林伍德), Nelson’s second-in-command.

Event at stake: Battle of Trafalgar (特拉法加海戰), 21 October 1805, off Cape Trafalgar (特拉法加角), southern Spain.

Big picture: Napoleon (拿破崙) needed naval control of the English Channel (英倫海峽) to invade Britain. Villeneuve’s Franco-Spanish fleet, blockaded at Cádiz (加的斯), was ordered south toward the Mediterranean; Nelson intercepted before it could escape.

HMS Victory (勝利號), Nelson’s flagship at Trafalgar, preserved today in Portsmouth. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

By October 1805, Napoleon (拿破崙) had already abandoned his invasion of Britain and marched the Grande Armée east toward Austria, but Admiral Villeneuve’s (維爾納夫) combined Franco-Spanish fleet of 33 ships still needed to be neutralised. Nelson’s (納爾遜) 27 ships intercepted them off Cape Trafalgar (特拉法加角) on 21 October. Rather than form the traditional parallel line of battle — ships trading broadsides in a slow, often indecisive exchange — Nelson split his fleet into two columns and drove them perpendicular straight through the enemy line, breaking it into isolated, disorganised clusters he could overwhelm one at a time.

The manoeuvre exposed his lead ships to raking fire (縱射) with no ability to shoot back during the approach — a calculated gamble accepting short-term risk for decisive advantage once through. Before the attack, Nelson raised the signal “England expects that every man will do his duty” (英國期待人人恪盡職守), rallying a fleet that trusted his judgement completely. The plan worked: 17 enemy ships were captured or destroyed, and not a single British vessel was lost. Nelson himself, aboard HMS Victory (勝利號), was struck by a sniper’s musket ball on the quarterdeck and died roughly three hours later, informed of the victory before he succumbed.

Trafalgar (特拉法加海戰) permanently destroyed Napoleon’s capacity to challenge British naval power, securing Royal Navy (皇家海軍) dominance for over a century and eliminating any realistic threat of invasion. It also cemented Nelson as Britain’s most mythologised military hero — Nelson’s Column (納爾遜紀念柱) in Trafalgar Square (特拉法加廣場) still marks the victory. Strategically, the battle illustrates a recurring lesson: doctrine built for a symmetrical, evenly matched fight can be beaten decisively by an asymmetrical approach that accepts calculated short-term vulnerability for a faster, more total outcome.

「觸事而真」：僧肇如何以〈不真空論〉調和印度般若空觀與中國玄學，成為中國佛教哲學的奠基之作

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
若萬物既非真實存在（有），也非徹底虛無（無），那麼我們每天觸摸、看見、感受的世界，究竟是什麼？
背景速覽

核心人物：僧肇（384–414年），鳩摩羅什門下高足；鳩摩羅什（西域龜茲高僧，中國佛經四大翻譯家之一）。

核心思想：〈不真空論〉；「即萬物之自虛，不假虛而虛物」；「觸事而真」；「非有非無」中道。

時代背景：魏晉南北朝玄學盛行，「有」「無」之辯是當代思想核心議題（王弼貴無、裴頠崇有）。鳩摩羅什於後秦弘始三年（401年）抵長安，主持大規模譯經事業，系統引入印度中觀學派般若空觀，僧肇身處譯場核心，親炙其學，得以精準辨析中觀「空」義與玄學「無」之根本差異。

鳩摩羅什塑像，克孜爾石窟（僧肇之師，長安譯場主持者）。來源：維基媒體共享資源

僧肇十三歲家貧為書傭，因抄寫《老子》而愛好玄學，後讀《維摩詰經》而轉信佛教，二十歲已以佛學辯才聞名長安。後秦弘始三年（401年），鳩摩羅什抵長安主持譯經，僧肇隨侍左右，參與《大品般若經》《中論》等大乘經典翻譯，深得中觀學派「緣起性空」精髓。當時玄學界正為「有」「無」問題爭論不休：王弼主張「貴無」，以無為萬物本體；裴頠反駁「崇有」，斥無不能生有。僧肇發現，兩派其實都將「空」誤解為某種實體性的「無」，與印度中觀「性空」義相去甚遠。

僧肇於是撰〈不真空論〉，指出萬物「非有非無」：說「有」，則萬物皆因緣和合而生，並無獨立自存之本質，故非真有；說「無」，則萬物畢竟顯現於眼前，可觸可用，故非徹底虛無。他提出關鍵命題「即萬物之自虛，不假虛而虛物」——萬物之空，不是外加於物之上的空，而是萬物本身因緣生滅、不真不實的內在性質。由此他總結「觸事而真」：真理不在事物之外另覓，而就在日常所觸之事物本身的緣起本質之中，此即「即事而真」的中道觀。

〈不真空論〉連同僧肇另外兩篇論文〈物不遷論〉〈般若無知論〉，合稱《肇論》，被譽為中國佛教哲學史上第一部真正貫通印度中觀學與中國本土思想語彙的著作，一舉終結了魏晉以來「格義佛教」（借玄學概念比附佛理）的階段。後世三論宗奉《肇論》為根本典籍，天台、禪宗亦深受其「即事而真」思想啟發。僧肇年僅三十一歲即被後秦皇帝姚興處死，其英年早逝與思想的成熟深度，形成中國哲學史上令人惋惜的強烈對比。

Proving God With Logic Alone: Thomas Aquinas’s (阿奎那) Five Ways (五路論證), and How He Fused Aristotle (亞里士多德) With Christian Theology to Build Natural Law (自然法) Theory

🤔 Predict Before You Read
If you had to prove God exists using only observations available to any rational person — motion, causation, the existence of things — without appealing to scripture or faith, what kind of argument would you construct?
Context

Key figures: Thomas Aquinas (阿奎那, 1225–1274), Dominican friar (道明會修士); Aristotle (亞里士多德), whose works Aquinas systematically integrated with Christian doctrine; Averroes (阿威羅伊), Islamic Aristotelian commentator whose translations reintroduced Aristotle to Europe.

Core ideas: The Five Ways (五路論證) — arguments from motion, causation, contingency, gradation, and design; Natural Law theory (自然法理論) — moral law knowable through reason; the Summa Theologica (神學大全, unfinished, 1265–1274).

Big picture: Aristotle’s works, lost to Latin Christendom for centuries, flooded back into Europe via Arabic translations and commentaries in the 12th–13th centuries, forcing theologians to reconcile pagan Greek rationalism with Christian revelation — a project Aquinas undertook more thoroughly than anyone before him.

Thomas Aquinas (阿奎那), whose synthesis of Aristotle and Christian theology shaped centuries of Western thought. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

By the 1260s, newly translated works of Aristotle (亞里士多德) — arriving in Latin Europe via Arabic commentators like Averroes (阿威羅伊) — were spreading through universities, and many theologians feared this pagan rationalism (理性主義) threatened Christian revelation. Thomas Aquinas (阿奎那), a Dominican friar (道明會修士) teaching in Paris and Naples, took the opposite view: reason and faith could not genuinely conflict, because both originated in God. His life’s project, the unfinished Summa Theologica (神學大全), attempted a systematic synthesis — using Aristotelian logic and metaphysics to demonstrate truths of the faith wherever demonstration was possible, reserving revelation for what reason alone could not reach.

His most famous contribution is the Five Ways (五路論證), five arguments for God’s existence built entirely from observable premises, not scripture. Each follows a similar structure: things move, so something must have started motion (the Unmoved Mover, 不動的推動者); things are caused, so an uncaused first cause (第一因) must exist; contingent things exist, implying a necessary being; things display gradations of perfection, implying a maximum; and natural things act purposefully, implying a designer. Aquinas did not intend these as knock-down proofs for sceptics — he considered them demonstrations that belief in God was rationally coherent, not merely a leap of faith.

Aquinas’s natural law theory (自然法理論) proved equally influential: moral truths, he argued, are knowable through reason by observing human nature’s inherent inclinations toward self-preservation, procreation, social life, and knowledge of truth — no direct revelation required. This framework shaped centuries of Catholic moral theology and later secular natural-rights philosophy, including John Locke’s (洛克) property arguments. Modern critics, from David Hume (休謨) onward, challenge whether motion or causation arguments can validly conclude anything about a specifically Christian God rather than merely a first cause — a debate philosophy of religion (宗教哲學) continues today.

The Test Question That Changed Everything: How Simply Framing an Exam Differently Can Lower Your Score — Stereotype Threat (刻板印象威脅) and What It Actually Proves

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Two groups take an identical, difficult verbal test. One group is told it “measures intellectual ability.” The other is told it’s “just a problem-solving exercise, not diagnostic of anything.” Do you think the framing alone could change scores — and for whom?
Claude Steele (史蒂爾), who co-discovered stereotype threat with Joshua Aronson. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In 1995, psychologists Claude Steele (史蒂爾) and Joshua Aronson (阿倫森) published a study in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology that identified a subtle but powerful performance drag. Black and white Stanford students of matched ability took a difficult verbal test. When the test was described as diagnostic of intellectual ability, Black students scored significantly lower than white students. When the identical test was described merely as a laboratory problem-solving exercise unrelated to ability, the racial gap virtually disappeared. Nothing about the test changed — only the framing. Steele and Aronson called this phenomenon stereotype threat (刻板印象威脅): the fear of confirming a negative stereotype about one’s group, which itself impairs performance.

The proposed mechanism is cognitive load (認知負荷): worrying about confirming a stereotype consumes working memory (工作記憶) and elevates physiological stress (壓力反應) — resources that would otherwise support the task itself. This isn’t limited to race. Subsequent studies found the effect in women taking advanced maths tests when gender was made salient, older adults taking memory tests when ageing stereotypes were primed, and white men taking sports-related tasks framed around “natural athleticism.” The common thread: any group facing a negative stereotype about a specific domain underperforms in that domain when the stereotype is made cognitively salient beforehand, even subtly — such as simply checking a demographic box first.

Stereotype threat has faced real replication scrutiny: a 2015 meta-analysis (Flore & Wicherts) found evidence of publication bias, suggesting the original effect sizes in gender-and-maths studies may be smaller than first reported. This doesn’t erase the phenomenon, but it tempers the more sweeping early claims. Practical interventions that show more consistent benefit include values-affirmation exercises (自我肯定練習) before testing and simply removing demographic questions until after the test is completed — both of which the original Steele and Aronson design directly inspired, and both now standard practice in some standardised-testing contexts.

💡 Move demographic questions to the end of a test, not the beginning — a small design choice that removes a source of measurable, unnecessary performance drag.

Check your answer
They manipulated only how the identical test was described — as diagnostic of intellectual ability versus a neutral problem-solving exercise. Since nothing about the test itself changed, the gap was driven by psychological framing and anxiety about confirming a stereotype, not by ability differences.

🫀 Physiology

How a Snail-Shaped Tube Turns Air Vibrations Into Sound: The Cochlea (耳蝸), Hair Cells (毛細胞), and the Fastest Sensory Transduction in the Human Body

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Sound is just air pressure waves. How does your body convert a physical vibration into an electrical signal your brain can interpret as “music” or “speech” — and how fast does that conversion actually have to happen?
The human ear (耳朵), showing the cochlea (耳蝸) where sound is converted into nerve signals. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Sound arrives as vibrating air pressure waves, which the eardrum (鼓膜) converts into mechanical motion, amplified roughly 20-fold by three tiny bones — the malleus, incus, and stapes (聽小骨鏈) — before reaching the cochlea (耳蝸), a fluid-filled, snail-shaped spiral in the inner ear (內耳). Inside runs the basilar membrane (基底膜), stiff and narrow near the entrance and progressively wider and floppier toward the tip. This graded stiffness means different frequencies (頻率) cause maximum vibration at different points along the membrane — high pitches peak near the entrance, low pitches near the far end — creating a physical frequency map called tonotopic organisation (音位排列).

Sitting atop the basilar membrane is the organ of Corti (柯蒂氏器), home to roughly 15,000 hair cells (毛細胞) per ear, each topped with stereocilia (靜纖毛) — microscopic hair-like projections arranged in graded rows. When the basilar membrane vibrates, stereocilia bend, mechanically stretching tip links (頂端連結) that pull open ion channels directly — no chemical intermediary, making this the fastest sensory transduction (感覺轉導) in the human body, operating in microseconds. This mechanical gating triggers an electrical signal in the auditory nerve (聽神經), which the brainstem and auditory cortex (聽覺皮層) then decode into pitch, timbre, and location.

This elegant system is also irreversibly fragile: outer hair cells amplify quiet sounds and sharpen frequency discrimination, but unlike many animal cells, mammalian hair cells (哺乳動物毛細胞) do not regenerate once destroyed — by loud noise, ototoxic drugs (耳毒性藥物), or ageing. Because high-frequency sounds peak at hair cells near the cochlea’s entrance, which take the most direct mechanical battering, noise-induced hearing loss (噪音性聽力損失) typically appears first at high frequencies — explaining why early hearing damage often shows up as difficulty distinguishing consonants in speech before a person notices any obvious loss.

💡 Hearing loss usually strikes high frequencies first because those hair cells sit nearest the cochlea’s entrance, absorbing the most mechanical punishment — protect your ears before you notice anything’s wrong.

💊 Health

Does Sunscreen Actually Prevent Skin Cancer? The Nambour Trial (南博爾試驗), SPF Myths, and What “Broad-Spectrum” Really Means

🤔 Predict Before You Read
If you apply SPF 30 sunscreen once in the morning and spend eight hours outdoors, are you protected the whole day? And does a higher SPF number mean proportionally more protection?
Melanoma (黑色素瘤), the skin cancer most strongly linked to UV exposure. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Scepticism about sunscreen efficacy has persisted for decades, largely because most supporting evidence was observational and confounded by sun-seeking behaviour. The Nambour Skin Cancer Prevention Trial (Green et al., 1999, updated 2011) resolved much of this: a randomised controlled trial in Queensland, Australia, followed over 1,600 adults for more than a decade, randomly assigning daily sunscreen use versus discretionary use. The daily-use group showed a 50% reduction in melanoma (黑色素瘤) incidence and roughly 40% fewer squamous cell carcinomas (鱗狀細胞癌) at 10-year follow-up — among the strongest causal evidence sunscreen genuinely prevents skin cancer, not merely correlates with lower-risk behaviour.

SPF (防曬指數, sun protection factor) measures protection against UVB (紫外線B) — the rays primarily responsible for sunburn and directly linked to skin cancer — but SPF numbers are not linear: SPF 30 blocks about 97% of UVB, SPF 50 blocks about 98%, a marginal gain often oversold in marketing. UVA (紫外線A) penetrates deeper, drives photoageing (光老化), and also contributes independently to skin cancer risk, which SPF alone doesn’t measure — this is why “broad-spectrum” (廣譜) labelling, covering both UVA and UVB, matters more than chasing higher SPF numbers alone.

The most common real-world failure isn’t the product but the dose and reapplication: most people apply roughly a quarter to a half of the amount used in SPF testing labs, and reapplication after two hours or after swimming or sweating is required regardless of the SPF number, since sunscreen degrades under UV exposure and can be physically removed. Contrary to persistent internet claims, sunscreen does not meaningfully block vitamin D (維生素D) synthesis at typical real-world application thickness (Neale et al., 2019), and the WHO/IARC classifies tanning bed UV radiation as a Group 1 carcinogen (一級致癌物) — a far greater risk than any sunscreen-related concern.

💡 The SPF number matters less than you think — reapplication every two hours and choosing “broad-spectrum” coverage matter more.

Do Compression Socks Actually Speed Up Recovery? What the Meta-Analyses Say About Squeezing Your Muscles After Exercise

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Compression garments are worn by elite marathoners, weekend gym-goers, and hospital patients alike. Do you think the mechanism and evidence are the same for all three uses — or completely different?
Compression garments (壓縮服) — originally medical devices, now common in sports recovery. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Compression garments (壓縮服) — tight-fitting sleeves, socks, or tights applying graduated external pressure — originated in medical contexts for treating venous insufficiency (靜脈功能不全) and deep vein thrombosis (深層靜脈血栓) prevention, where the physiological mechanism is well established: external pressure assists venous return (靜脈回流) against gravity. Their adoption in sports recovery assumes a similar logic applies to delayed onset muscle soreness (延遲性肌肉痠痛, DOMS) and exercise-induced muscle damage — that compression reduces swelling and inflammatory fluid accumulation, thereby easing soreness and speeding functional recovery.

The evidence for athletic use is real but modest. A widely cited meta-analysis (Marqués-Jiménez et al., 2016, in Physiology & Behavior, covering 42 studies) found small-to-moderate reductions in perceived muscle soreness and modest improvements in muscle power recovery when compression was worn during and after exercise, but effects on objective markers like creatine kinase (肌酸激酶, a blood marker of muscle damage) were inconsistent and often non-significant. Notably, the perceptual benefit — athletes simply feeling their muscles recovered faster — tends to be larger and more consistent than the measurable physiological benefit, suggesting a real but partly placebo-adjacent (安慰劑相關) component.

Practical guidance follows the evidence: compression garments are a low-risk, low-cost recovery tool worth using if an athlete finds them comfortable and subjectively helpful, particularly for reducing perceived soreness and swelling after long-duration or eccentric-heavy (離心收縮為主) exercise such as marathons or downhill running. They should not be relied upon as a primary recovery strategy — sleep, protein intake, and appropriate training load management remain far more evidence-backed. Wearing compression during long-haul flights, incidentally, has the strongest independent evidence base of all, for reducing venous stasis (靜脈淤滯) risk, not athletic recovery.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

Can You Beat the Market? Eugene Fama’s (法馬) Efficient Market Hypothesis (效率市場假說) Versus the Behavioural Finance Challenge That Shared His Nobel Prize

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Two economists shared the 2013 Nobel Prize in Economics for research that appears to contradict each other — one arguing markets are essentially unbeatable, the other cataloguing systematic mispricings. How can both be right?
Eugene Fama (法馬) at the 2013 Nobel Prize ceremony, shared with Robert Shiller and Lars Peter Hansen. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In 1970, Eugene Fama (法馬) formalised the efficient market hypothesis (效率市場假說, EMH): asset prices fully reflect all available information, so consistently “beating the market” through skill or analysis is essentially impossible except by chance or by taking on additional risk. Fama distinguished three forms — weak (past prices convey no edge), semi-strong (public information is already priced in), and strong (even private information is priced in) — and decades of index-fund performance data broadly support the semi-strong version: the vast majority of actively managed funds underperform low-cost index funds (指數基金) after fees, a finding Vanguard founder Jack Bogle built an entire industry around.

Robert Shiller (席勒), who shared Fama’s 2013 Nobel Prize despite reaching seemingly opposite conclusions, showed in a landmark 1981 paper that stock prices are far more volatile than can be justified by subsequent changes in dividends alone — implying prices sometimes deviate substantially from any reasonable model of fundamental value, driven by investor psychology (投資者心理) rather than information. Momentum (動量效應, Jegadeesh & Titman, 1993) and post-earnings-announcement drift are similarly well-documented, statistically robust anomalies that pure EMH struggles to explain, since prices should adjust to new information instantaneously, not drift predictably afterward for months.

The resolution most economists now accept is a spectrum rather than a binary: markets are efficient enough that reliably beating them net of fees and risk is extraordinarily difficult for the vast majority of participants, yet not so perfectly efficient that behavioural anomalies (行為異常) — driven by overconfidence, herding, and narrative-driven mispricing — never occur or never persist. The practical takeaway for most investors sits closer to Fama’s camp: broad low-cost diversification (低成本分散投資) beats stock-picking for almost everyone, even while acknowledging Shiller’s evidence that markets are not perfectly rational machines.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

Why Do Some Countries Get Stuck Just Below Rich? The Middle-Income Trap (中等收入陷阱) and the Search for What Lets Nations Escape It

Long-run GDP per capita divergence — the backdrop against which the middle-income trap is debated. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
Since the 2000s, dozens of countries — Malaysia, Thailand, Brazil, South Africa — grew into middle-income status (中等收入國家) then stalled for decades, unable to reach high-income levels, while South Korea, Taiwan, and Singapore made the leap within a generation. Only around 12 of 101 tracked economies escaped by 2023 (World Bank, 2024).
📖 Theory
Standard growth theory (增長理論, Solow model) predicts convergence: poorer countries absorb existing technology and catch up in capital per worker, so middle-income status should be a temporary staging point toward high-income convergence, not a durable trap.
⚠️ Anomaly
The “middle-income trap” (中等收入陷阱, Gill & Kharas, 2007) describes the opposite: countries that grew on cheap labour lose that edge as wages rise, without yet building the innovation capacity or high-skill industries (高技術產業) to compete with rich economies — caught between low-wage competitors below and innovation leaders above, growth decelerates sharply.
🧪 Challenger
Institutionalists (Acemoglu, North) blame extractive institutions (榨取性制度) blocking innovation-driven growth; structuralists (Rodrik’s “premature deindustrialization,” Studwell) credit South Korea’s deliberate industrial policy (產業政策) — export discipline, targeted protection — which the Washington Consensus (華盛頓共識) discouraged elsewhere. Open question: whether such industrial policy is replicable without Cold War-era geopolitical leverage.
💡 Cheap labour gets a country to middle income; only institutions or deliberate industrial strategy seem to get it past that point — and economists still don’t agree which matters more.
📖 Gill, I. & Kharas, H. (2007). An East Asian Renaissance. World Bank. World Bank (2024). World Development Report: The Middle-Income Trap. Rodrik, D. (2016). “Premature deindustrialization.” Journal of Economic Growth, 21(1). Studwell, J. (2013). How Asia Works.

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 6 & 10 — When Framing Changes the Outcome: Stereotype threat shows that psychological framing measurably changes individual test performance without changing anyone’s actual ability — the same test, described differently, produces a different score. Robert Shiller’s excess-volatility findings reveal the same structure at market scale: prices swing far more than underlying fundamentals justify, driven by investor psychology and narrative rather than new information. In both domains, the invisible frame surrounding a measurement matters as much as the measurement itself.

So what: Whether you’re designing a test or evaluating a stock pitch, ask what psychological frame is shaping the number in front of you — the measurement is rarely as neutral as it looks, and knowing the frame is often the first step to seeing past it.
