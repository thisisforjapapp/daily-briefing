The Spacecraft That Waited Nine Years to Photograph a Frozen Heart

🤔 Predict Before You Read
On 14 July 2015, after a nine-year, three-billion-mile journey, NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew within 12,500 km of a world so distant that even Hubble had only ever seen it as a fuzzy blur. Before reading on: what do you think a close-up photograph revealed, and why might scientists have been surprised by what they found on the surface of a supposedly frozen, dead little world?
A model of NASA's New Horizons spacecraft, which flew past Pluto on 14 July 2015 after a nine-year journey. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

On 14 July 2015, NASA's New Horizons (新視野號) spacecraft, launched in January 2006, sped past Pluto (冥王星) at over 49,000 km/h after a nine-year journey covering more than 3 billion miles. Before this encounter, even the Hubble Space Telescope (哈勃太空望遠鏡) could only resolve Pluto as a handful of blurry pixels, leaving its surface essentially unmapped and its geology a total mystery. New Horizons carried out its entire close-approach sequence autonomously, since a radio signal took over four hours one-way to reach Earth, meaning any command sent during the flyby would have arrived long after the critical moment had already passed.

What New Horizons found stunned planetary scientists: rather than a dead, crater-battered ice ball, Pluto displayed a massive, bright, heart-shaped plain roughly 1,600 km across, later named Tombaugh Regio (湯博區) after Pluto's 1930 discoverer Clyde Tombaugh (克萊德·湯博). Its smooth western lobe, called Sputnik Planitia (史普尼克平原), showed almost no impact craters at all, indicating the surface is geologically young, resurfaced within perhaps the last million years by slowly convecting nitrogen ice (氮冰), not the four-billion-year-old crust anyone had expected on such a small, cold world so far from the Sun.

The mission also revealed towering water-ice mountains up to 3,500 metres tall, hints of a possible subsurface ocean, and a thin, layered blue-tinted atmosphere backlit spectacularly by the Sun as the spacecraft looked back after passing by. The findings forced planetary scientists to abandon the assumption that small, distant worlds must be geologically dead, reopening debate over Pluto's 2006 demotion from "planet" to "dwarf planet" (矮行星) by the International Astronomical Union, since a world this active never fit anyone's mental picture of an inert leftover chunk of ice.

一件黃袍，如何在一夜之間造出一個新皇朝？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
後周顯德七年（960年），殿前都點檢趙匡胤奉命率軍北上抵禦契丹與北漢聯軍，大軍行至距開封僅四十里的陳橋驛紮營過夜。你認為，這位手握禁軍大權的統帥，在這個看似普通的行軍途中，將如何在一夜之間改寫整個中原的皇位歸屬？
背景速覽

核心人物：趙匡胤（殿前都點檢，後周禁軍統帥）；趙普（趙匡胤心腹謀士）；趙匡義（趙匡胤胞弟）；柴宗訓（後周恭帝，年僅七歲）。

事件：960年，趙匡胤率軍行至陳橋驛，部下將黃袍披於其身，擁立為帝，隨即回師開封，逼後周恭帝禪讓，建立宋朝。

時代背景：五代十國以來，中原政權更迭頻繁，多由禁軍將領憑藉兵權奪位；後周世宗柴榮英年早逝，繼位的恭帝年幼，主少國疑，禁軍將領趙匡胤軍中聲望與實權俱增，朝廷內外人心浮動。

宋太祖趙匡胤畫像，即陳橋兵變的主角及大宋開國之君。來源：維基媒體共享資源

後周顯德七年（960年）正月，邊境急報契丹與北漢聯兵南侵，年幼的恭帝柴宗訓在宰相范質等人主持下，倉促命殿前都點檢趙匡胤統率禁軍北上禦敵。趙匡胤本是後周世宗柴榮一手提拔的心腹戰將，屢立戰功，掌控禁軍多年，軍中將士多感念其恩德，早已流傳「點檢作天子」的讖語。大軍行至距開封僅四十里的陳橋驛，天色已晚，遂就地紮營過夜，趙匡胤之弟趙匡義與謀士趙普當夜密議，暗中鼓動將士擁立主帥稱帝，一場精心策劃的兵變就此悄然醞釀。

翌日拂曉，將士齊集趙匡胤帳外，趙普等人取出早已備妥的黃袍，不由分說披於仍在假寐的趙匡胤身上，眾將跪拜山呼萬歲，趙匡胤故作推辭，稱「汝等自貪富貴，立我為天子，能從我命則可，不然我不能為若主也」，將士齊聲允諾服從號令。趙匡胤隨即約法三章，嚴令入城後不得驚犯太后、少主及朝臣，不得侵掠百姓，違者族誅，大軍旋即回師直撲開封。留守開封的禁軍將領石守信、王審琦等本是趙匡胤舊部，早與兵變將領暗通聲氣，開封城門不戰而開，趙匡胤兵不血刃便進佔皇城。

趙匡胤入城後，宰相范質、王溥等後周舊臣被迫俯首稱臣，年僅七歲的恭帝與符太后被迫下詔禪位，趙匡胤依前朝禪讓舊例登基稱帝，改國號為「宋」，改元建隆，是為宋太祖，五代最後一個政權後周就此覆亡。這場幾乎不流血的兵變，因軍紀嚴明而未致生靈塗炭，卻也開創了往後宋代刻意抑制武將兵權、重文輕武的治國基調——趙匡胤深知自己憑禁軍兵權篡位之易，即位後不久便以「杯酒釋兵權」收回諸將兵權，防止陳橋兵變的一幕在自己身上重演。

327,000 Flights to Feed a City the Soviets Tried to Starve

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In June 1948, the Soviet Union cut off all road, rail, and canal access to West Berlin, a city of over two million people stranded deep inside Soviet-controlled East Germany, hoping to force the Western Allies out entirely. Before reading on: with every ground route blocked, what could the Western powers possibly do to keep an entire city fed and fuelled for months on end?
Context

Key figures: General Lucius D. Clay (盧修斯·克萊), US military governor of occupied Germany; General William H. Tunner (威廉·特納), organiser of the airlift's logistics; Ernst Reuter (恩斯特·羅伊特), West Berlin's mayor.

Event at stake: the Soviet Union's June 1948 blockade of all ground access to West Berlin (西柏林), and the Western Allies' response.

Big picture: the crisis became the first major confrontation of the Cold War (冷戰), testing whether the West would abandon Berlin or find an unprecedented way to supply it by air alone.

A US Air Force C-54 Skymaster landing at Tempelhof Airport during the Berlin Airlift. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In June 1948, the Soviet Union (蘇聯) cut off all road, rail, and canal access into West Berlin (西柏林), an enclave of over two million people located deep inside Soviet-controlled East Germany, after the Western Allies introduced a new currency, the Deutsche Mark (德國馬克), in their occupation zones. Soviet leader Joseph Stalin (約瑟夫·史太林) calculated that starving and freezing the city into submission would force the Western powers to abandon West Berlin outright, or at minimum extract major concessions, since conventional military relief looked impossible without risking a direct war so soon after the Second World War had ended.

Rather than retreat or fight, US military governor Lucius D. Clay (盧修斯·克萊) proposed something never attempted at this scale: supplying an entire city of over two million people entirely by air. General William H. Tunner (威廉·特納), who had run the gruelling "Hump" airlift over the Himalayas during the war, was brought in to organise the operation, landing cargo planes at Berlin's Tempelhof (天普霍夫) and Gatow airports roughly every three minutes at its peak, hauling in coal, flour, and medicine around the clock through some of Europe's worst winter weather.

Over 15 months, Allied aircraft flew more than 278,000 flights, delivering roughly 2.3 million tonnes of supplies, and the operation's sheer persistence, rather than any single dramatic event, is what broke the stalemate: Stalin lifted the blockade in May 1949 once it became clear the airlift could sustain the city indefinitely and was becoming a propaganda embarrassment rather than an effective pressure tactic. The episode transformed West Berlin's relationship with the Western Allies from occupied enemy territory into a defended ally, and it set the template for the Cold War standoffs that followed for the next four decades.

廬山慧遠如何以「形盡神不滅」說服士大夫信奉佛教？

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
東晉時期，佛教初傳中土未久，士大夫多質疑：人一旦死去，形體腐朽，靈魂又豈能獨自留存，繼續承受因果報應？廬山東林寺的高僧慧遠提出「形盡神不滅」之說，試圖以中國固有的哲學語言，回應這個攸關佛教能否在中土立足的根本詰問。你認為，慧遠將如何說服當時深受儒道思想薰陶的士大夫，接受這套來自印度的輪迴觀念？
背景速覽

核心人物：慧遠（334–416年，東晉高僧，廬山東林寺開山祖師）；桓玄（東晉權臣，曾下令沙門敬拜王者）；劉遺民、宗炳等（廬山白蓮社士大夫成員）。

事件／思想：慧遠著《形盡神不滅論》，主張形體雖會朽壞，「神」（精神主體）卻不隨之消滅，藉以支撐佛教的輪迴報應之說。

時代背景：東晉士族清談盛行，佛教初傳中土，儒道思想根深蒂固，「形神關係」是佛教能否在中國思想界立足的核心爭議，慧遠身處廬山，一方面調和佛理與傳統玄學，一方面周旋於朝廷權臣之間，力保佛教僧團的獨立地位。

廬山東林寺開山祖師慧遠畫像，提出「形盡神不滅」之說。來源：維基媒體共享資源

慧遠早年博綜六經，尤善老莊之學，後從高僧道安出家，篤志佛法，東晉太元六年（381年）率弟子南下廬山，創建東林寺，此後三十餘年足不出山，卻透過書信與各地名士、朝廷權貴往來論學，聲望極高。當時佛教初傳中土未久，士大夫普遍質疑：人死之後形體腐朽消散，若無獨立於形體之外的精神主體，因果報應、輪迴轉世之說便無從落實，這成為佛教能否真正在中國思想界站穩腳跟的根本難題，慧遠遂撰文正面回應這一詰問。

慧遠在《形盡神不滅論》中提出，「神」並非依附於形體的某種物質性存在，而是「精極而為靈者也」，是超越形體、不可以常理度量的精神主體；形體如同薪火之喻中的「薪」，會隨著燃燒而窮盡，但火種本身卻可傳遞至另一根薪柴之上，繼續燃燒不滅——「火之傳於薪，猶神之傳於形；火之傳異薪，猶神之傳異形」。慧遠藉此論證，形體雖有生滅，「神」卻能在形體消亡後轉附他形，延續輪迴，為佛教因果報應之說提供了一套中國士大夫較能理解的哲學語言。

慧遠這套學說不僅是抽象思辨，更具有現實政治意義：當權臣桓玄意圖下令沙門須向王者跪拜，慧遠即撰《沙門不敬王者論》，主張出家人已超脫世俗倫常，不應受制於君臣禮法，成功令桓玄收回成命，保住了佛教僧團相對獨立於朝廷的地位。慧遠又於廬山與劉遺民、宗炳等一百二十三位士大夫結成「白蓮社」，共同修習淨土法門，被後世淨土宗尊奉為初祖，「形盡神不滅」之說也成為魏晉南北朝形神之辯的重要一環，深刻影響其後范縝《神滅論》等反對意見的辯難對象。

The One Rule Kant Thought Could Replace Every Other Ethical Rule

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Imagine trying to decide whether it's ever okay to tell a lie, just this once, to get out of an awkward situation. Immanuel Kant argued there's a single test you could apply to any action at all to determine whether it's morally permissible, without reference to consequences, feelings, or religious commandments. Before reading on: what do you think that single test could possibly be?
Context

Key figure: Immanuel Kant (伊曼努爾·康德), German philosopher.

Problem at stake: whether morality can rest on a single rational principle rather than on consequences, feelings, divine command, or social convention.

Moment: Kant developed the categorical imperative (絕對命令) in Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785), directly against the consequentialist tradition represented by utilitarian thinkers like Jeremy Bentham (傑里米·邊沁).

A portrait of Immanuel Kant, whose categorical imperative sought to ground morality in reason alone. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In his 1785 work Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (道德形而上學基礎), Immanuel Kant (伊曼努爾·康德) argued that morality cannot depend on outcomes, feelings, or self-interest, since those all vary from person to person, but must instead rest on reason (理性) alone, applied consistently to everyone. He proposed a single test, the categorical imperative (絕對命令): "act only according to that maxim whereby you can, at the same time, will that it should become a universal law." An action is wrong, on this view, if the reasoning behind it collapses the moment everyone adopted it.

Kant illustrated the test with lying: imagine someone lies to get a loan they can't repay, then asks whether everyone could adopt "lie whenever convenient" as a universal rule. If everyone lied whenever convenient, the entire practice of promise-making would collapse, since no one would ever believe a promise again, so the liar's own scheme would become self-defeating, unable even to exist as a workable practice. This differs sharply from Bentham's (邊沁) utilitarianism (效益主義), which permits a lie whenever it produces more happiness than harm; Kant instead judged actions by the logical structure of the underlying rule, never by results.

Kant offered a second formulation of the same principle, the "humanity formula": always treat other people as ends in themselves, never merely as a means to your own ends, a demand that rules out manipulation and exploitation regardless of the outcome produced. Critics, including later utilitarian and virtue-ethics philosophers, have pointed out the theory's rigidity, since it appears to forbid even a lie told to protect an innocent person from a murderer, a famous objection Kant himself controversially accepted. Even so, the categorical imperative remains one of the most influential attempts to ground morality in pure reason rather than consequence.

🧠 Psychology

Why Hearing a Claim Three Times Makes It Feel True

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Imagine you hear the same claim repeated several times over a few weeks, from different sources, even if none of them ever actually proves it's true. Before reading on: do you think simple repetition alone, with zero new evidence added, could make a false statement start to feel more believable to you?
A newspaper front page — repeated exposure to a claim, true or false, is enough to make it feel more believable. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The illusory truth effect (錯覺真相效應) describes how simply repeating a statement, even one that's false, makes people rate it as more believable, entirely independent of whether any actual evidence supports it. Psychologists Lynn Hasher, David Goldstein, and Thomas Toppino first documented the effect in a 1977 study, where participants rated plausible-sounding trivia statements as truer on later exposures simply because they had seen them before, regardless of the statements' actual accuracy. The effect is robust enough that later research found it works even on statements that directly contradict a person's own existing general knowledge, not just ambiguous or unfamiliar claims.

The likely mechanism is processing fluency (加工流暢性): a statement encountered before is easier and faster for the brain to process the second or third time, and people unconsciously misread that ease of processing as a signal of truth, rather than correctly attributing it to mere familiarity. A 2015 study by Fazio, Brashier, Payne, and Marsh found the effect persists even when participants explicitly knew the correct answer beforehand, showing that repetition can override existing knowledge rather than simply filling a gap in it, a far more troubling implication for how misinformation spreads.

The illusory truth effect has direct real-world stakes for advertising, political messaging, and the spread of misinformation (失實資訊) on social media, where repeated exposure to a claim, even one repeatedly flagged as false, can still nudge belief upward over time through pure repetition alone. Fact-checking research shows that merely repeating a false claim in order to debunk it can sometimes reinforce the false belief instead, which is why current guidance favours leading with the correct fact itself rather than restating the myth and hoping the denial is what people remember.

The Pointless Reflex That Even Fetuses Perform in the Womb

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Hiccups strike everyone occasionally, an involuntary spasm followed by an odd closing "hic" sound, usually for no obvious reason at all. Before reading on: what part of the body do you think actually triggers a hiccup, and why might unborn babies hiccup inside the womb months before they ever take a breath of air?
A diagram of the human respiratory system, including the diaphragm, whose sudden spasm triggers a hiccup. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

A hiccup (呃逆, medically singultus) begins with a sudden, involuntary spasm of the diaphragm (橫膈膜), the dome-shaped muscle beneath the lungs responsible for normal breathing, immediately followed by the epiglottis (會厭) snapping shut over the windpipe, producing the characteristic "hic" sound as air is abruptly stopped mid-inhale. The reflex runs through a circuit involving the phrenic nerve (膈神經) and vagus nerve (迷走神經), both of which connect the brainstem to the diaphragm, and irritation anywhere along that pathway, from eating too fast to a stomach stretched with carbonated drinks, can set the whole reflex arc off unpredictably.

Hiccups are unusually well documented before birth: ultrasound studies have observed fetuses hiccuping regularly from as early as nine weeks of gestation, long before the lungs are used for actual breathing, which has led some researchers to propose the reflex may help train the diaphragm and airway-closing muscles needed for breathing and swallowing after birth. This developmental theory remains debated, since adult hiccups serve no clear ongoing purpose and, unlike coughing or sneezing, don't protect the airway from anything, making persistent hiccups one of the few common bodily reflexes that looks like an evolutionary leftover rather than an active defence.

Most hiccup bouts resolve on their own within minutes, and the huge range of home remedies, from holding your breath to drinking water upside-down, work inconsistently because they attempt to interrupt the nerve circuit through raised carbon dioxide levels or vagus nerve stimulation, rather than addressing any single reliable cause. Chronic hiccups lasting more than 48 hours are rare but can signal an underlying issue such as acid reflux or nerve irritation, and in the well-documented case of Charles Osborne, hiccups reportedly persisted for 68 years, from 1922 to 1990, without ever being conclusively explained by doctors.

The "Syndrome" Named After a Meal That Was Never Actually Proven

🤔 Predict Before You Read
For decades, many people have avoided monosodium glutamate (MSG), a common flavour-enhancing seasoning, believing it causes headaches, flushing, and chest tightness after eating certain meals, a cluster of symptoms once popularly called "Chinese restaurant syndrome." Before reading on: do you think rigorous double-blind testing has actually confirmed MSG causes these symptoms, or might something else be behind the reputation?
Crystals of monosodium glutamate (MSG), the seasoning wrongly blamed for "Chinese restaurant syndrome." Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The idea that monosodium glutamate (麩胺酸鈉, MSG), a seasoning made from glutamate, an amino acid naturally present in tomatoes, cheese, and seaweed, causes headaches and flushing traces to a single 1968 letter published in the New England Journal of Medicine by physician Robert Ho Man Kwok, describing symptoms he experienced after eating at Chinese-American restaurants. The letter was speculative and anecdotal, not a controlled study, yet it was picked up by media outlets and popularised the term "Chinese restaurant syndrome" (中餐館症候群), a label now widely criticised as both scientifically unfounded and ethnically stigmatising toward Chinese cuisine specifically.

Subsequent double-blind, placebo-controlled studies, the gold standard for testing such claims, have generally failed to reliably reproduce the reported symptoms when MSG is given to well-informed participants unaware of which sample contains it. A 2006 review in the Journal of Nutrition found that when large MSG doses were given without food, some sensitive individuals reported mild, short-lived symptoms, but the effect vanished almost entirely once MSG was consumed with a normal meal, exactly how it is actually eaten in everyday cooking, undermining the idea of a robust, food-based "MSG reaction."

The US Food and Drug Administration (美國食品藥物管理局) and the European Food Safety Authority both classify MSG as generally safe for the general population at typical dietary levels, and it naturally occurs in far larger amounts in aged parmesan cheese and ripe tomatoes than in a typical seasoned dish. Researchers increasingly attribute the persistence of "Chinese restaurant syndrome" partly to confirmation bias and partly to a documented, race-tinged double standard, since nearly identical glutamate levels in Italian or Japanese dishes rarely attracted the same public suspicion or scrutiny.

🏋 Sports Science

The Supplement Aisle's Favourite Powder Might Not Be Doing Much

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs), sold in brightly coloured powders throughout gym supplement aisles, are marketed as essential for muscle growth and recovery. Before reading on: if you're already eating enough protein from regular food each day, do you think adding a separate BCAA supplement on top would meaningfully add anything?
The chemical structure of leucine, one of the three branched-chain amino acids marketed in BCAA supplements. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Branched-chain amino acids (支鏈氨基酸, BCAAs) are three essential amino acids, leucine (亮氨酸), isoleucine (異亮氨酸), and valine (纈氨酸), that the body cannot produce itself and must obtain from diet, marketed heavily as standalone powders that supposedly boost muscle protein synthesis (肌肉蛋白質合成) and speed recovery. The marketing logic is superficially sound, since leucine specifically does trigger the biochemical pathway that initiates muscle building, but BCAAs make up only three of the roughly twenty amino acids the body actually needs to fully complete that muscle-building process from start to finish.

This is where the supplement's core limitation lies: a 2017 meta-analysis in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, along with several subsequent reviews, found that BCAAs alone, without the other essential amino acids, fail to meaningfully increase muscle protein synthesis or improve strength and hypertrophy outcomes compared with a complete protein source like whey (乳清蛋白) or a normal high-protein meal, which already supplies leucine alongside every other amino acid the muscle-building process actually requires.

For someone already consuming adequate total daily protein, generally cited around 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight for active individuals, a standalone BCAA supplement adds essentially nothing beyond what a complete protein source already provides, making it a largely redundant purchase for most gym-goers. BCAAs may still offer modest value in narrow situations, such as training in a fasted state or during extended endurance events where a small amount of amino acids is more convenient than a full meal, but as a daily supplement for muscle growth, whole protein sources remain the better-supported choice.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

The Flower Bulb That Once Cost More Than a House

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In the winter of 1636–37, in the Dutch Republic, contracts for a single tulip bulb reportedly traded for prices exceeding what a skilled craftsman earned in years. Before reading on: what do you think eventually happened when buyers realised a flower bulb, however rare and beautiful, could never actually be worth that much?
Jan Brueghel the Younger's "Satire on Tulip Mania" (c. 1640), depicting monkeys engaged in the speculative bulb trade. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Tulip mania (鬱金香狂熱) unfolded in the Dutch Republic (荷蘭共和國) during the 1630s, after tulips, imported from the Ottoman Empire in the late 16th century, became a prestigious status symbol among the newly wealthy Dutch merchant class. Rare, virus-striped varieties, most famously the crimson-and-white Semper Augustus (永恆奧古斯都), commanded especially extreme prices, and by the winter of 1636–37, contracts for individual bulbs were reportedly changing hands for sums exceeding ten times a skilled craftsman's annual wage, traded increasingly through futures contracts on bulbs still buried in the ground rather than bulbs anyone could actually hold.

The market collapsed abruptly in February 1637, when a routine bulb auction in Haarlem (哈勒姆) reportedly failed to attract its usual buyers, and word of that failed sale spread quickly enough to trigger a rapid, self-reinforcing wave of contract defaults and panic selling across the entire speculative bulb-futures market. Prices for common bulb varieties are estimated to have fallen by well over 90% within weeks, leaving traders holding contracts obligating them to buy bulbs at prices now wildly disconnected from what anyone would actually pay, a mismatch the Dutch courts were eventually asked to help resolve.

Modern economic historians, notably Anne Goldgar, have significantly revised the popular legend: tulip mania's economic damage was likely far more contained than later retellings suggested, largely confined to a relatively narrow group of specialist bulb traders rather than the broader Dutch economy, which continued largely uninterrupted afterward. The episode nonetheless endures as history's most frequently cited early example of a speculative bubble, a recurring pattern later economists would recognise again in the South Sea Bubble, the dot-com crash, and other episodes where price detaches entirely from any plausible underlying value.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

The Rent That Never Runs Out: A Sugar Quota Nine Decades Old

A 19th-century botanical illustration of sugarcane, the crop at the centre of decades of US import quotas. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
US sugar import quotas, dating to 1934 and renewed through each modern Farm Bill (農業法案), keep domestic sugar prices roughly double the world price, costing American consumers and food manufacturers billions of dollars annually while benefiting a few dozen politically organised producers.
📖 Theory
Mancur Olson's (曼瑟爾·奧爾森) The Logic of Collective Action (1965) and Gordon Tullock's (戈登·塔洛克) rent-seeking (尋租) theory predict small, concentrated interest groups will reliably out-organise a far larger but diffuse public, since each individual consumer loses too little per person to justify lobbying against the arrangement.
⚠️ Anomaly
This logic predicts protection should be nearly permanent everywhere, yet many countries, including New Zealand's sweeping 1984 tariff liberalisation, dismantled deeply entrenched protectionist programs anyway; even within the US, most farm subsidies shrank considerably after the 1990s while sugar quotas alone have persisted almost untouched for nine decades.
🧪 Challenger
Gene Grossman (基恩·格羅斯曼) and Elhanan Helpman's (埃爾赫南·赫爾普曼) "Protection for Sale" model argues rent-seeking rarely disappears in liberalising countries, it shifts from tariffs into subtler quotas and standards instead; why some concentrated interests like sugar survive decades of reform pressure while others don't remains a genuinely open question in political economy.
💡 The sugar quota shows rent-seeking doesn't vanish under reform pressure — it just learns to wear a subtler disguise.

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 5 & 11 — The Universalisation Test Applied to Rent-Seeking: Kant's categorical imperative asks whether a self-serving action could survive everyone doing it; the decades-long sugar quota effectively fails that exact test, since an economy where every single industry successfully won the same protection would collapse into total paralysis, yet it persists precisely because most other groups either can't organise as tightly as sugar producers have, or simply haven't tried as hard.

So what: the next time a policy or personal shortcut looks obviously self-serving but keeps surviving scrutiny, ask Kant's question: would it still work if literally everyone else did the exact same thing? If not, its survival probably owes more to weak opposition than to genuine fairness.
