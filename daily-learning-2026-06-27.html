🔭 Astronomy & Cosmos

The Storm That Refuses to Die: Inside Jupiter’s Great Red Spot (木星大紅斑), the Solar System’s Oldest Known Hurricane

🤔 Predict Before You Read
A storm roughly 1.3 times the diameter of Earth has been raging on Jupiter (木星) for at least 350 years. What does that tell you about Jupiter’s atmosphere compared to Earth’s — and why might a storm that large be self-sustaining rather than dissipating?
Jupiter’s Great Red Spot (木星大紅斑) captured by NASA’s Juno spacecraft during its seventh perijove flyby — NASA/JPL-Caltech/SwRI/MSSS, Wikimedia Commons

On 15 July 1878, two American astronomers sketching Jupiter noticed an especially vivid crimson oval in the southern hemisphere — dramatic enough to spark a burst of observations that continue to this day. But the storm itself is almost certainly far older. Italian astronomer Giovanni Cassini (卡西尼) recorded a persistent oval feature in Jupiter’s atmosphere as early as 1665, and while there is some scholarly debate about whether it is truly the same storm, the current Great Red Spot (大紅斑) has been tracked continuously since at least 1830. At its historical maximum it spanned roughly 40,000 kilometres, about three Earth diameters (地球直徑); today it has shrunk to approximately 16,000 kilometres — still larger than Earth (地球) — and its rate of contraction has been one of the most puzzling mysteries in planetary science (行星科學).

The Great Red Spot is an anticyclone (反氣旋) — a high-pressure vortex (高壓渦旋) rotating counterclockwise (反時針) in the southern hemisphere, in the opposite sense to Earth’s high-pressure systems because Jupiter’s circulation obeys the same Coriolis force (科里奧利力) but in a gas giant (氣態巨行星) with no solid surface to drain its energy. On Earth, hurricanes (颶風) dissipate over land because friction robs them of energy. Jupiter has no land: it is gas all the way down. The storm is also driven by internal heat (內部熱能) — Jupiter radiates roughly 1.7 times the energy it receives from the Sun, because it is still slowly contracting from its formation 4.5 billion years ago. That continuous energy source helps maintain atmospheric motion indefinitely. Wind speeds at the storm’s edges exceed 600 kilometres per hour, while the eye-like centre is eerily calm.

NASA’s Juno spacecraft (朱諾號探測器), which has been orbiting Jupiter since 2016, transformed understanding of the spot from above and below. Microwave radiometry (微波輻射測量) showed the storm extends perhaps 500 kilometres deep into the atmosphere — far deeper than meteorologists expected and deep enough to intersect zones of chemical change. Juno’s close flybys in 2019 revealed that smaller storms merge into the Great Red Spot and are consumed, possibly feeding the giant vortex. The reddish colour itself remains debated: the leading hypothesis is that ultraviolet light from the Sun (太陽紫外線) breaks ammonia (氨) and acetylene (乙炔) compounds into complex organic (有機物) chromophores (發色團) that produce the distinctive rust hue. Whatever its chemistry, the Great Red Spot is a reminder that planetary atmospheres can sustain structures at scales, speeds, and durations utterly unlike anything on Earth.

💡 Jupiter’s Great Red Spot persists because a gas giant has no surface friction to drain a storm’s energy — a planet without land is a planet where weather can last centuries.
Answer this without looking back: Why does the Great Red Spot persist for centuries when large Earth hurricanes die within days, and what did the Juno spacecraft reveal about the storm’s vertical structure?
Check your answer
Earth hurricanes dissipate over land because surface friction removes their energy; they also lose heat over cooler water. Jupiter has no solid surface — the gas extends for thousands of kilometres — so friction is absent. Jupiter also radiates more heat than it receives from the Sun (internal gravitational contraction), continuously driving atmospheric circulation. The Great Red Spot is an anticyclone (反氣旋) rotating counterclockwise in the southern hemisphere, sustained by this internal energy and the absence of surface drag. Juno’s microwave radiometry showed the storm extends roughly 500 km deep into the atmosphere — far deeper than expected — and that smaller vortices are regularly absorbed into it, potentially feeding its persistence.

天下易主：1644年多爾袞如何以二十萬鐵騎奪取中原，終結大明二百七十六年的江山

🤔 讀前預測
崇禎皇帝在煤山自縊時，明朝已亡。清軍此前從未統治過漢人，卻能在數年內席捲中原，幾乎未遭頑強抵抗。你認為這是什麼原因？
背景

人物：多爾袞（1612–1650年），清攝政王，皇太極之弟，實際決策者；順治帝（福臨，1638–1661年），清世祖，入關後第一位皇帝；李自成（1606–1645年），農民起義軍首領，建立大順政權；崇禎帝（朱由檢，1611–1644年），明朝末代皇帝；吳三桂（1612–1678年），明朝遼東總兵，關鍵人物。

事件：1644年，農民軍首領李自成攻克北京，崇禎皇帝於煤山自縊，大明滅亡。多爾袞率清軍借道山海關，聯合吳三桂擊潰李自成，同年九月順治帝定都北京，清朝正式取得中原政權。

大局：明朝末年，天災、農民起義（李自成、張獻忠）、遼東女真威脅三重夾擊。崇禎帝大殺將帥，朝廷嚴重內耗。清太宗皇太極數度南下，但始終未能突破山海關防線。1644年的關鍵在於：吳三桂的抉擇改變了一切。

多爾袞（睿親王，1612–1650年）畫像，清朝入關的實際主導者 — Wikimedia Commons

1644年三月，李自成率農民軍攻陷北京，崇禎帝在煤山（今景山）自縊，大明二百七十六年的統治就此終結。李自成在紫禁城（Forbidden City）僅盤踞四十二天，便因軍紀敗壞、搶掠無度，大失民心。與此同時，遼東總兵吳三桂正率軍勤王途中，抵達山海關。李自成索餉、侮及吳三桂家眷，吳三桂一怒之下，決意向清攝政王多爾袞借兵。這一刻，影響了此後三百年的中國歷史。多爾袞抓住機會，立即親率清軍八旗（Eight Banners），與吳三桂聯合出擊。

1644年五月，清軍與吳三桂聯軍在山海關外以一片石大敗李自成。李自成僅逃回北京一日，便焚宮西逃，所建大順政權土崩瓦解。多爾袞率順治帝入關，九月定都北京，向全天下宣示清朝繼承明朝正統。多爾袞的政治手腕極為精明：他沿用明朝官制，保留漢族官員，為崇禎帝發喪，以「為明復仇」的名義招攬人心。但與此同時，他頒布剃髮令（Tonsure Decree），強制漢人剃髮改滿洲髮式，以「留頭不留髮，留髮不留頭」的鐵腕鎮壓抵抗，引發江南大規模反清鬥爭（揚州十日、嘉定三屠）。

清朝入關之所以如此迅速成功，原因是多方面的。其一，多爾袞麾下的八旗軍事力量此時正值巔峰；其二，明朝官僚體系雖龐大卻已腐朽，地方精英普遍選擇效忠新朝以自保；其三，清廷刻意保留科舉制度（Imperial Examination）和儒家禮制，大幅降低了政權更迭的文化衝擊。抗清力量南明政權（Southern Ming）雖先後建立了弘光、隆武、永曆等政權，但各系互相傾軋，始終無力北伐，最終逐一被清軍消滅。康熙帝平定三藩之亂（1683年）後，清朝對全中國的統治方告穩固，延續至1912年。

The Road to Moscow and Back: How Napoleon’s (拿破崙) 1812 Russian Campaign Destroyed the Greatest Army Europe Had Ever Seen

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Napoleon (拿破崙) commanded the largest army ever assembled in European history to that point — over 600,000 men. He had never lost a major campaign. What would you predict were the factors that brought his Russian invasion to catastrophic failure?
Context

Key figures: Napoleon Bonaparte (拿破崙·波拿巴), Emperor of France; Tsar Alexander I (沙皇亞歷山大一世); Mikhail Kutuzov (庫圖佐夫), Russian commander-in-chief; Joachim Murat (繆拉), commander of French cavalry; Marshal Michel Ney (奈伊元帥), who commanded the rear guard in the catastrophic retreat.

Event at stake: In June 1812 Napoleon invaded Russia with roughly 685,000 troops, the Grande Armée (大軍團), aiming to force Tsar Alexander back into the Continental System (大陸封鎖體系) and crush Russian power. By December, fewer than 100,000 men crossed back west alive.

Big picture: Napoleon had redrawn the map of Europe repeatedly since 1805. Russia was the last major power outside his orbit. But the relationship had frayed since Tilsit (提爾西特), and Russia’s withdrawal from the Continental System — the economic blockade of Britain (英國) designed to strangle it — gave Napoleon his justification and, he calculated, his necessity to act.

The French IV Corps during the 1812 Russian Campaign — Wikimedia Commons

Napoleon crossed the Niemen River (涅曼河) on 24 June 1812 with approximately 685,000 men drawn from across his empire — French, Polish, German, Italian, Austrian, Prussian — a truly multinational force. The problem was immediate: the Russian armies refused to stand and fight. General Barclay de Tolly (巴克萊·德·托利) fell back systematically, trading space for time and exhausting the French through sheer distance. The strategy was not pre-planned genius; it was improvisation born of weakness. But its effect was devastating. French supply lines stretched impossibly thin across the scorched Russian steppe (草原), and summer heat, dysentery (痢疾), and horse losses from lack of forage killed tens of thousands before a single major battle was fought. By the time the Grande Armée reached Smolensk (斯摩稜斯克) in mid-August, it had already lost perhaps 100,000 men to disease and desertion.

The Battle of Borodino (博羅季諾戰役) on 7 September 1812 was the bloodiest single day of the Napoleonic Wars (拿破崙戰爭), killing or wounding roughly 70,000 men on both sides. Kutuzov (庫圖佐夫) — the 67-year-old, one-eyed field marshal appointed under popular pressure — fought a brutal attritional battle and then deliberately abandoned Moscow (莫斯科). When Napoleon rode into the ancient capital on 14 September expecting a surrender delegation, he found a ghost city. The Russians had evacuated; Muscovites, ordered by Governor Rostopchin (羅斯托普欽), systematically burned the city’s food stores and much of the city itself. Napoleon waited in the Kremlin (克里姆林宮) for five weeks for Tsar Alexander to negotiate. No message came. The Grande Armée sat in a burned-out city with winter approaching, its supply situation already critical.

On 19 October Napoleon began the retreat. Winter arrived earlier and harsher than usual. The thermometer fell to −37°C. The route west was the same devastated corridor the army had crossed in summer — no food, no fodder. Men died of frostbite (凍傷), starvation, and Cossack raids by the thousands daily. The crossing of the Berezina River (別列津納河) in late November — engineers working in near-freezing water to build temporary bridges under artillery fire — saved perhaps 40,000 men but cost 20,000 lives in the chaos. Marshal Ney (奈伊), commanding the shattered rear guard, became legendary for personally picking up a musket and fighting alongside his men. Of the 685,000 who crossed into Russia, perhaps 70,000–100,000 returned fit for duty. The campaign fatally weakened Napoleon’s European dominance; within two years he would abdicate (遜位).

💡 Napoleon’s Russian disaster shows that military genius is not transferable across all terrains: vast distances, hostile climate, and an enemy who refuses your preferred battle can render tactical brilliance irrelevant.
Answer this without looking back: Why did the Russian strategy of retreating rather than fighting destroy the Grande Armée even before the winter set in, and what was the critical miscalculation Napoleon made about what taking Moscow would achieve?
Check your answer
The Russian retreat meant the French had to march hundreds of kilometres through scorched, depopulated countryside with supply lines stretching impossibly far from France. Napoleon’s logistical system depended on living off the land (forage and local supply), which worked in densely farmed Western Europe but failed in the vast, deliberately emptied Russian steppe (草原). The army lost roughly 100,000 men before Borodino purely to disease, heat, and starvation. Napoleon’s miscalculation: he believed capturing Moscow would force Tsar Alexander to the negotiating table, because in Western European wars a capital’s fall typically ended wars. But Russia’s political centre of gravity was not Moscow — the Tsar governed from St. Petersburg (聖彼得堡) — and Russian political culture in 1812 did not treat a capital’s loss as equivalent to defeat.

☯ 東方哲學

西天取法：玄奘的萬里求道如何將「萬法唯識」帶回東土，改寫了中國佛教的哲學地圖

🤔 讀前預測
「萬法唯識」（Yogācāra/唯識學）主張：所有你所感知到的「外在世界」，其實只是意識的呈現，並無獨立於心識之外的客觀實體。如果這個命題為真，它對「學習」、「記憶」與「認知」的本質有何根本衝擊？
背景

人物：玄奘（602–664年），唐代高僧，法相宗創始人；無著（Asaṅga，約310–390年）、世親（Vasubandhu，約350–430年），唯識學的印度創立者；唐太宗李世民（598–649年），支持玄奘譯經的帝王。

事件：唐貞觀元年（627年），玄奘不顧禁令，獨自西行，歷時十七年，行程五萬里，抵達那爛陀寺（Nālandā）求法，攜回梵文佛典657部，翻譯成漢文75部1335卷，創立法相宗，將瑜伽行派唯識學正式引入中國。

大局：隋唐時期，中國佛教已高度中國化。天台、三論、涅槃諸宗各有體系，但所依據的梵文原典或有缺漏、或有錯譯。玄奘的使命，是以第一手的原典譯本重建佛學的義理基礎，並輸入唯識這一印度哲學最精密的心識分析體系。

玄奘法師（602–664年）畫像——唐代最偉大的求法僧，《大唐西域記》作者 — Wikimedia Commons

唐太宗貞觀元年（627年），玄奘年約二十五歲，以一人之力，悄然出發，穿越西域大漠，歷經生死危難，抵達佛教學問中心那爛陀寺。他在那裡求學十五年，拜戒賢法師（Śīlabhadra）門下，精研瑜伽行派（Yogācāra）唯識學的核心典籍，包括《瑜伽師地論》（Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra）與《成唯識論》（Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi）。玄奘歸唐後，在唐太宗全力支持下，設立譯場，以空前嚴謹的標準重譯大批梵典。他的譯文被稱為「新譯」，與此前鳩摩羅什的「舊譯」並列，成為中國佛典翻譯史上兩座高峰。

玄奘帶回的唯識學（Vijñānavāda / Yogācāra）是一套精密的心識哲學。其核心命題是「萬法唯識」——宇宙萬象皆是意識的顯現，沒有獨立於心識之外的客觀實體。唯識學建立了一個八識體系：眼、耳、鼻、舌、身五識，加上統合感官的第六意識、執取「自我」的第七末那識（Manas），以及儲存所有業力種子的第八阿賴耶識（Ālayavijñāna）。阿賴耶識是唯識學最精緻的發明——它是心識的底層倉庫，儲存過去所有行為（業，karma）留下的潛在種子，這些種子成熟後，「熏習」（impression）現行識，生起我們所感知的一切。換言之，你所體驗的「現實」，是無數業種子不斷熏習呈現的結果，並非外在世界直接映射的影像。

玄奘創立的法相宗（Faxiang School）在中國影響深遠，但也引發了哲學爭議。法相宗強調精密的義理分析，與天台宗的圓融觀、禪宗的頓悟路線形成張力。禪師往往批評法相宗過於執著名相分析，忘失了直指人心的宗旨。然而唯識學的影響超越了法相宗本身：阿賴耶識概念為後來淨土宗、密宗乃至現代佛教心理學提供了意識深層的理論框架。玄奘的《大唐西域記》（Great Tang Records on the Western Regions）更成為研究七世紀中亞、南亞歷史地理的第一手史料，考古學家至今仍憑此書定位那爛陀寺遺址與古代絲路諸地。

💡 唯識學最深刻的洞見：你以為在感知「外在世界」，其實是在讀取意識底層的業種子庫——認識論的革命，比任何外在征服都更根本。
不看回去，試試回答：唯識學的「阿賴耶識」是什麼？它在整個八識體系中扮演什麼角色？為何它被稱為意識的「倉庫」？
查看答案
阿賴耶識（Ālayavijñāna）是唯識學八識體系中最深層的一識，字面意義是「藏識」或「倉庫識」。它儲存了過去所有行為（業，karma）留下的潛在種子（Bīja）。這些種子並不立即顯現，而是在因緣成熟時「熏習」現行識，生起我們所感知的世界萬象——包括自我感、感官經驗，乃至夢境。前七識（五根識、意識、末那識）的活動又反過來在阿賴耶識中種下新的種子，形成無間斷的循環。因此，唯識學主張沒有獨立於心識之外的客觀世界：所謂「外在」，不過是阿賴耶識種子熏習的顯現。

What Works Is What’s True: William James (威廉·詹姆士) and the Radical Claim That Ideas Are Tools, Not Mirrors of Reality

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Most philosophical traditions try to define “truth” as something ideas have when they correctly represent reality — a correspondence between mind and world. What would it mean instead to say an idea is “true” only if it is useful, if it makes a practical difference to how you live?
Context

Key figures: William James (威廉·詹姆士, 1842–1910), psychologist and philosopher at Harvard (哈佛大學); Charles Sanders Peirce (皮爾士, 1839–1914), who coined pragmatism (實用主義) in 1878; John Dewey (杜威, 1859–1952), who extended it into education and democracy; F.C.S. Schiller (席勒) in Britain.

Rival view being challenged: The dominant European tradition treated truth as a property ideas have when they correspond to a fixed, pre-existing reality — the “spectator theory of knowledge,” in James’s dismissive phrase. Both rationalists (理性主義者) like Descartes (笛卡爾) and empiricists (經驗主義者) like Locke (洛克) assumed the mind’s job was to accurately mirror the world.

Historical moment: Late 19th-century America was defined by explosive industrialisation, Darwinian evolution (達爾文進化論), and a pragmatic culture that valued what works over what is elegant. James saw philosophy as similarly in need of a biological, consequentialist reorientation.

William James (威廉·詹姆士, 1842–1910), psychologist, philosopher, and founder of American pragmatism — Wikimedia Commons

Charles Sanders Peirce (皮爾士) first articulated pragmatism in 1878 as a method for clarifying ideas: the meaning of any concept is the sum of its conceivable practical effects. Hard and diamond — what would it mean to call one “harder” than the other unless it produced a different outcome when tested? Peirce intended this as a tool for cutting through metaphysical fog. James, his friend and Harvard (哈佛大學) colleague, seized the method and pushed it far further. In his 1907 lectures collected as Pragmatism (《實用主義》), James argued that truth itself is not a static correspondence but a process: ideas become true “insofar as they help us get into satisfactory relations with other parts of our experience.” Truth “happens to an idea,” he wrote — it is not a property an idea has, but an achievement it earns by proving useful in guiding action.

James applied this relentlessly. His “Will to Believe” (1896) argued that in cases of genuine uncertainty where the decision is forced (you cannot stay neutral), it is rationally permissible — even required — to adopt beliefs on the basis of practical and emotional need rather than evidence alone. This is most famous in his defence of religious belief (宗教信仰): if believing in God gives life meaning and motivates moral action, and if there is no decisive counter-evidence, then the belief is pragmatically justified. James was also the founding figure of American psychology (心理學): his 1890 Principles of Psychology (《心理學原理》) introduced the concept of the “stream of consciousness” (意識流) — the continuous, ever-changing flow of thought that cannot be broken into discrete atoms, influencing novelists from Virginia Woolf (吳爾芙) to James Joyce (喬伊斯).

Critics attacked pragmatism from both sides. Bertrand Russell (羅素) and G.E. Moore (摩爾) argued that James conflated truth with usefulness: a belief might be useful yet false, and false yet useful. James’s reply was that “useful” had to be understood globally — useful in the long run, for everyone, coherently integrating all experience — not merely locally convenient. John Dewey (杜威) extended pragmatism into education (教育哲學), arguing that children learn by doing, not by passively absorbing fixed truths: knowledge is always knowledge-in-use. The tradition’s influence is enormous and often unacknowledged: the entire “evidence-based medicine” (循證醫學) culture, with its insistence on testing whether interventions “work,” is deeply pragmatist in spirit. So is modern cognitive-behavioural therapy (認知行為療法, CBT): beliefs are not evaluated as abstractions but by their practical consequences for functioning.

Peirce (皮爾士) intended pragmatism as a method for clarifying the meaning of concepts by specifying their practical consequences — a logical and semantic tool, not a theory of truth. James extended it into a full theory of truth: ideas are true insofar as they prove useful in guiding action and integrating experience, and truth is not a static property but a dynamic achievement. Russell’s (羅素) objection was that James conflated truth with usefulness: a belief could be useful yet false (e.g., a placebo that works), or true yet useless. Truth, Russell argued, is a correspondence relation between proposition and fact, entirely independent of whether believing it benefits anyone. James’s reply was that “useful” must be understood as coherently useful in the long run across all experience — not merely locally convenient — but critics felt this simply redefined “useful” to mean something too close to “true” to do real philosophical work.

You Don’t Remember What Happened — You Remember How It Ended: Kahneman’s (卡尼曼) Peak-End Rule (峰終法則) and the Memory Illusion

🤔 Predict Before You Read
If you went through a painful medical procedure, do you think you would accurately remember how painful it was overall — integrating the pain across every moment? Or would your memory distort toward certain moments? If so, which moments would loom largest?
Daniel Kahneman (卡尼曼, 1934–2024), psychologist and Nobel laureate (諾貝爾獎得主) in Economics, whose colonoscopy studies defined the peak-end rule — Wikimedia Commons

In the early 1990s, Daniel Kahneman (卡尼曼) and his colleagues conducted a now-classic colonoscopy (結腸鏡檢查) study. Patients rated their pain every 60 seconds throughout the procedure. Afterwards, they also rated the overall experience retrospectively. The findings were striking and counterintuitive (反直覺的): the remembered pain did not correlate with the total integrated pain — the sum of all those 60-second ratings. It correlated almost entirely with just two variables: the single worst moment (the peak, 峰值) and how painful the final moments were (the end, 終點). Duration (持續時間) was almost irrelevant. A longer procedure that ended gently was remembered as less painful than a shorter one that ended badly, even if it had caused more total suffering. Kahneman called this the peak-end rule (峰終法則), and a parallel cold pressor experiment (冷壓測試) — holding your hand in freezing water — confirmed the same pattern with laboratory precision.

The peak-end rule is an instance of a broader phenomenon Kahneman called the distinction between the “experiencing self” (體驗自我) and the “remembering self” (記憶自我). The experiencing self lives moment to moment, accumulating pleasure and pain in real time. The remembering self constructs a narrative after the fact, and that narrative is what we actually use to make future decisions. Because the remembering self ignores duration — what Kahneman called “duration neglect” (持續時間忽視) — there is a systematic gap between what we actually feel and what we choose to do again. The colonoscopy study had a striking practical implication: Donald Redelmeier (雷德爾邁爾) showed that deliberately adding a less painful minute at the end of the procedure — leaving the scope still but uncomfortable rather than actively probing — made patients’ retrospective ratings significantly more positive, and they were more likely to comply with follow-up procedures. The intervention added nothing enjoyable; it merely improved the ending.

The peak-end rule has been replicated across experiences ranging from vacations (假期) to listening to music to financial loss scenarios, though more recent studies have found the original effect is moderate and varies with the type of experience and how ratings are collected. Its implications are vast: user experience designers (用戶體驗設計師) know that the last interaction with a product disproportionately shapes overall satisfaction; customer service scripts focus on resolving complaints pleasantly even after long waits; palliative care (安寧療護) researchers consider whether the quality of a patient’s final days shapes how loved ones remember an illness. Kahneman himself drew a darker conclusion: since we make decisions based on memories rather than real-time experience, and memories are systematically distorted, we are routinely choosing things that will not make us as happy as we expect — because we are optimising for how things are remembered, not how they feel.

Check your answer
Duration neglect (持續時間忽視) is the finding that the total length of a painful or pleasant experience has almost no effect on how it is retrospectively evaluated — the memory is dominated by the peak intensity and the intensity at the end. Redelmeier’s intervention: after the clinically necessary colonoscopy procedure ended, the scope was left in place for roughly 60–90 additional seconds in a partially withdrawn, less painful position rather than being removed immediately. This “worse ending but gentler final moment” improved patients’ retrospective ratings of the whole procedure, because the last experienced moment was less painful than the actual clinical peak. Total pain was higher (the procedure was longer) but remembered pain was lower — demonstrating that remembered experience and lived experience are genuinely distinct, and that the remembering self’s decisions are made from a systematically biased record.

(1996). “Patients’ Memories of Painful Medical Treatments.” Pain, 116–126; Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, Fast and Slow. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.

The Hormonal Gap: How Testosterone (睾酮), the Androgen Receptor (雄激素受體), and SHBG Explain Why Two People Training Identically Can Build Muscle at Vastly Different Rates

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Two men train together for a year with identical programmes and nutrition. One gains dramatically more muscle than the other. Both follow the same protocol. Beyond genetics for muscle fibre composition, what hormonal and molecular variables might explain the gap?
Molecular structure of testosterone (睾酮, T) — Wikimedia Commons

Testosterone (睾酮, T) is a C19 steroid hormone (類固醇激素) synthesised primarily in the Leydig cells (萊迪希細胞) of the testes (睪丸) in males, and in smaller amounts by the adrenal glands (腎上腺) and ovaries (卵巢) in both sexes. Its production is regulated by the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis (下丘腦-垂體-性腺軸): the hypothalamus (下丘腦) releases gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH, 促性腺激素釋放激素), which triggers the pituitary (垂體) to secrete luteinising hormone (LH, 黃體生成素), which in turn stimulates Leydig cells. Normal adult male serum testosterone (血清睾酮) ranges roughly from 300 to 1,000 ng/dL, but this figure is misleading. Only the “free” unbound fraction — roughly 1–3% — is biologically active. The rest is bound to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG, 性激素結合球蛋白) or albumin (白蛋白), acting as a circulating reservoir. SHBG levels vary enormously between individuals: high SHBG dramatically reduces bioavailable testosterone, even when total testosterone appears normal on a blood test.

The anabolic (合成代謝) effects of testosterone are mediated almost entirely through the androgen receptor (AR, 雄激素受體), a nuclear receptor (核受體) found in skeletal muscle (骨骼肌), bone (骨骼), and many other tissues. Testosterone diffuses across the cell membrane, binds the AR, and the hormone-receptor complex translocates to the nucleus (細胞核), where it acts as a transcription factor (轉錄因子) — upregulating genes for insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1, 類胰島素生長因子), satellite cell (衛星細胞) activation, and nitrogen retention. Critically, individuals differ substantially in AR density (receptor number per cell) and AR sensitivity, which is partly determined by a CAG repeat polymorphism (CAG重複多態性) in the AR gene: shorter CAG repeats correlate with higher receptor sensitivity and, on average, greater anabolic response to a given testosterone level. This means two men with identical serum testosterone can experience meaningfully different muscle-building responses to identical training — because one’s receptors are more sensitive or more numerous.

Testosterone follows a clear circadian rhythm (晝夜節律): it peaks in the early morning (7–8 AM) and falls by 30–50% through the day, which is why clinical testing standardises blood draws before 10 AM. Resistance exercise acutely raises testosterone for 15–30 minutes post-exercise (the magnitude depends on exercise volume, intensity, and rest periods), but the long-term training effect on resting testosterone is modest in already-healthy men — chronic training optimises the HPG axis rather than dramatically elevating baseline levels. Age-related testosterone decline (hypogonadism, 性腺功能減退症) begins around 35–40 at roughly 1% per year; symptoms typically emerge when total testosterone falls below 300 ng/dL. Testosterone is also converted peripherally to oestradiol (雌二醇) by aromatase (芳香化酶), and to the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT, 二氫睾酮) by 5-alpha-reductase (5-alpha還原酶) — explaining why some androgenic side effects (hair loss, prostate enlargement) involve DHT rather than testosterone directly.

Total serum testosterone is only partially predictive because most of it (~97%) is bound to SHBG (性激素結合球蛋白) or albumin and is biologically inactive. Only the free fraction crosses cell membranes to engage the androgen receptor (AR, 雄激素受體). Variable 1: SHBG levels differ substantially between individuals; high SHBG dramatically reduces bioavailable testosterone even when total testosterone appears normal. Variable 2: AR sensitivity and density differ due to a CAG repeat polymorphism (CAG重複多態性) in the AR gene — shorter CAG repeats confer higher receptor sensitivity, so the same testosterone signal produces a stronger transcriptional response (more IGF-1, satellite cell activation, nitrogen retention) in one person than another. Together these mean anabolic response is a function of free testosterone × receptor responsiveness, not total testosterone alone.

Your Posture Isn’t the Problem: What the Evidence Actually Says About Slouching, Sitting, and Back Pain (背痛)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
You have probably been told that sitting up straight, not slouching, and maintaining “correct” posture prevents back pain (背痛). Is this advice supported by the scientific evidence — or is it a deeply rooted cultural belief with little biomechanical backing?
The natural curvatures of the spinal column (脊柱) — Wikimedia Commons

Low back pain (腰痛) is the leading cause of disability (殘疾) worldwide, and the conventional response has always been to correct posture. Workplaces invest in ergonomic chairs (人體工學椅) and standing desks (站立式辦公桌); physiotherapists (物理治療師) teach patients to “sit tall”; gyms warn trainees against rounded backs. The problem is that systematic reviews (系統評論) have repeatedly failed to find a consistent link between spinal posture and low back pain. A comprehensive review by Roffey et al. (2010) in the journal Spine examined the best available occupational research and concluded there was “no conclusive evidence” that sustained awkward posture causes low back pain. A major 2019 review by Slater et al. in Musculoskeletal Science & Practice reviewed 54 studies and found no reliable association between lumbar (腰椎) lordosis (前凸), sitting posture, or “poor” alignment and either the presence or severity of back pain. People with “textbook” posture have back pain; people who sit slumped all day often have none.

Part of the explanation lies in what biomechanics (生物力學) research has revealed about spinal loading. The spine (脊柱) is not a fragile stack of blocks that needs constant correct alignment to survive — it is a robust, actively stabilised structure surrounded by muscles, ligaments (韌帶), and intervertebral discs (椎間盤) that handle compressive and shear forces across a wide range of postures. Nachemson’s classic intradiscal pressure (椎間盤內壓) measurements from the 1960s showed that sitting upright produces higher disc pressure than lying down — but those measurements have since been challenged and refined. The more recent consensus is that no single posture is universally dangerous or safe; what matters is movement variability (動作多樣性). Staying in any fixed position for extended periods — whether perfect or imperfect posture — reduces circulation to spinal tissues and increases fatigability. The real culprit is not how you sit but how long you sit without moving.

Standing desks (站立式辦公桌) are now marketed as the posture antidote to sitting, but the evidence is similarly underwhelming. A 2018 Cochrane review found that sit-stand desks produce modest reductions in sitting time (~1 hour/day) but have no reliable effect on back pain outcomes. Prolonged standing introduces its own problems: varicose veins (靜脈曲張), leg fatigue, and increased discomfort. The nocebo effect (反安慰劑效應) is also significant: telling patients their back is structurally vulnerable and that their posture is damaging amplifies pain by increasing fear, vigilance, and avoidance of movement — exactly the opposite of what promotes recovery. Evidence-based back pain management (Hartvigsen et al. 2018, Lancet) now emphasises staying active, graded return to normal activities, and psychological factors over postural correction. Movement breaks every 30–40 minutes matter more than whether your lumbar (腰椎) curve is “perfect.”

Prime Before You Sprint: The Science of Post-Activation Potentiation (後激活增強效應) and Why a Heavy Squat Makes You Jump Higher

🤔 Predict Before You Read
If you did a set of very heavy squats five minutes before a vertical jump test, do you think you would jump higher or lower than without the prior squats? Why might fatiguing your muscles first actually enhance rather than impair your explosive performance?
Strength training equipment — Wikimedia Commons

Post-Activation Potentiation (PAP, 後激活增強效應) is the phenomenon whereby a prior high-intensity muscular contraction transiently increases the force output (力量輸出) and rate of force development (RFD, 力量發展速率) of subsequent explosive movements. In practice: do a set of heavy squats at 85–95% of your one-repetition maximum (1RM), rest for 5–10 minutes, then perform a vertical jump, sprint, or Olympic lift — and your performance in that explosive activity will likely exceed what it would have been without the priming set. This counterintuitive finding has been replicated across dozens of studies. Hodgson et al. (2005), reviewing the mechanistic literature in Sports Medicine, attributed the primary mechanism to phosphorylation (磷酸化) of myosin regulatory light chains (MLC, 肌球蛋白調節輕鏈) in Type II fast-twitch muscle fibres (快肌纖維). This phosphorylation, triggered by the heavy set, increases the calcium sensitivity (鈣離子敏感性) of the contractile apparatus — meaning the same calcium signal now generates more cross-bridge cycling (橫橋循環) force.

The PAP effect competes directly with fatigue from the heavy priming set. This creates a characteristic temporal window: immediately after a heavy set, fatigue dominates and performance declines. As fatigue dissipates faster than potentiation fades, there is an optimal window — typically 5–12 minutes after the priming effort — where potentiation is still near its peak but fatigue has largely recovered. The optimal window varies significantly between individuals based on training status: highly trained athletes recover from fatigue more rapidly and show potentiation for longer, typically benefiting from slightly longer rest periods (8–12 minutes). Less-trained individuals may be more heavily fatigued by the same relative intensity and need either a shorter window or lighter priming sets. The PAP effect is also modality-specific: priming with a back squat (深蹲) enhances lower-body explosive outputs; upper-body heavy pressing enhances upper-body power more than lower-body tasks.

PAP has been practically applied in two contexts: as a warm-up strategy in competition (using a heavy lift 8–12 minutes before a sprint or jump event) and as a training method called “complex training” (複合訓練), which pairs a heavy strength movement with a biomechanically similar plyometric (增強式訓練) exercise in the same session — for example, heavy squat followed by box jumps (跳箱). Lorenz (2011) in the Strength and Conditioning Journal summarised evidence that complex training improves both peak power (峰值功率) and athletic performance metrics beyond traditional strength or plyometric training alone, though the effect size varies with programming. The key practical limitation is the rest period requirement: the 5–12 minute window is logistically awkward in team settings, and the heavy priming set itself must be in the athlete’s toolkit (novice lifters are too fatigued by the load to benefit). Used correctly, PAP is one of the few legally available tools that genuinely acutely enhances explosive performance — not just conditions it over months.

The primary mechanism is phosphorylation (磷酸化) of myosin regulatory light chains (MLC) in Type II fast-twitch fibres, triggered by the high-intensity priming set. Because resting calcium concentrations in the muscle are unchanged, the same neural signal now produces more cross-bridge cycling and higher force output. Trained athletes have faster fatigue recovery and thus benefit from longer rest periods within this range.

Borrowing in Yen, Investing in Yield: How the Currency Carry Trade (套息交易) Exploits a Crack in Finance Theory — and Why It Periodically Explodes

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Finance theory says that interest rate differentials between countries should be exactly offset by currency movements — so a high-interest currency should depreciate to equalise returns. If markets were perfectly efficient, there should be no profit from borrowing cheap and investing at higher rates. Why might this theory persistently fail in practice?
Foreign exchange currencies — Wikimedia Commons

The currency carry trade (套息交易) is one of the most persistently profitable and persistently dangerous strategies in global finance. The mechanics are simple: borrow in a low-interest-rate currency — classically the Japanese yen (日圓, JPY) at near-zero rates — convert to a high-interest-rate currency such as the Australian dollar (澳元, AUD) or New Zealand dollar (紐西蘭元, NZD), and invest in that currency’s money market or sovereign bonds. The interest rate differential (利差) is the gross profit. Finance theory says this should not work: Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIP, 未拋補利率平價) holds that a high-yield currency should depreciate by exactly the amount of the yield advantage — otherwise arbitrage (套利) would eliminate the profit. Empirically, the opposite tends to happen: high-yield currencies tend to appreciate over short-to-medium periods, compounding the carry trader’s return. This systematic violation of UIP is called the “forward premium puzzle” (遠期溢價謎題), one of the most replicated anomalies in international finance.

Lustig and Verdelhan (2007, American Economic Review) documented that a portfolio of carry trades produced Sharpe ratios (夏普比率) comparable to equity markets over decades, which is extraordinary for a currency strategy. The explanation for why the profit persists involves two factors. First, carry trades are exposed to “crash risk” (崩潰風險): they deliver small, steady gains for extended periods and then suffer sudden, severe losses in precisely the moments when other risk assets (風險資產) are also crashing — they are short volatility (做空波動率). This makes the strategy rational on average but extremely painful in tail events. Second, the strategy is capacity-constrained: as more capital floods into carry trades, the differential compresses; when sudden risk-off (避險情緒) episodes trigger simultaneous unwinding, leverage amplifies the move.

The yen carry trade (日圓套息交易) unwind of 2007–2008 was a systemic event. As the global financial crisis (全球金融危機) erupted, risk appetite collapsed. Investors rushed to buy back yen to repay their loans, driving JPY up 30% against AUD in weeks — simultaneously as Australian assets fell. The carry trader suffered a double hit: the investment side fell and the funding currency rose. Brunnermeier, Nagel, and Pedersen (2009, Journal of Finance) showed that carry trade drawdowns cluster with global market crashes, liquidity spirals, and VIX (恐慌指數) spikes. More recently, the August 2024 yen carry unwind — triggered by the Bank of Japan (日本銀行) unexpectedly raising rates — caused significant dislocations across global equities in days. The carry trade thus encodes a fundamental tension: the strategy earns a risk premium (風險溢價) for being short the insurance that rational investors need in a crisis — and that insurance comes due at the worst possible moments.

Answer this without looking back: What is the “forward premium puzzle,” and why does the yen carry trade unwind so violently during financial crises, producing a double loss for carry traders?
Check your answer
The forward premium puzzle (遠期溢價謎題): Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIP, 未拋補利率平價) predicts that a currency with a higher interest rate should depreciate by the amount of the rate differential, eliminating the carry profit. Empirically, high-yield currencies tend to appreciate (not depreciate) over short-to-medium terms — the opposite of theory — meaning carry traders earn both the interest differential and a currency gain. The puzzle has persisted in data for decades. The yen unwind double loss: when a risk-off (避險) event strikes, investors must buy back yen to repay carry loans, driving JPY sharply higher (sometimes 20–30% in weeks). Simultaneously, the high-yield assets in which carry proceeds were invested (e.g., Australian bonds, equities, commodities) also fall. The carry trader loses on both legs at once: the funding currency rises and the investment currency falls — correlation goes to 1 with all other risk-asset losses at the worst possible moment.

⚖️ Political Economics

Who Holds the Debt When No One Agrees? Argentina’s Sovereign Default (主權違約), Holdout Creditors, and the Architecture of Debt Restructuring

🤔 Predict Before You Read
When a country cannot repay its debts, it negotiates a “haircut” with creditors. But what happens if some creditors refuse the deal and demand full repayment? Who has more leverage — the sovereign nation or the lone holdout creditor?
Flag of Argentina (阿根廷) — Wikimedia Commons
📍 Event
In December 2001, Argentina (阿根廷) defaulted on $100 billion in sovereign debt — the largest in history at the time. After years of recession, currency-board constraints pegging the peso (比索) to the dollar, and IMF (國際貨幣基金組織) austerity programmes, the country was insolvent. Argentina restructured its debt twice — in 2005 and 2010 — with roughly 93% of bondholders accepting steep haircuts (~65–70 cents on the dollar). But holdout creditors, led by hedge fund NML Capital (a unit of Elliott Management), bought the distressed bonds at deeply discounted prices and refused to participate in either exchange, pursuing full face-value repayment through US courts. In 2012, Judge Thomas Griesa (格里薩法官) in New York ruled that Argentina must pay the holdouts under the pari passu (均等清償) clause — meaning it could not pay restructured bondholders without simultaneously paying holdouts in full. Argentina refused; the ruling effectively froze its 2014 interest payments and triggered a “technical default,” even though Argentina had the money and willingness to pay restructured creditors. Argentina settled under President Macri (馬克里) in 2016.
📖 Theory
Bulow and Rogoff (1989, American Economic Review) showed that a sovereign’s ability to borrow depends on reputation: a country that defaults loses access to capital markets and is punished by exclusion. This suggests creditors hold long-run leverage because repeat access to debt matters. The theory predicts that sovereigns should pay even when the law cannot enforce it, because future borrowing costs matter.
⚠️ Anomaly
Argentina repeatedly defaulted (1989, 2001, 2014, 2020) yet re-accessed capital markets relatively quickly each time — suggesting the reputational punishment is milder than Bulow-Rogoff predict. Worse, the holdout strategy proved that a minority of creditors could block a settlement that 93% of bondholders had accepted, creating a “vulture fund” (禿鷹基金) problem: it is rational to hold out if others agree to haircuts, because you may get paid in full while free-riding on the collective restructuring. This collective action problem makes future voluntary restructurings harder to achieve.
🧩 Challenger
The solution most widely adopted since 2014 is Collective Action Clauses (CACs, 集體行動條款): contractual provisions that bind all bondholders to a restructuring once a supermajority (typically 75%) agrees. CACs were phased into sovereign bond contracts after the Argentina crisis, and the IMF (國際貨幣基金組織) made them a standard recommendation. Whether they fully solve the problem remains open — Ecuador’s 2020 restructuring and Ukraine’s 2022 pause showed CACs can work, but the mechanism’s resilience in a large, complex default remains untested.

(2016). “Sovereign Debt: Now What?” Yale Journal of International Law; IMF (2014). “Strengthening the Contractual Framework to Address Collective Action Problems in Sovereign Debt Restructuring.”

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 6 & 10 — The Peak-End Rule and the Carry Trade Crash

Kahneman’s peak-end rule shows that how we remember an experience is dominated by its worst moment and its final moment — not the total accumulated pleasure or pain. The yen carry trade has the same structure in reverse: it delivers small, steady gains across hundreds of calm trading days, but its “peak pain” — a sudden 30% yen spike in a crisis — combined with the way it ends (forced liquidation at the worst price) dominates how market participants remember and model the strategy. Fund managers who have lived through a carry crash know it viscerally; those who only see the Sharpe ratio see the long smooth accumulation. In both psychology and finance, aggregate experience is a poor guide to decision-making; what drives future choices is the vividness of the worst moment and how things ended. So what: Before entering any strategy or experience with long smooth gains and rare catastrophic losses, ask not “what is the average return?” but “what will the ending feel like when it goes wrong — and can I live with that memory?”
