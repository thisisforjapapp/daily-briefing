The Grainy Dot That Became a World With a Heart

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In July 2015, after a nine-year journey across three billion miles, a piano-sized spacecraft raced past a distant icy world for a single, irreversible flyby. Before reading on, what do you think scientists actually expected to see on its surface, and what did the real images reveal that overturned those expectations?
Pluto (冥王星) in true colour, imaged by NASA’s New Horizons (新視野號) spacecraft during its July 2015 flyby, the heart-shaped Tombaugh Regio visible at centre-right. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

On 14 July 2015, NASA’s (美國太空總署) New Horizons (新視野號) spacecraft shot past Pluto (冥王星) at over 49,000 km/h, ending a nine-and-a-half-year journey covering nearly five billion kilometres, the most distant object any spacecraft had ever explored up close. Launched in 2006, months before Pluto was reclassified a “dwarf planet” (矮行星), the piano-sized probe had only hours near its target: no orbit, no second pass, just one narrow window to gather data before hurtling onward into the Kuiper Belt (柯伊伯帶). Scientists expected a dead, crater-battered ball of ice resembling Earth’s Moon.

Instead, the images beamed back over the following months revealed a startlingly young, active world. A vast, smooth plain of nitrogen ice (氮冰), shaped unmistakably like a heart and later named Tombaugh Regio (湯博區) after Pluto’s 1930 discoverer Clyde Tombaugh (克萊德·湯博), dominated one hemisphere, almost entirely free of impact craters, meaning something had resurfaced it within the last 10 million years, geologically yesterday. Mountains of water ice (水冰) taller than the Rockies rose nearby, and possible cryovolcanoes (冰火山) suggested Pluto still generates internal heat despite sitting 5.9 billion kilometres from the Sun (太陽).

The flyby forced planetary scientists to abandon the assumption that small, distant worlds must be geologically dead. Nitrogen ice appears to convect slowly across Tombaugh Regio’s basin like a lava lamp, continuously erasing craters, while Pluto’s largest moon Charon (凱倫) showed its own canyon system stretching over 1,600 kilometres. New Horizons then pressed onward, flying past the Kuiper Belt object Arrokoth (阿羅科斯) in 2019, the most distant object ever explored up close, and still transmits data from beyond 8 billion kilometres today.

一件黃袍如何開創三百年大宋

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
顯德七年（960年）正月，後周禁軍統帥率軍北上禦敵，行至陳橋驛時忽然停下。翌日清晨，將士擁立他為帝，一件黃袍加身，改變了此後三百餘年的中國歷史。你認為，這場看似突如其來的兵變，究竟是將士自發之舉，還是早有預謀？
背景速覽

核心人物：趙匡胤（後周殿前都點檢）；柴宗訓（後周恭帝，年僅七歲）；趙普、趙光義。

事件：960年陳橋兵變，趙匡胤黃袍加身，代周建宋。

時代背景：五代十國以來政權更迭頻繁，武將擁兵自重，後周世宗英年早逝，幼主繼位，政局不穩。

宋太祖趙匡胤畫像，960年陳橋兵變黃袍加身，代後周而建立宋朝。來源：維基媒體共享資源

後周顯德七年（960年）正月初一，邊境忽然傳來北漢與遼國聯兵入侵的警報，年僅七歲的後周恭帝柴宗訓與符太后倉皇之間，只得倚仗殿前都點檢趙匡胤率禁軍出征禦敵。趙匡胤統領大軍出東京開封，行至陳橋驛（今河南封丘東南）時，天色已晚，大軍就地紮營。是夜，軍中忽然謠傳「今上幼弱，未能親政，我輩出死力破敵，誰則知之？不若先立點檢為天子，然後北征，未晚也」，將士議論紛紛，人心浮動，趙匡胤之弟趙光義與心腹趙普則在暗中穿針引線，部署妥當。

翌日清晨，趙匡胤仍在營帳中酣睡，忽被喧嘩聲驚醒，出帳一看，只見諸將早已擐甲執兵，齊聲呼喊「諸軍無主，願策太尉為天子」，不由分說便將一件象徵天子的黃袍披在他身上，跪拜山呼萬歲，聲震原野。趙匡胤佯作推辭再三，聲稱事出突然，非其本意，眾將堅執不從，趙匡胤遂與諸將約法：入城後不得驚犯太后及幼主，不得侵凌朝臣百姓，違者必斬。約定既定，大軍隨即回師開封，守城將領本為趙匡胤舊部，早已暗中接應，開城迎入，幾乎兵不血刃。

趙匡胤入城後，逼令後周宰相范質、王溥率百官承認既成事實，柴宗訓被迫禪位，趙匡胤登基稱帝，改國號為「宋」，是為宋太祖，五代最後一個朝代後周就此覆亡。鑑於自身正是以禁軍統帥身份奪權，趙匡胤即位後深忌武將專擅，建隆二年（961年）設宴石守信等宿將，即歷史上著名的「杯酒釋兵權」，勸令諸將交出兵權，換取田宅厚祿以終天年，從此確立宋代重文輕武、強幹弱枝的政治格局，影響有宋一代三百餘年國策。

🏛 History — Western

The Peace Treaty That Invented the Modern Country

🤔 Predict Before You Read
After thirty years of religious war had killed roughly a third of the population in some German territories, diplomats from over a hundred states gathered in two cities to negotiate for four years. Before reading on, what do you think the resulting 1648 settlement actually established that still shapes how every country in the world interacts today?
Context

Key figures: Holy Roman Emperor Ferdinand III (斐迪南三世); diplomats representing France, Sweden, Spain, and over 100 German states.

Event at stake: The 1648 Peace of Westphalia (西發里亞和約), ending the Thirty Years’ War (三十年戰爭).

Big picture: A religious conflict within the Holy Roman Empire (神聖羅馬帝國) escalated into a continent-wide struggle that killed an estimated 8 million people.

The town hall in Münster (明斯特), Germany, where the 1648 Peace of Westphalia (西發里亞和約) was signed, ending the Thirty Years’ War. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The Thirty Years’ War (三十年戰爭) began in 1618 when Protestant nobles in Bohemia (波希米亞) threw two Catholic officials out of a Prague castle window, an incident known as the Defenestration of Prague (布拉格擲窗事件), triggering a revolt against Habsburg (哈布斯堡王朝) rule. What began as a religious dispute within the Holy Roman Empire (神聖羅馬帝國) spiralled into a continental war drawing in Sweden, France, Spain, and Denmark, each pursuing territorial and dynastic ambitions under a religious banner. By the 1640s, some German territories had lost over a third of their population to combat, famine, and disease.

Exhausted and unable to achieve outright victory, the warring powers sent delegations to two Westphalian (威斯特伐利亞) cities, Münster (明斯特) and Osnabrück (奧斯納布呂克), beginning formal negotiations in 1644. Talks dragged on for four years partly over protocol: delegates from over 100 states argued for months about seating order and titles before substantive negotiations even began, since precedence implied rank among sovereigns. The 1648 treaties, signed in Münster’s town hall, granted rulers within the Empire the right to determine their own state’s religion and, crucially, recognised each participating state as sovereign, answerable to no higher authority, religious or imperial.

This principle, later termed “Westphalian sovereignty” (西發里亞主權), became the conceptual foundation of the modern international system: states as legally equal units with exclusive authority over their own territory, free from outside interference in internal affairs. Historians debate how deliberately negotiators intended this outcome versus how much later scholars retroactively imposed the framework onto a messier settlement, but the practical effect endured. The Holy Roman Empire limped on for another 150 years, fatally weakened, while the sovereign nation-state, not the transnational religious empire, became the basic unit of world politics for centuries to come.

一個平民思想家如何以「兼愛」挑戰整個貴族世界

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
戰國初年，一位出身工匠階層的思想家四處奔走，反對當時貴族盛行的厚葬久喪、繁複禮樂，甚至親率弟子跋涉十日十夜，只為阻止一場即將爆發的戰爭。你認為，他憑甚麼說服敵國君主放棄攻打一個弱小城邦？
背景速覽

核心人物：墨子（墨翟），戰國初期思想家，創立墨家學派。

核心思想：兼愛（不分親疏遠近平等相愛）、非攻（反對侵略戰爭）。

時代背景：春秋戰國之際諸侯兼併頻仍，儒家禮樂厚葬之風盛行於貴族階層，墨子出身平民工匠，另闢蹊徑，對抗當時主流思潮。

墨子畫像，戰國初期思想家，創立墨家學派，提倡「兼愛」「非攻」。來源：維基媒體共享資源

墨子，名翟，相傳原為宋國人，後長期活動於魯國，出身低微，早年習於工匠之技，精於器械製作，與當時多出身貴族士人的儒家學者迥然不同。墨子目睹諸侯征伐不斷、生靈塗炭，又見貴族厚葬久喪、鐘鼓之樂耗費民財，遂創立墨家學派，聚眾講學，弟子多來自工匠、農民等下層社會，組織嚴密，首領稱「鉅子」，行事紀律森嚴，甚至有「墨者之法，殺人者死」的內部規條，儼然一個具高度組織力的社會團體。

墨子學說核心為「兼愛」，主張愛人如愛己，不應因親疏貴賤而有差等之別，這與儒家「親親有術，尊賢有等」的差等之愛形成鮮明對立，孟子即批評墨子「兼愛」是「無父」之說。與「兼愛」相輔而行的是「非攻」，墨子認為侵略戰爭是天下大害，曾親率弟子三百人，日夜兼程十日十夜趕赴楚國郢都，與公輸班（魯班）當場模擬攻防九次，公輸班九次攻城器械皆被墨子破解，楚王終於放棄攻打宋國的計劃，此事後世稱為「止楚攻宋」。

墨子又提出「尚賢」、「尚同」、「節用」、「節葬」、「非樂」等主張，反對世襲貴族壟斷官職，主張選賢任能；反對厚葬久喪、盛大禮樂，認為徒耗民財而無益於治國。墨家學說在戰國一度與儒家並稱「顯學」，門徒遍及各國，然而其嚴苛的組織紀律與儉樸主張，亦使其在漢代以後逐漸式微，直至清末民初，梁啟超、胡適等學者重新發掘墨學價值，墨子「兼愛非攻」的理念方才重獲關注。

The Thought Experiment That Asks You to Design Society Before You’re Born

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Imagine designing the rules for an entire society, its laws, its distribution of wealth, its opportunities, but with no idea in advance whether you would be born rich or poor, healthy or disabled, into a majority or minority group. Before reading on, what do you think a rational person negotiating from behind that blindfold would actually choose?
Context

Key figure: John Rawls (羅爾斯, 1921–2002), American political philosopher at Harvard University (哈佛大學).

Idea at stake: The “veil of ignorance” (無知之幕) thought experiment from Rawls’s 1971 book A Theory of Justice (《正義論》).

Big picture: A challenge to utilitarianism (功利主義), which Rawls argued could justify sacrificing a minority’s basic rights to maximise overall happiness.

John Rawls (羅爾斯), whose 1971 book A Theory of Justice introduced the “veil of ignorance” (無知之幕) thought experiment. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In his 1971 book A Theory of Justice (《正義論》), Harvard philosopher John Rawls (羅爾斯) proposed a thought experiment to answer an old question: what makes a society’s basic rules genuinely fair? He asked readers to imagine a hypothetical “original position” (原初狀態) in which people negotiating society’s fundamental rules sit behind a “veil of ignorance” (無知之幕), knowing nothing about their own future race, gender, wealth, talents, or social class. Rawls argued this forces genuinely impartial reasoning, since no one could rig the rules to favour their own circumstances, because they wouldn’t yet know what those circumstances would be.

Rawls argued rational people negotiating under this veil would not choose strict utilitarianism (功利主義), which permits sacrificing a minority’s wellbeing if it maximises the total happiness of the majority, because anyone might turn out to be in that sacrificed minority. Instead, he proposed they would choose principles guaranteeing equal basic liberties for all, and would only accept social and economic inequalities if arranged so the greatest benefit went to society’s least advantaged members, a principle he called the “difference principle” (差異原則). This offered a rigorous alternative to both raw utilitarianism and unregulated free-market libertarianism (自由至上主義).

Critics attacked from multiple directions: philosopher Robert Nozick (諾齊克) argued Rawls’s redistribution violated individuals’ rights to justly acquired property, while communitarian critics like Michael Sandel (桑德爾) questioned whether a truly disembodied, history-less self behind the veil could reason about justice at all, since real people’s values are shaped by the very identities Rawls asked them to strip away. Despite these challenges, A Theory of Justice remains one of the most influential works of 20th-century political philosophy, reshaping debates on distributive justice and welfare policy across law, economics, and political science.

🧠 Psychology

Why a Smaller Menu Sometimes Sells More

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In a now-famous 2000 experiment, researchers set up two tasting tables in an upscale grocery store, one offering 24 varieties of jam, the other just 6. Before reading on, which table do you think attracted more browsers, and which one actually sold more jam to the people who stopped by?
A grocery store aisle — the setting of Iyengar and Lepper’s famous jam-tasting experiment on choice overload (選擇超載). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In 2000, psychologists Sheena Iyengar (希娜·艾恩嘉) and Mark Lepper (馬克·萊珀) set up a tasting booth in an upscale California grocery store, alternating between displaying 24 varieties of jam and, at other times, just 6. The larger display attracted more browsers, roughly 60% of passing shoppers stopped to look, compared to 40% at the smaller table, confirming the intuitive assumption that more choice draws more interest. But when it came to actually buying jam afterward, the results flipped: only 3% of shoppers who browsed the 24-jam table made a purchase, compared to 30% of those who browsed the 6-jam table.

This pattern, now widely called “choice overload” (選擇超載) or “overchoice” (過度選擇), a close cousin of decision fatigue (決策疲勞) where accumulating small decisions depletes mental resources, suggests that beyond a certain point, additional options make decisions harder rather than easier, raising anxiety about picking the “wrong” one and sometimes leading people to defer the decision entirely. Researchers propose this happens because evaluating trade-offs between many similar options taxes working memory (工作記憶) and triggers a fear of foregone alternatives, the nagging sense that a better option existed among those not chosen.

The original jam study has faced replication challenges: a 2010 meta-analysis by Benjamin Scheibehenne (沙伊貝亨) and colleagues, pooling over 50 similar studies, found the choice overload effect is real but inconsistent, appearing strongly in some contexts, such as retirement fund options, and disappearing in others, depending on how meaningfully the options differ and how much time pressure or expertise the chooser has. The safer takeaway isn’t “always offer fewer choices,” but that large option sets should be curated thoughtfully, since raw quantity of choice is not the same as quality of decision-making.

🫀 Physiology

The Involuntary Confession Your Own Face Makes

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Charles Darwin once called it “the most peculiar and most human of all expressions,” a physical reaction that happens involuntarily and cannot be faked, often at the exact moment you’d most like to hide your feelings. Before reading on, what do you think actually triggers it, and why can’t you simply will yourself to stop?
The sympathetic nervous system (交感神經系統) — the same involuntary pathway that governs heart rate and sweating also drives blushing (臉紅). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Blushing (臉紅) begins when the brain’s amygdala (杏仁核) and related emotional-processing regions detect social threat, specifically the sense of being watched, judged, or caught in an embarrassing moment, and signal the sympathetic nervous system (交感神經系統) to release adrenaline (腎上腺素). This triggers rapid dilation of blood vessels (血管擴張) in the face, ears, and upper chest, an area with a uniquely dense network of small, close-to-the-surface vessels compared to the rest of the body, allowing visible reddening to appear within seconds and fade just as quickly once the triggering moment passes.

What makes blushing unusual is that it is almost impossible to consciously suppress once triggered, since the sympathetic nervous system operates largely outside voluntary control, the same system governing heart rate and sweating. Darwin (達爾文) noted in his 1872 book on emotional expression that blushing appears uniquely human, tied to self-conscious awareness of how one appears to others, rather than a general fear or pain response; animals show fear reactions but nothing resembling embarrassment-triggered facial reddening, suggesting it evolved alongside complex social cognition (社會認知) rather than basic survival threat detection.

Some researchers propose blushing may serve a social function despite its discomfort: because it cannot be faked, an involuntary blush after a social mistake may signal genuine remorse to onlookers, making the blusher appear more trustworthy rather than simply guilty. For people with chronic excessive blushing, a condition sometimes called erythrophobia (臉紅恐懼症), the anticipatory anxiety about blushing itself often triggers more blushing, a self-reinforcing loop that cognitive behavioural therapy (認知行為治療) and, in severe cases, targeted nerve interruption can help address.

What Switching to a Standing Desk Actually Does to Your Body

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Standing desks became a workplace fixture after headlines warned that “sitting is the new smoking,” promising to burn more calories, ease back pain, and offset the health risks of a desk job. Before reading on, what do you think the research shows: does simply standing instead of sitting meaningfully improve your health?
A sit-stand desk (可調式站立辦公桌) — evidence suggests alternating positions and moving regularly matters more than standing alone. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

The “sitting is the new smoking” (久坐如吸煙) alarm grew out of large observational studies linking prolonged daily sitting to higher rates of cardiovascular disease (心血管疾病), type 2 diabetes (二型糖尿病), and earlier death, even among people who exercised regularly. Standing desks were marketed as a straightforward fix, and manufacturers pointed to studies showing standing burns modestly more calories than sitting, roughly 8 to 10 extra calories per hour, and can reduce the after-meal blood sugar (血糖) spike compared with sitting for the same period, giving the intervention plausible biological grounding beyond simple market hype.

However, a 2024 systematic review in the International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity, examining data from over 83,000 UK adults, found that standing for long periods on its own showed no meaningful reduction in cardiovascular risk and was actually associated with increased risk of circulatory problems like varicose veins (靜脈曲張) once standing exceeded roughly two hours continuously per day. The modest extra calorie burn from standing is far too small to meaningfully offset a sedentary lifestyle (久坐生活方式) on its own, undercutting the simplest version of the standing-desk pitch.

What the evidence does consistently support is movement itself, taking short walking breaks every 30 to 60 minutes, rather than static standing in place, since it is muscle contraction and change in posture, not the standing position itself, that most reliably improves blood sugar regulation and circulation. The most practical use of a standing desk isn’t standing continuously all day, but alternating between sitting and standing every 30 minutes or so while building in regular short walks, treating the desk as one small tool among several.

🏋 Sports Science

The Pre-Workout Ritual That Might Be Working Against You

🤔 Predict Before You Read
For decades, gym-goers have been told to hold each major muscle group in a stretched position for 20-30 seconds before any workout, to “warm up” and prevent injury. Before reading on, what do you think research has found about how this specific type of stretching affects the strength and power you’re about to produce?
Strength training (力量訓練) — research shows static stretching immediately beforehand can temporarily reduce power output. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Static stretching (靜態伸展), holding a muscle at its lengthened end position without movement for 15-60 seconds, has been standard pre-workout advice for generations, based on the logic that a looser muscle should move more safely through a full range of motion. But a substantial body of research since the early 2000s, including a widely cited 2016 meta-analysis by Behm and colleagues in the European Journal of Applied Physiology, found that static stretching performed immediately before strength or power activities actually reduces maximal force output and jump height by an average of roughly 5%, an effect called “stretch-induced strength loss” (伸展誘發肌力下降).

The proposed mechanism involves both mechanical and neural factors: prolonged static stretching temporarily reduces the muscle-tendon unit’s stiffness (肌腱僵硬度), meaning less elastic energy gets stored and returned during rapid, powerful movements, while the nervous system also appears to temporarily reduce its own motor unit (運動單元) recruitment and firing rate following a sustained stretch, dialling down the signal telling the muscle to contract forcefully. This effect is generally short-lived, dissipating within about 10 to 15 minutes, but for anyone stretching immediately before a heavy lift, sprint, or jump, that brief window is exactly when it matters most.

Current sports science guidance instead recommends dynamic stretching (動態伸展), actively moving joints through a full range of motion, such as leg swings or walking lunges, which raises muscle temperature and prepares the nervous system for activity without the temporary force deficit. Static stretching remains valuable and appropriate, but its best-supported use is after exercise for flexibility and recovery, or during separate dedicated mobility sessions, rather than as the immediate pre-workout ritual gym culture inherited from earlier decades.

📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

The Flower Bulb That (Almost) Broke the Dutch Economy

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In the 1630s, a single tulip bulb of the rarest variety reportedly traded for more than the price of a house in Amsterdam, changing hands as a paper contract promising a bulb not yet dug up. Before reading on, what do you think happened when this market suddenly stopped rising, and how severe was the damage that followed?
Tulips (鬱金香) — rare varieties became the object of one of history’s most-cited speculative bubbles in 1630s Holland. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Tulips (鬱金香) arrived in the Netherlands from the Ottoman Empire (鄂圖曼帝國) in the late 16th century, and by the 1630s, rare, virus-streaked varieties like the Semper Augustus (常青奧古斯都) had become status symbols among the Dutch merchant elite, prized for unpredictable, flame-like petal patterns that growers didn’t yet know were caused by a mosaic virus rather than deliberate breeding. As demand grew, trading shifted from actual bulbs, which could only be planted and sold within a narrow annual window, to futures contracts (期貨合約) promising delivery of bulbs still in the ground, letting speculation accelerate far faster than the physical flower market ever could.

By the winter of 1636-37, prices for the rarest bulb varieties had reportedly risen more than tenfold within weeks, with contemporary accounts describing a single bulb trading for roughly the price of a well-appointed Amsterdam canal house, though modern economic historians note surviving price records are patchier and less extreme than popular legend suggests. In February 1637, at a routine bulb auction in Haarlem, buyers simply failed to show up, and prices collapsed within days across the entire market, leaving traders holding futures contracts for bulbs suddenly worth a fraction of the agreed price.

Economist Peter Garber (加伯) has argued in influential research that Tulip Mania’s (鬱金香狂熱) broader economic damage was far more limited than folklore claims: it involved a relatively small, specialised network of merchants and collectors rather than the general Dutch population, and the Dutch Golden Age (荷蘭黃金時代) economy continued thriving for decades afterward. The episode nonetheless endures as history’s most-cited shorthand for speculative bubbles, and economic historians still debate whether it represents genuine irrational mania or a market rationally pricing rare genetic novelties that simply ran out of new buyers.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

The Minimum-Wage Study That Economics Textbooks Didn’t See Coming

Minimum wage (最低工資) rates vary by US state — the 1994 New Jersey/Pennsylvania comparison became one of labour economics’ most debated natural experiments. Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
In 1992, New Jersey (新澤西州) raised its state minimum wage (最低工資) from $4.25 to $5.05 per hour while neighbouring Pennsylvania’s rate stayed unchanged, and economists David Card (大衛·卡德) and Alan Krueger (艾倫·克魯格) surveyed 410 fast-food restaurants on both sides of the border before and after the increase.
📖 Theory
Standard neoclassical labour economics (新古典勞動經濟學) predicts a minimum wage set above the market-clearing wage acts like a price floor, reducing the quantity of labour employers demand, meaning higher minimum wages should measurably shrink low-wage employment, textbook supply-and-demand logic taught for decades.
⚠️ Anomaly
Card and Krueger’s 1994 study found New Jersey’s fast-food employment did not fall relative to Pennsylvania’s after the wage hike, and by some measures rose slightly, directly contradicting the textbook prediction and triggering methodological disputes that continue among economists three decades later.
🧪 Challenger
Card and Krueger proposed monopsony power (買方壟斷力), where employers hold enough wage-setting power over workers that a wage floor can raise pay without cutting jobs. Whether monopsony fully explains fast-food labour markets specifically remains an open question among labour economists.
💡 A famous study found raising the minimum wage didn’t cost jobs where theory said it must — a reminder that textbook curves don’t always survive contact with real data.
📖 Card, D. & Krueger, A.B. (1994), American Economic Review, “Minimum Wages and Employment.” Neumark, D. & Wascher, W. (2000), rebuttal, American Economic Review. Manning, A. (2003), Monopsony in Motion.

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 6 & 9 — More Preparation, Less Follow-Through: The jam study showed that offering 24 choices instead of 6 caused shoppers to buy less, not more, because extra options increase mental load without proportionally improving decisions. Static stretching before a lift shows a strikingly similar pattern: an extra 20 seconds of “preparation” that feels helpful actually saps the very power output the stretcher came to use. Both cards puncture the same assumption, that adding more input, whether options or preparation, always improves the output; sometimes it just adds friction.

So what: Before doing “more” of something meant to help, whether offering extra choices or a longer warm-up ritual, check whether it’s actually calibrated to your goal, or just habit dressed up as diligence.
