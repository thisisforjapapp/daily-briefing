Astronomy & Cosmos

Finding a World Before Seeing It

Predict before you read

If a planet keeps missing its predicted position, what would you check before inventing another planet?

Colour-calibrated Voyager 2 (旅行者二號) view of Neptune (海王星); a modern spacecraft image, not the discovery telescope’s view. Image credit and licence
Extends the earlier view of this planet’s rings (2026-09-07): this time, how a prediction directed discovery.

Before Neptune (海王星) was recognised in 1846, the clue was a different planet refusing to follow the timetable. Uranus (天王星) was not quite where calculations placed it. Urbain Le Verrier (勒維耶) and John Couch Adams (亞當斯) independently asked whether an unseen planet’s gravitational pull (引力) could account for the discrepancy. Johann Galle (伽勒) then found Neptune near Le Verrier’s predicted position. The exciting part was the direction of reasoning: from a visible disturbance to a previously unidentified cause.

Ordinarily, a model starts with bodies and predicts their motion. Here, the astronomers worked backwards: what additional body could produce the observed departures? This is an inverse problem (逆問題). Imagine hearing a regular tapping through a wall: its pattern constrains possible causes, but does not uniquely identify one. Likewise, a calculation could guide a telescope without replacing it. Finding a moving object where the calculation indicated supplied a new observation, rather than merely redescribing the old orbital mismatch.

Now look at the pale blue-green disc in the image, reconstructed from Voyager 2 (旅行者二號) observations. Cloud features belong to a world a spacecraft could inspect during its 1989 flyby; they were not visible evidence available to the discoverers. NASA (美國太空總署) describes an atmosphere containing methane (甲烷), which absorbs red light. The image and the discovery story therefore answer different questions: what this world looks like, and why observers knew where to look. A successful prediction is powerful evidence, but it does not make every proposed invisible cause correct.

A prediction becomes more informative when it leads to a genuinely new observation.
Why was finding the planet more informative than simply fitting the old orbital discrepancy?
Check your answer
The calculation used the old discrepancy to predict where an additional object should be. Observing that object supplied a separate test; fitting the discrepancy alone could not establish that the proposed cause existed.
Sources: NASA: the 1846 discovery · NASA: atmosphere and exploration

一張郵票，怎樣把陌生人接進同一個制度？

閱讀前先想一想

要讓不認識郵差的人也放心寄信，除了有人跑得快，還需要甚麼？

明治初年，前島密推動近代郵政。焦點是如何把原有送信服務改造成可廣泛使用的公共網絡；背景是新政府重組交通與行政制度。
前島密肖像；郵政改革需要組織網絡，並處理原有業者的利益。 圖片來源及授權

一八七一年，日本開始近代郵政服務，最初連接東京、京都及大阪等地。前島密推動的改變，不只是把送信的人跑快一點。想像寄件者把信交到櫃位：他未必認識沿途接力的人，卻需要知道怎樣付費、在哪裏投寄，以及收件者如何收到。郵票把已付郵資變成可辨認的標記；一封信於是可以交給制度接力，而不必每一程重新商議。

這種安排的力量，在於把許多地方的交接變成可以重複的程序。日本郵政的制度史將低廉而統一的收費、全國收投網絡、預付郵資及平等使用列為近代郵政的重要特徵。它們並非創辦當日便全部完成，而是逐步建立。郵票本身不能送信；它必須配合路線、據點、收費及人員。小小的紙片之所以有用，是因為背後不同的人願意按同一套規則辦事。

但新制度不會自動令所有人得益。原有飛腳業者反對官營郵政，前島密其後說服部分業者轉向運輸業務；一八七二年成立的陸運元會社便是這段轉變的一環。這提醒我們，改革既是技術安排，也是利益重新分配。官方創辦史可核對制度沿革，卻不能單憑它判定每個地方都立即享有同等服務。理解一項公共制度，要同時看它如何連接人，以及誰承擔轉型成本。

郵票的力量不在紙片，而在陌生人可以共同依循的規則。
為何郵票必須配合網絡和程序，才能成為可信的寄信方式？
揭示答案
郵票只標示已付郵資；若沒有相互銜接的路線、據點、人員和收投規則，下一站仍不知道如何接手。可信的服務來自整套制度共同運作。

The Port That Made Arrivals Wait

Predict before you read

How could a trading port respond to infection without permanently shutting its gates?

The city’s governing council faced plague and the needs of a maritime trading economy. Its 1377 decision created a waiting rule for arrivals from affected places, centuries before modern explanations of infection.
The later waterfront isolation complex; these surviving buildings are not the facilities used in 1377. Image credit and licence

In 1377, Ragusa (拉古薩), today’s Dubrovnik (杜布羅夫尼克), required arrivals from plague-affected places to spend thirty days apart before entry. A port lived by movement: people, ships and goods brought both income and danger. The waiting period offered a conditional route in, between unrestricted entry and complete exclusion. This was before germ theory (病菌學說). Officials did not need to know the precise organism to suspect that allowing potentially infected arrivals straight into the town could be dangerous.

The arrangement made time and separation into tools of administration. The rules also restricted townspeople’s contact with the isolation area and controlled who could deliver food. That detail matters: drawing a boundary means little if ordinary errands constantly cross it. Quarantine (檢疫隔離) therefore depended on supplies, supervision and compliance as well as an instruction to wait. The familiar forty-day association came later; the 1377 measure specified thirty. The later waterfront buildings pictured here belong to this longer institutional history, not to the original decree’s construction programme.

It would be too neat to call this modern public health fully formed. An early rule does not tell us how consistently it was enforced, whom it burdened, or how much disease it actually prevented. Historical accounts show a response to uncertainty, not a controlled experiment proving an ideal waiting period. The interesting institutional idea is narrower: access could be delayed and conditional rather than simply allowed or forbidden. Assessing such a policy still requires asking who supplies people while they wait and whether its costs and protections are distributed fairly.

A boundary works through the arrangements around it, not just the line itself.
Why were food deliveries part of the isolation rules rather than a minor logistical detail?
Check your answer
People needed supplies, but uncontrolled visits could undermine separation. The policy therefore depended on organising essential contact, not merely declaring a waiting period.

東方哲學

先要求守德，還是先讓人活得下去？

閱讀前先想一想

一個人知道甚麼是對的，是否就一定有條件長期做到？

戰國時期的孟子與齊宣王討論王道。這段對話將仁政連到百姓的生計與教養，追問統治者是否也要為道德生活的條件負責。
後世所繪孟子像；這是思想家的傳世形象，並非生前寫生。 圖片來源及授權

孟子與齊宣王談仁政時，沒有只要求百姓修養品德。他在《梁惠王上》指出，少數士人或能在沒有穩定生計時仍守住志向，但不應以這種要求衡量所有人。當人連養活父母、妻兒也成問題，統治者卻先放任他們陷入困境，再等犯罪後施罰，孟子稱之為「罔民」。問題於是由百姓為何不夠好，轉到掌權者有沒有提供讓人可以好好生活的條件。

「恆產」在這段論述中，指可持續依靠的生計；「恆心」則關乎相對穩定的道德志向。兩者的關係不是有錢便自然有德，而是匱乏與恐懼會妨礙一般人實踐已有的道德能力。因此，仁政不能只在犯錯之後懲罰，也要在之前安排民生與教養。這是一個關於政治責任的論證：若制度使基本生活難以維持，就不能把所有失敗都歸咎於個人的意志。

不過，這是先秦思想家的規範主張，不是證明貧困必然導致犯罪的現代統計定律。孟子仍重視教育與個人努力，也承認有人能在逆境中守德。今天借用這個角度，較穩妥的做法是多問一層：我們要求的行為，需要哪些物質和社會條件？這個問題可揭示責任分配上的盲點，卻不能代替對具體政策成效的研究，更不應成為貶低貧困者品格的標籤。

評價選擇之前，也要看人有沒有實踐好選擇的條件。
「恆產」與「恆心」的關係，為何不是「有錢就有德」？
揭示答案
穩定生計是一般人實踐道德的重要條件，而非充分保證；孟子仍要求教養和努力，也承認少數人能在匱乏中守德。論點主要指向統治者的責任。
資料：《孟子・梁惠王上》第七章原文 · 斯坦福哲學百科：孟子的民生、教養與努力
Philosophy — Western

What Would Make You Change Your Mind?

Predict before you read

Is admitting that you could be wrong the same as having no reason to believe anything?

In nineteenth-century philosophy, Charles Sanders Peirce (查爾斯・桑德斯・皮爾士) developed a scientific account of inquiry. His concern was how beliefs become answerable to experience and other investigators, without demanding an impossible starting point of total certainty.
Portrait of Peirce, whose account joins inquiry with the possibility of correction. Image credit and licence
Extends the earlier discussion of pragmatism (2026-06-27): this time, genuine doubt and revisable belief.

Imagine a scale that gives a different reading whenever you step on it twice. You now have a concrete reason to investigate. Merely announcing that every scale might be unreliable would not identify the same problem. Charles Sanders Peirce (查爾斯・桑德斯・皮爾士) distinguished genuine doubt from a philosopher’s declaration of universal doubt. Inquiry begins with beliefs we actually have and disturbances we actually encounter. We cannot simply erase all our commitments by writing a dramatic opening sentence, then reconstruct knowledge from nowhere.

Fallibilism (可錯論) holds that our beliefs remain open to correction. It does not require pretending that all explanations are equally supported. Peirce’s scientific method directs inquiry towards consequences other investigators can examine: if this account is right, what should happen, and what observation would trouble it? In the scale example, checking it with known weights and comparing repeated readings makes disagreement investigable. This is my everyday illustration of the argument, not a historical experiment he reported. The important move is from private confidence towards checks that can resist our wishes.

There is a demanding limit here. A community can inherit the same blind spots, and imagining an eventual convergence of inquiry does not guarantee that today’s consensus is final. Peirce’s account of truth has itself attracted objections about whether inquiry must converge. Still, provisional belief need not mean paralysis. A useful distinction is between acting on the best available reasons and declaring those reasons immune to revision. Before defending a view, try identifying one possible observation that would count against it—and why that observation would matter.

Confidence can guide action while leaving a door open to correction.
What distinguishes genuine doubt from merely saying that everything could be wrong?
Check your answer
Genuine doubt arises from a specific difficulty that unsettles an actual belief and gives inquiry something to resolve. A blanket declaration supplies neither a particular problem nor a test.
Sources: Peirce: The Fixation of Belief (1877) · Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: pragmatism, inquiry and truth

Does the Way You Pay Change What You Buy?

Predict before you read

If the price is identical, would paying by card necessarily leave your spending unchanged?

The contactless payment (非接觸式支付) symbol illustrates a payment method, not the size of its behavioural effect. Image credit and licence
Extends mental accounting (2026-09-01): this time, how payment methods enter the decision.

Picture buying lunch and adding something small at the counter. The price is the same whether you hand over cash or tap a card, but the experience of paying is not. Behavioural economics (行為經濟學) asks whether this difference changes decisions. A 2024 meta-analysis (統合分析) combined results from 71 papers and found a small average cashless effect (無現金支付效應): spending was generally higher with cashless payment. Small and average are essential words. The finding is not a rule that every person overspends whenever they tap.

One proposed mechanism is the pain of paying (付款痛感): spending feels different when the loss of money is more tangible or more closely connected to the purchase. Cashless methods can change that experience, but the pooled spending result does not establish one exclusive psychological cause. Payment convenience, context and the type of purchase can also matter. Think of two questions that need different evidence: whether a payment method changes expenditure, and which mental process produces the change. A reliable answer to the first does not automatically settle the second.

Here is a practical experiment you could design, rather than a treatment the research guarantees: choose a comparable spending category, write a limit before shopping, and compare totals over several occasions. Keep track of unusual purchases so that a holiday meal does not masquerade as a payment effect. Such a personal comparison remains imperfect, but it moves attention from the feeling of control to what was actually spent. There is no need to conclude that cash is universally better; the more useful question is which arrangement makes your own trade-offs visible.

The price tag and the experience of paying can influence different parts of a decision.
Why does a pooled cashless spending effect not prove that reduced payment discomfort is its only cause?
Check your answer
The spending outcome can arise through several pathways. Establishing a particular psychological mechanism requires evidence that distinguishes it from convenience, purchase context and other explanations.
Sources: Journal of Retailing: Less cash, more splash? (2024)

What Does the Other Arm Learn?

Predict before you read

If only one arm trains, what might change when researchers test the other arm?

Motor cortex (運動皮質) diagram: part of the system controlling movement, not a map of a single proven transfer pathway. Image credit and licence
Extends unilateral training (2026-07-09): this time, what transferred strength does—and does not—measure.

A person practises a strength task with one arm, while the other does not perform that training. Later, researchers test both sides. The untrained side can improve too: cross-education (交叉教育效應), documented across studies of unilateral resistance training (單側阻力訓練). This is a useful puzzle because we often imagine strength as a property located entirely inside the muscle that worked. A strength test instead measures the output of a coordinated system. Its result can change even when the tested side did not complete the training programme.

Neural adaptation (神經適應) is central to explanations of this transfer. Training one side engages a nervous system with connections and representations relevant to both sides; it does not simply build a second muscle at a distance. Researchers have examined changes in muscle electrical activity and responses to stimulation of the brain. A 2018 systematic review (系統綜述) supported a central neural contribution, but the measured changes did not neatly explain the size of the transferred strength gains. Evidence for a broad mechanism is stronger than evidence for one complete pathway.

That distinction matters when interpreting progress. A better result on a specific force task is not identical to hypertrophy (肌肉肥大), and it does not establish equal improvement in every movement. Study protocols, tasks and participants differ, so an average transfer cannot predict one person’s response. Nor does it mean an injured limb should be trained without clinical guidance, or that training one side replaces a balanced programme. The broader lesson is about measurement: what looks like a change in a body part may partly reflect changes in how the whole system uses it.

A stronger test result can reflect changed control, not only changed muscle size.
Why should improved force on the untrained side not be described as proof of equal muscle growth?
Check your answer
The outcome is performance on a strength task. Neural control can improve that performance without establishing comparable local muscle growth; those outcomes require different measurements.
Sources: Cross-education: strength meta-analysis (2017) · Neural adaptations: systematic review (2018)

When a Positive Test Is Mostly a Question

Predict before you read

Can a reasonably accurate screening test (篩查測試) produce more false alarms than true positives?

A miner awaiting a lung screening examination. The numerical example in this card is invented for explanation and does not describe this programme. Image credit and licence

Consider a deliberately hypothetical screening test (篩查測試), not the performance of any named medical test. Among 1,000 people, suppose 10 have the condition. The test detects 9 of those 10, but also flags 50 of the 990 people without it. There are now 59 positive results, only 9 of which identify the condition. Positive predictive value (陽性預測值) is therefore about 15%. The surprise comes from counting both routes to a positive result, rather than looking only at how well the test detects disease.

The underlying prevalence (患病率) matters because the two starting groups can be radically different sizes. A modest false-positive rate (假陽性率) applied to a very large unaffected group may produce many alarms. A positive screening result therefore usually starts further assessment rather than completing a diagnosis (診斷). False positives also differ from overdiagnosis (過度診斷): in the latter, a real abnormality is detected that would never have caused harm during the person’s lifetime. One concerns an incorrect signal; the other concerns finding something real that did not need to become a patient’s problem.

This helps explain why finding more cases is not enough to show that a screening programme helps. Lead-time bias (提前診斷偏差) can lengthen measured survival after diagnosis simply by moving the diagnosis earlier, without delaying death. The National Cancer Institute (美國國家癌症研究所) therefore discusses mortality outcomes (死亡率結果) alongside harms such as unnecessary follow-up. Actual decisions depend on the particular condition, age, risk and recommended follow-up. The useful question after a positive result is what it means for this test and person, and what confirmatory step is appropriate—not whether the hypothetical percentages above apply to you.

Ask how many positive results are true, not only how many cases a test can detect.
In the example, why is the answer 9 out of 59 rather than 9 out of 10?
Check your answer
Nine out of ten describes detection among people who have the condition. A person with a positive result belongs to the group of all positive results: 9 true positives plus 50 false positives, making 59.
Sources: NCI: benefits, false positives and overdiagnosis · NCI: what screening statistics tell us
Sports Science

What Are You Buying With Another Minute?

Predict before you read

If longer rests improve a workout, must each extra minute keep adding benefit?

A group strength session illustrates the setting; the photograph is not from either cited study. Image credit and licence
Extends rest intervals between sets (2026-08-10): this time, the uncertainty and cost of adding more rest.

The clock between sets is doing two jobs: allowing recovery and consuming the time available for the session. A 2016 trial assigned 21 trained men to one-minute or three-minute rests for eight weeks. The longer-rest group improved more in the tested maximum lifts and front-thigh muscle thickness. That is evidence against treating short rests as automatically superior for muscle growth. It is not evidence that every muscle measured grew significantly more, or that three minutes is an individually optimal prescription for every exercise.

One explanation concerns volume load (訓練總負荷), the combined weight and repetitions performed. If fatigue sharply reduces later-set performance, shorter rests can reduce the work completed under otherwise similar instructions. A 2024 Bayesian meta-analysis (貝葉斯統合分析) found a small average hypertrophy (肌肉肥大) advantage for rests longer than sixty seconds, with substantial uncertainty and differences between studies. It did not detect appreciable additional differences above ninety seconds. That absence of a clear difference is not proof that every longer interval is equivalent in every programme.

The decision therefore depends on what you are trying to preserve. During a demanding exercise, extra recovery may help maintain repetitions and load; in a tightly limited session, waiting longer also leaves time for fewer sets or exercises. Those are different comparisons, and a study holding the prescribed programme constant does not automatically answer a fixed-time scheduling question. A useful observation is whether the next set repeatedly collapses in performance despite unchanged effort. Read that alongside technique, total session time and the broader programme, rather than treating either breathlessness or the stopwatch as a direct measure of growth.

Rest has value through the work it enables; its opportunity cost is time.
Why does a fixed-programme comparison not settle the best choice for a fixed-time session?
Check your answer
Longer rests may preserve performance when the same sets are prescribed, but a fixed session length may require fewer sets or exercises. The total work and time constraints have changed.
Sources: The 2016 one-minute versus three-minute trial · Give it a rest: 2024 systematic review
Investing & Economic Thinking

A Safe Repayment Can Still Have a Moving Price

Predict before you read

Could a bond (債券) lose market value even if nobody becomes more worried about repayment?

A historical bond certificate. The calculations describe hypothetical payments, not the terms or value of this document. Image credit and licence

Imagine two otherwise identical promises, each paying 100 units in one year and nothing before then. If a newly available promise becomes cheaper, the older one cannot stay equally attractive at its old price. This is the intuition behind interest-rate risk (利率風險) in a fixed-rate bond (定息債券). Its promised payments do not rise just because the market now offers better returns. To offer a competitive yield (收益率), its price can fall. No new fear of default (違約) is needed for that repricing.

For a numerical illustration, a zero-coupon bond (零息債券) paying 100 in one year is worth about 96.15 at a 4% annual yield: 100 divided by 1.04. At 5%, the same payment is worth about 95.24. For payment ten years away, the corresponding values are about 67.56 and 61.39. These simplified calculations isolate the timing of one certain payment; they are not quoted market prices. Duration (存續期) formalises sensitivity to changes in yields, taking account of the timing of all a bond’s payments rather than just its final date.

Holding a bond until maturity (到期日) can make an interim sale unnecessary, provided the issuer meets its obligations and the terms are as expected. It does not erase opportunity cost (機會成本), inflation (通脹), or the possibility that money is needed sooner. A bond fund also has a changing collection of holdings rather than necessarily one repayment date matching your need. Before calling an investment safe, specify the thing to be protected: a future nominal payment, purchasing power, or a sale value next month. Those are different promises to ask of the same instrument.

Safety needs a date and a definition: repayment certainty is not price stability.
Why does the same rise in yield have a larger price effect on the ten-year example?
Check your answer
The payment is discounted for more years, so a change in the annual discount rate compounds across more periods. The promised amount is unchanged; its present value changes more.
Sources: SEC: interest-rate risk and bond maturity · Investor.gov: bonds and their risks
Political Economics

When Cheaper Rice Means Buying Less

Predict before you read

Could a household buy less of a staple when its price falls, without making an irrational choice?

Rice plants illustrate the staple crop; this photograph is not evidence from the household experiment. Image credit and licence
EVENT
In a subsidised-price experiment among very poor households in China (中國), Robert Jensen (羅伯特・詹森) and Nolan Miller (諾蘭・米勒) found strong evidence of Giffen behaviour (吉芬行為) for rice in Hunan (湖南), with weaker evidence for wheat in Gansu (甘肅).
THEORY
A price fall creates a substitution effect (替代效應) towards the cheaper food, but also an income effect (收入效應): the household can afford a different diet. Under tight subsistence constraints (維生限制), these forces can oppose each other.
ANOMALY
Some households bought less rice when subsidised, using their greater purchasing power to improve the diet. This contradicts an unconditional “cheaper always means buying more” slogan, not a demand model that already allows the opposing income effect.
CHALLENGER
The refinement is conditional: staple dependence, limited alternatives and the severity of poverty matter. The results do not imply that every poor household or every staple behaves this way. The policy question becomes how a subsidy changes the whole diet, not just purchases of the subsidised item.
An apparent exception can reveal the assumptions a simple rule left unstated.
Sources: Jensen and Miller: Giffen Behavior and Subsistence Consumption · NBER working paper and abstract

Connection of the Day · What an observation can establish

Cards 7 and 8 both separate a measured result from the explanation we want to attach to it. More force from an untrained arm does not directly measure muscle growth; a positive screening result does not by itself establish a diagnosis. In each case, an additional inference requires evidence that distinguishes competing explanations.

The analogy has a limit: cross-education concerns adaptation after training, while screening classifies a condition that may already exist. Their probabilities and mechanisms are different. The useful shared question is: what did this measurement actually observe, and what extra step would justify my conclusion?
