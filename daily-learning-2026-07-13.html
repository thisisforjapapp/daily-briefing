The Star That Song Dynasty Astronomers Watched Explode in 1054 — What’s Left of It Today: The Crab Nebula (蟹狀星雲)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
In July 1054, Chinese court astronomers recorded a “guest star” bright enough to see in daylight for over three weeks, then it faded from view within two years. What do you think that star actually was, and what do you think remains of it today?
The Crab Nebula (蟹狀星雲), remnant of the supernova recorded in 1054. Credit: Wikimedia Commons / NASA, ESA

On 4 July 1054, imperial astronomers of the Song dynasty (宋朝) recorded a “guest star” (客星) suddenly blazing near the constellation Taurus (金牛座), bright enough to be visible in daylight for over three weeks and remaining visible at night for nearly two years, according to records preserved in the official History of Song (宋史). Similar sightings appear in contemporary Japanese court diaries. Unknown to these observers, they were watching a supernova (超新星) — the catastrophic death explosion of a massive star, its outer layers blasted outward at thousands of kilometres per second, briefly outshining every other star in the sky.

What remains today is the Crab Nebula (蟹狀星雲, also called M1), an expanding cloud of glowing gas and dust roughly 6,500 light-years away, still growing outward at about 1,500 kilometres per second nearly a thousand years later. At its centre sits the Crab Pulsar (蟹狀星雲脈衝星) — a neutron star (中子星) barely 20 kilometres wide but denser than the Sun, spinning roughly 30 times per second and sweeping a beam of radiation past Earth like a cosmic lighthouse, discovered by radio astronomers in 1968 shortly after pulsars themselves were first identified.

Modern telescopes reveal the nebula in ways Song astronomers never could: Hubble’s visible-light images show delicate orange filaments of expelled gas, Chandra’s X-ray imaging (錢德拉X射線天文台) reveals the pulsar’s energetic wind sculpting the nebula’s interior, and Spitzer’s infrared data traces cooler dust. By measuring the nebula’s current expansion rate and tracing it backward, astronomers calculated an explosion date matching the historical 1054 sighting almost exactly — a rare case where a written human record and a physical measurement independently confirm the same event across nearly a millennium.

「霸王別姬」與「約法三章」：項羽劉邦楚漢相爭如何決定漢朝四百年基業

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
假如你是鴻門宴上的項羽，明知劉邦是最大威脅，卻選擇放他一馬，你認為這是仁慈，還是致命的政治誤判？
背景速覽

核心人物：項羽（西楚霸王）、劉邦（漢高祖）、范增（項羽謀士）、張良／蕭何／韓信（劉邦「漢初三傑」）。

事件：西元前206年至前202年楚漢相爭，劉邦最終於垓下之戰擊敗項羽，建立漢朝。

時代背景：秦朝滅亡後，項羽自立西楚霸王，大封十八路諸侯，劉邦受封漢中卻暗中積蓄實力，雙方由滅秦盟友迅速演變為爭奪天下的死敵。

項羽畫像。來源：維基媒體共享資源

秦亡之後，項羽率諸侯聯軍入關，聽聞劉邦已先入咸陽並派兵把守函谷關，勃然大怒，於鴻門設宴，意欲藉機除去劉邦。宴上范增數次以眼色及玉玦暗示項羽下令，項羽猶豫不決；范增遂命項莊舞劍助興，實則伺機刺殺劉邦，項伯察覺後起身對舞掩護。劉邦藉如廁之機，在張良、樊噲協助下抄小路逃回軍營，僅留張良代為辭謝，項羽終究未能痛下殺手，錯失了此後楚漢相爭中唯一能輕易除去劉邦的機會。

項羽分封諸侯後自恃武力天下無敵，卻屢屢失於政治謀略；劉邦入關時已與關中父老「約法三章」——殺人者死，傷人及盜抵罪，餘悉除去秦朝苛法——迅速贏得民心。楚漢相爭期間，劉邦任用韓信為大將，韓信明修棧道、暗度陳倉奪取關中，其後在井陘背水一戰，以寡擊眾大破趙軍，展現驚人軍事才能；項羽雖屢戰屢勝，卻疲於在各戰線奔波應付，糧道亦屢遭截斷，雙方一度議和，以鴻溝為界，中分天下。

劉邦背約追擊，聯合韓信、彭越大軍將項羽合圍於垓下，韓信布下十面埋伏之陣。夜裡漢軍四面唱起楚地歌謠，楚軍士卒聞歌思鄉，鬥志盡失，紛紛逃散，史稱「四面楚歌」。項羽自知大勢已去，與愛姬虞姬訣別，慷慨悲歌「力拔山兮氣蓋世」，是為「霸王別姬」。項羽率殘部突圍至烏江畔，自覺無顏再見江東父老，拔劍自刎，年僅三十一歲。劉邦統一天下，建立漢朝，開啟其後四百餘年的基業。

When Athens’s Own Democracy Voted for Disaster: The Peloponnesian War (伯羅奔尼撒戰爭) and the Sicilian Expedition

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Athens (雅典), the wealthiest and most powerful city in Greece, votes to send its fleet on a risky invasion far from home — against the warnings of its most cautious general. What do you think convinced the assembly to go ahead anyway?
Context

Key figures: Pericles (伯里克利), Athenian statesman who shaped early war strategy; Alcibiades (亞西比德), a brilliant but reckless general who championed the invasion; Nicias (尼西阿斯), the cautious commander who opposed it yet was placed in charge; Thucydides (修昔底德), Athenian general turned historian and eyewitness.

Event at stake: The Peloponnesian War (伯羅奔尼撒戰爭, 431–404 BCE) between Athens (雅典) and Sparta (斯巴達), particularly the disastrous Sicilian Expedition (西西里遠征, 415–413 BCE).

Big picture: Athens’s naval empire had made it rich and dominant after defeating Persia decades earlier, while Sparta’s land-based alliance grew increasingly alarmed by Athenian expansion.

The opposing alliances of the Peloponnesian War (伯羅奔尼撒戰爭). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Thucydides (修昔底德) famously wrote that “what made war inevitable was the growth of Athenian power and the fear this caused in Sparta (斯巴達).” Pericles (伯里克利) initially urged Athens (雅典) to avoid pitched land battles against Sparta’s superior army, retreating the population behind the city’s Long Walls (長牆) and relying on naval superiority instead — a strategy upended when a devastating plague swept through the overcrowded city in 430 BCE, killing perhaps a quarter to a third of the population, including Pericles himself the following year.

In 415 BCE, the charismatic Alcibiades (亞西比德) persuaded the Athenian assembly to launch a massive invasion of Syracuse (敘拉古) in Sicily, promising glory and resources despite Nicias’s (尼西阿斯) explicit warnings that the expedition was reckless overreach; political maneuvering placed Nicias in joint command of a campaign he had argued against. Days after the fleet sailed, Alcibiades was recalled to face criminal charges and defected to Sparta instead, leaving command to the far more cautious Nicias, whose hesitation let Syracuse’s defenses strengthen for months.

The expedition dragged on for two disastrous years; Athens’s fleet was ultimately trapped and destroyed in Syracuse’s harbour, and the retreating land army was annihilated attempting to march overland, wiping out virtually the entire expeditionary force. Sparta, now funded by Persian gold, built a navy capable of challenging Athens directly and eventually blockaded and starved the city into surrender in 404 BCE. Thucydides’s unfinished History of the Peloponnesian War (伯羅奔尼撒戰爭史) became a founding text of political realism, documenting how a democracy’s own ambition and internal politics could prove more destructive than any external enemy.

「白馬非馬」：公孫龍與名家如何用邏輯詭辯挑戰常識，卻為中國邏輯學開先河

🤔 閱讀前先猜猜
如果有人堅稱「白馬不是馬」，並且言之成理地說服你無法反駁，你認為這只是強詞奪理，還是真正觸及了語言與邏輯的深層問題？
背景速覽

核心人物：公孫龍（約公元前320至前250年，戰國時期趙國人）；惠施（名家另一代表，以「歷物十事」聞名）；孔穿（孔子六世孫，曾與公孫龍當面辯論「白馬非馬」）。

核心思想：名家；白馬非馬論；堅白論（離堅白）。

時代背景：戰國時期百家爭鳴，各國君主延攬辯士游說天下，公孫龍曾為趙國平原君門客；名家以析辭辯理、探究「名」與「實」之間的關係著稱，卻因論證看似違反常識而屢遭儒家、墨家批評為「苛察繳繞」的詭辯之學。

先秦諸子百家出生地地圖，名家人物亦包含其中。來源：維基媒體共享資源

公孫龍在趙國平原君門下時，提出「白馬非馬」這一著名命題，震動當時的辯論場。他的論證是：「馬」這個名指涉的是形狀，「白」這個名指涉的是顏色，「白馬」則是形與色合一而成的複合概念，其內涵已與單純的「馬」不同——一個指涉形狀本身，一個指涉「形加色」的組合，兩者在「名」的層次上並不完全等同，因此嚴格而言「白馬」不能簡單等同於「馬」。這個論證聽起來違反常識，卻迫使聽者認真思考「名」與「實」究竟是否真的一一對應。

公孫龍另有「堅白論」，以一塊又白又堅硬的石頭為例：眼睛看見石頭的白色，卻感受不到它的堅硬；手觸摸感受到石頭的堅硬，卻看不見它的顏色。既然人的感官無法同時、同一地把握「堅」與「白」兩種屬性，公孫龍便主張「堅」與「白」在認識論上其實是彼此分離的，稱為「離堅白」。這一論證進一步延伸出「名」是否能完整對應「實」、感官經驗的局限如何影響我們對世界的認識等哲學問題，觸及了語言與知識論的核心。

公孫龍曾與孔子六世孫孔穿當面辯論「白馬非馬」，孔穿最終未能駁倒他的論證，卻仍被平原君嘲笑「以文亂法」。荀子在《正名篇》中批評名家「甚察而不惠，辯而無用，多事而寡功」，認為這類辯論徒逞口舌之能，無益於治國安邦。名家其後迅速衰落，未能形成如儒墨般的長期學派，但「白馬非馬」的命題並未因此被遺忘——近代學者馮友蘭、胡適等重新評價名家，肯定公孫龍在概念分析與邏輯思辨上的貢獻，視其為中國邏輯學與語言哲學的重要先驅。

Better to Be Feared Than Loved? Niccolò Machiavelli (馬基維利) and the Birth of Political Realism

🤔 Predict Before You Read
A political advisor writes a handbook openly explaining how rulers should lie, betray allies, and use calculated cruelty to hold onto power — and it becomes one of the most influential books in Western political thought. Do you think Machiavelli was endorsing evil, or simply describing how power actually works?
Context

Key figures: Niccolò Machiavelli (馬基維利, 1469–1527), Florentine (佛羅倫斯) diplomat and political theorist; Cesare Borgia (凱撒·波吉亞), the ruthless Renaissance prince Machiavelli held up as a model of effective rule; the Medici family (美第奇家族), who imprisoned and tortured Machiavelli before he wrote his most famous work seeking their favour.

Core ideas: virtù (德才, the skill and boldness to seize opportunity) versus fortuna (命運, fortune or chance); the argument that political success must be judged by outcomes, not conventional morality.

Big picture: Written in 1513 after Machiavelli lost his government post and was tortured on suspicion of conspiracy, at a time when Italy’s fractured city-states were repeatedly invaded by France and Spain.

Niccolò Machiavelli (馬基維利), portrait by Santi di Tito. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Machiavelli (馬基維利) served the Florentine Republic (佛羅倫斯共和國) as a diplomat for over a decade, during which he closely observed Cesare Borgia (凱撒·波吉亞) ruthlessly but effectively consolidate control over the Romagna region — an episode he later recounted almost admiringly in The Prince (君主論). After the Medici family (美第奇家族) returned to power in 1512 and the Republic fell, Machiavelli was accused of conspiracy, imprisoned, and tortured on the strappado before being exiled to the countryside; he wrote The Prince partly hoping to win back Medici favour, dedicating it to Lorenzo de’ Medici.

The Prince broke sharply from the earlier “mirrors for princes” tradition, which instructed rulers to govern according to Christian virtue. Machiavelli argued instead that a ruler who always keeps his word and acts justly will be destroyed by rivals who do not; his famous line holds it is “better to be feared than loved, if you cannot be both” — though he warns fear must never curdle into hatred, which breeds conspiracy. Virtù (德才) is the skill to master fortuna (命運), which he compares to a violent river: uncontrollable in flood, yet manageable if one builds dikes and channels before the water rises.

The term “Machiavellian” became shorthand for cunning, amoral manipulation, though scholars still debate whether he was cynically describing power as it actually operates, a proto-realist, or covertly satirising tyranny — his other major work, Discourses on Livy (論李維), praises republican government at length. Either way, The Prince founded political realism (政治現實主義) by treating politics as a domain with its own logic, separate from conventional morality, influencing thinkers from Thomas Hobbes (霍布斯) to modern international relations theory.

Why Repeating a Claim Makes It Feel True: The Illusory Truth Effect (錯覺真相效應)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
You hear the same claim three separate times over a month, from three different sources. Do you think simply hearing it repeated makes you more likely to believe it — even if you’re generally sharp and skeptical?
Repeated advertising claims exploit the same fluency shortcut behind the illusory truth effect (錯覺真相效應). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

In a 1977 study, psychologists Lynn Hasher, David Goldstein and Thomas Toppino (哈舍、戈爾茨坦與托皮諾) had participants rate the plausibility of trivia statements across three separate sessions spaced weeks apart. Statements repeated across sessions were consistently rated as more truthful than new statements — regardless of whether they were actually true. Participants weren’t tracking accuracy at all; mere repetition alone was enough to shift their judgment toward “true,” even for claims they had no independent way to verify.

The mechanism is processing fluency (加工流暢度): a statement heard before is easier and faster for the brain to process the second time, and the brain misreads that ease as a signal of accuracy — an evolutionarily efficient but easily fooled shortcut. The effect persists even for statements that are obviously false or absurd once they’ve been repeated enough times, and it operates largely below conscious awareness, meaning people rarely notice familiarity is doing the persuading rather than evidence.

A 2015 study by Lisa Fazio and colleagues in the Journal of Experimental Psychology: General found the effect persists even among people who already possess the correct, contradicting knowledge — prior expertise doesn’t fully protect against the fluency-driven illusion. This has direct implications for misinformation (虛假資訊) spreading across multiple sources: a false claim repeated by several separate outlets can feel independently verified purely because it feels familiar, even when no single source actually checked the facts.

The Two Hormones Arguing Inside Your Brain Every Time You Decide to Eat: Ghrelin (飢餓素) and Leptin (瘦素)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Your stomach growls right on schedule before lunch, even on a day you skipped breakfast entirely. What do you think is actually driving that timed hunger signal — is it really just an empty stomach?
Leptin (瘦素), the fat-derived hormone that signals satiety to the brain. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Ghrelin (飢餓素) is produced mainly by the lining of the stomach (胃) and rises sharply before habitual mealtimes — often anticipating your usual eating schedule rather than responding purely to actual energy need — then signals the arcuate nucleus (弓狀核) within the hypothalamus (下丘腦) to stimulate appetite and food-seeking behaviour. Discovered in 1999 by Kojima and colleagues, ghrelin is often called the body’s “hunger hormone,” and its levels typically fall again once a meal begins, well before digestion has meaningfully progressed.

Leptin (瘦素) works in the opposite direction, produced by fat cells (脂肪細胞) in rough proportion to total body fat stores, signalling the same hypothalamic circuits to suppress appetite and increase long-term energy expenditure. It was discovered through the 1994 study of genetically obese “ob/ob” mice by Jeffrey Friedman’s laboratory, whose classic parabiosis experiments — surgically joining the blood circulation of two mice — showed a missing satiety signal, not just missing willpower, was driving their compulsive overeating.

In obesity, leptin levels are actually high, proportional to expanded fat stores, but the brain becomes progressively less responsive to the signal — a phenomenon called leptin resistance (瘦素抵抗), analogous to insulin resistance (胰島素抵抗). This helps explain why sustained weight loss through willpower alone is so difficult: the brain’s hunger-suppressing feedback loop is effectively broken despite abundant fuel reserves. Sleep deprivation compounds the problem, measurably lowering leptin and raising ghrelin within as little as two nights of poor sleep.

💊 Health

Why Your Doctor Won’t Give You Antibiotics for a Cold — And Why That’s Actually Good For You

🤔 Predict Before You Read
You’ve got a bad cold with a sore throat and thick green mucus. You ask your doctor for antibiotics “just in case.” Do you think antibiotics will actually speed up your recovery?
A bacterial culture testing antibiotic sensitivity and resistance (抗藥性). Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Antibiotics (抗生素) work by disrupting mechanisms specific to bacterial (細菌) cells — cell wall synthesis, bacterial protein synthesis, or bacterial DNA replication — but the common cold, most cases of flu, and the large majority of sore throats and bronchitis are caused by viruses (病毒), which lack cell walls and independent metabolic machinery for antibiotics to target. Taking antibiotics for a viral infection provides no clinical benefit whatsoever for how quickly or completely you recover from the illness itself.

Despite this, unnecessary prescribing remains common — the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC, 美國疾病控制與預防中心) estimates roughly 30% of outpatient antibiotic prescriptions are inappropriate, often driven by patient expectation and diagnostic time pressure rather than genuine bacterial infection. Every unnecessary course of antibiotics directly contributes to antibiotic resistance (抗藥性): surviving bacteria exposed to the drug pass resistance genes to their offspring, gradually reducing how well antibiotics work for everyone — a problem the World Health Organization (世界衛生組織) lists among the top global public health threats.

A persistent myth holds that green or yellow mucus proves a bacterial infection — it doesn’t. The colour comes from enzymes released by immune cells (neutrophils, 嗜中性白血球) fighting the infection, present regardless of whether the underlying cause is viral or bacterial. Most sore throats are viral; only an estimated 5–15% of adult pharyngitis cases are bacterial strep throat, which requires a rapid strep test to confirm, not visual inspection. For genuinely viral illness, rest, fluids, and symptom relief remain the actual evidence-based treatment.

💡 Colored mucus and antibiotics both feel like signs of progress, but neither one actually tells you whether an infection is bacterial.

Check your answer
The color comes from enzymes in immune cells (neutrophils) present in mucus regardless of whether the infection is viral or bacterial, so it doesn’t reliably distinguish between the two — unnecessary antibiotics for viral infections provide no benefit and contribute to antibiotic resistance.

🏋 Sports Science

Why Elite Runners Train Where the Air Is Thin: Altitude Training (高原訓練) and “Live High, Train Low”

🤔 Predict Before You Read
Athletes fly to mountain towns like Kenya’s Iten or Colorado’s Boulder to train where there’s less oxygen available per breath. Why would training somewhere your body performs worse actually help you perform better later, back at sea level?
A high-altitude training base (高原訓練), used to trigger red blood cell adaptations. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Reduced oxygen partial pressure (氧分壓) at altitude triggers the kidneys to release erythropoietin (紅血球生成素, EPO), a hormone that stimulates bone marrow to produce more red blood cells (紅血球) over roughly two to three weeks, increasing the blood’s oxygen-carrying capacity. This is the same natural hormone whose synthetic version fuelled major doping scandals in endurance sports like professional cycling (自行車運動), since artificially boosting EPO produces the same blood-oxygen advantage without the weeks of altitude exposure.

A landmark 1997 study by Benjamin Levine and James Stray-Gundersen found athletes who lived at moderate altitude (roughly 2,500 metres) but travelled down to train at lower altitude gained the red-blood-cell benefit without sacrificing training intensity — since thin air at altitude makes it harder to hit top speeds and power outputs during hard workouts. Pure “live high, train high” protocols showed smaller overall benefit, because impaired training quality partly cancelled out the blood adaptation gains.

The benefits fade within about two to three weeks of returning to sea level as red blood cell counts normalise, so competition timing matters considerably. Altitude tents and hypoxic chambers attempt to simulate the “live high” sleeping effect without relocating entire training camps, though individual response varies significantly — some athletes are measurable “non-responders.” Crucially, the body’s natural EPO response has a genetic ceiling, unlike synthetic doping, which is why altitude training remains legal while injected EPO does not.

💡 Altitude training doesn’t make you fitter directly — it tricks your body into building more oxygen-carrying blood cells that keep paying off once you’re back at sea level.
🧠 Answer this without looking back: Why did the “Live High, Train Low” strategy outperform simply living and training at high altitude the whole time?
Check your answer
Living at altitude still produced the EPO-driven red blood cell increase, but training at lower altitude let athletes maintain higher training intensity and speed, avoiding the performance-limiting effect that low oxygen has during actual hard workouts — combining both benefits at once.
📖 Levine, B.D. & Stray-Gundersen, J. (1997). Journal of Applied Physiology, 83(1). Wilber, R.L. (2007). Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 39(9).
📈 Investing & Economic Thinking

The Doctor Who Bet Against the Housing Market — and Nearly Lost His Fund Before Being Proven Right: Michael Burry (邁克爾·貝瑞)

🤔 Predict Before You Read
An investor spends years correctly predicting the 2008 housing crash and buys contracts that will pay off massively once it happens — yet his own investors nearly force him to sell before the crash even arrives. What do you think almost broke his fund before he was proven right?
The 2008 financial crisis (金融海嘯) that Michael Burry’s trade predicted years in advance. Credit: Wikimedia Commons

Michael Burry (邁克爾·貝瑞), a former neurologist turned value investor, founded Scion Capital (西恩資本) largely from his own garage. Around 2005, he personally read through thousands of pages of subprime mortgage bond (次級房屋抵押貸款債券) prospectuses that few other investors bothered examining in such depth, discovering that the underlying loan quality was deteriorating badly — adjustable-rate “teaser” loans were approaching resets that borrowers plainly couldn’t afford — despite the bonds themselves still carrying high credit ratings. He concluded a housing market (房地產市場) crash was coming.

Since no product existed to bet directly against individual mortgage bonds, Burry persuaded Goldman Sachs (高盛) and other banks to create custom credit default swaps (信用違約掉期, CDS) — essentially insurance contracts that would pay out only if the underlying bonds defaulted. He had to pay ongoing premiums for over a year while housing prices kept rising and the bonds kept performing normally, causing Scion’s reported paper value to fall even though Burry’s underlying thesis about eventual defaults remained unchanged.

Scion’s investors, unable to independently verify Burry’s illiquid, hard-to-price positions, grew alarmed and demanded redemptions; Burry controversially suspended withdrawals to prevent a forced unwind before his bet could pay off, straining relationships badly. When the subprime mortgage market (次級房貸市場) collapsed in 2007, Scion’s credit default swap positions ultimately returned roughly 489% net to investors — yet Burry closed the fund shortly afterward, worn down by years of hostile investor pressure endured while waiting to be proven right.

⚖️ Political Economics — Theory in the Wild

The Dollar’s Impossible Job: Robert Triffin (特里芬) and the Paradox at the Heart of the Global Reserve Currency

The US dollar (美元), the world’s dominant reserve currency (儲備貨幣). Credit: Wikimedia Commons
🌍 Event
Decades of persistent U.S. current account deficits (經常帳赤字) coexist with the U.S. dollar (美元) remaining the world’s dominant reserve currency (儲備貨幣) — the opposite of what earlier economic theory predicted would happen to a currency running such sustained deficits.
📖 Theory
Economist Robert Triffin (羅伯特·特里芬), testifying to the U.S. Congress in 1960, argued the country issuing the world’s reserve currency faces a built-in conflict: it must run deficits to supply the world with enough dollars for trade and reserves, yet doing so indefinitely erodes confidence in that currency’s value.
⚠️ Anomaly
Despite the U.S. running current account deficits for over 40 consecutive years, the dollar has not lost reserve status — it still comprises roughly 58% of global reserves per IMF data, far ahead of any rival, defying the confidence collapse Triffin’s logic implies.
🧪 Challenger
Economist Barry Eichengreen’s (巴里·艾肯格林) “exorbitant privilege” (過度特權) framework argues deep U.S. capital markets, network effects, and the lack of a credible alternative (a fragmented euro, a not-fully-convertible yuan) sustain dollar dominance regardless of Triffin’s logic; whether this is a stable equilibrium or merely a delayed reckoning remains an open question.
💡 The dollar’s continued dominance may not prove Triffin was wrong — it may just mean the reckoning he predicted hasn’t arrived yet.
📖 Triffin, R. (1960). Gold and the Dollar Crisis. Yale University Press. Eichengreen, B. (2011). Exorbitant Privilege. Oxford University Press. IMF COFER database (2024).

🔗 Connection of the Day

Cards 6 & 8 — When a Convenient Signal Replaces Real Verification: Psychology’s illusory truth effect and Health’s antibiotic myth are the same shortcut wearing different clothes: a claim that “just sounds familiar” and mucus that “just looks infected” both give the brain an easy, fluent signal that feels like evidence but isn’t. Repetition never checks a fact against reality, and colour never checks a pathogen against a lab test — both let processing ease substitute for genuine verification.

So what: Before trusting a claim or a symptom because it “feels obviously true,” ask what would actually need to be checked to know for sure — a second, independent source for a fact; a rapid strep test for a sore throat — rather than accepting the fluent-feeling shortcut.
